### PEG material parameters

- **`a` is three different quantities in the brush literature and they are not interchangeable.** The Alexander-de Gennes *effective monomer length* (0.35 nm, a **contour** length), the *volumetric* monomer size `v₀^(1/3)` (0.392 nm), and the *Kuhn length* `b` (1.1 nm). Their cubes differ by a factor of 39. Use `PegWater`'s three named properties; never write `a` for a volume.
- **Excluded volume is a PAIR quantity and does not coarse-grain linearly**: `v_K = n_K² v_m`, not `n_K v_m`, because the interaction is `(v/2)∫c²` and `c_K = c_m/n_K`. For PEG that is 118.4 Å³ per Kuhn pair against 12.25 Å³ per monomer pair, and getting it wrong moves a thermal-blob count by `n_K² = 9.67`. The check that catches it is that the Ginzburg parameter must be invariant under the choice of segment.
- **A thermal-blob count is a convention, and its prefactor is a published ~30× bracket.** The scaling `g_T = (b³/v)²` and Yamakawa's exact `z(g_T) = 1` differ by `1/0.32992² = 9.19` — which for PEG almost exactly cancels an `n_K²` coarse-graining error, 1222 against 1160. **Quote the Fixman `z` and the swelling `α = √(1+4z/3)`, which carry no convention; the Gen-1 chains are 6–20 % swollen, which is not what "0.06 of a blob" sounds like.**
- A **volume fraction** in this project always means the physical one, `N σ v₀ / h`. Sources that write `φ = n a³` are quoting a *reduced density*, 1.408× smaller for PEG — and a fitted prefactor always travels with the `φ` convention it was fitted under.
- **In the *bulk solution*, the binding crossover is the dilute→semidilute one, approached from below**, at `φ# = (αN)^(−4/5) ≈ 0.026` — not the semidilute→concentrated one at `φ ≈ 0.2–0.3`. Checking distance from the upper boundary says nothing about the lower one; `C-0001` made exactly that mistake. **But this is a property of the bulk solution and the grafted layer does not inherit it** — see the translational-entropy bullet below, and `CH-0002`.
- **The upper crossover is a ONE-PARAMETER FAMILY, `φ_c(n) = (v_K/b³)·n^(−1/2)`, and `n` — the Kuhn segments the correlation blob must keep — IS the convention.** `n = g_T` ends the des Cloizeaux exponent (0.0041–0.0319 for PEG/water); `n = 1` ends the blob picture (0.141). **Nothing in the family reaches the `0.2–0.3` in circulation**, and the two constructions that do — `v_m/v₀ = 0.203` and `1 − 2χ = 0.257` — are the same textbook expression read on a **monomer** instead of a Kuhn segment, i.e. `C-0002`'s `a`-trap again. The textbook `φ** ≈ v/b³` is in the **Kuhn-reduced** convention, `7.09×` larger than this project's physical one.
- **The des Cloizeaux window is non-empty if and only if the chain exceeds a thermal blob: `φ**/φ* = √(N_K/g_T)`, exactly.** Both edges are `φ_c(n)` at a different `n`, so the material prefactor cancels. For every Gen-1 chain `N_K = 22–120` against `g_T = 126–1160`: **the window is EMPTY and the `9/4` exponent never starts.** The measured `α φ^(9/4)` limb survives as a **fit** — a fit needs data, not a blob — and its validity ceiling is therefore the range it was fitted over (`φ ≤ 0.49–0.63`), never a scaling crossover.
- **A regime crossover cannot be used as an operating ceiling when the device is already past it.** Read as one, the derived `φ**` is violated at the *resting height* at 162 of 162 `C-0018` states — no bias at all would be usable. The number a bias ceiling needs is where the constitutive law stops being **supported**, and that ceiling **saturates**: above `φ ≈ 0.4` the crossover gap has fallen below `C-0005`'s 1.46 nm everywhere, so resolving it further buys nothing.
- **This project has TWO routes to the excluded volume differing by 2.5× (`A₂` osmometry, `χ` Flory-Huggins) and two thermal-blob normalisations differing by 9.19** — a `2 × 2` that must be swept, not picked from. Three corners make the des Cloizeaux window empty; the fourth (`χ`, scaling `g_T`) gives `g_T = 19.5` and a window 2.5× wide, defeated only by the separate fact that the layer sits above its upper edge for the whole stroke.
- **Coil overlap (`Σ ≥ 5`, or `φ > φ* = N^(−4/5)`) is not a sufficient criterion for semidilute behaviour**, and this is documented in print for PEG specifically. For PEG in water `Σ = 5` is exactly equivalent to `φ = 1.085 φ#`, independent of layer height. `Σ ≥ 5` is **dropped** as this project's brush criterion (`P-5`); the adopted one is `L₀/R₀ ≥ 1`.
- **A grafted layer has no chain translational entropy, and that term is the whole of the bulk crossover.** Integrating `Π(φ) = (k_BT/v₀)[φ/N + α φ^(9/4)]` gives `f = (k_BT/v₀)[φ lnφ/N + (4α/5) φ^(9/4)]`; the first term is the ideal-gas entropy of chain centres of mass, and it is the only thing bending the exponent below 9/4. **Never carry a *bulk* `m_eff` into a *brush* pressure law** — `CH-0001` did, and `CH-0002` had to undo it. The grafted layer's own exponent is 2.00–2.56.
- **PEG/water at 300 K is a *marginal* solvent, not a good one.** The measured `A₂ ≈ 1.9e-3 mol·cm³/g²` gives an excluded volume of 12.25 Å³ against a 60.4 Å³ monomer, so the thermal blob is 1222 Kuhn segments (167 kDa) and any chain below ~40 kDa is unswollen. Blob arguments — Alexander-de Gennes heights, `k_BT` per blob, `ξ = s` — **do not apply to Gen-1 chains at all**.
- **Osmotic virial coefficients carry a factor-of-two trap.** Use `Π/(RT) = c/M + A₂c² + A₃c³` with `c` in g/cm³ and no factor of two; the activity convention has `a₂₂ = 2 M A₂`. Into this project's dimensionless `B = v/v₀` the conversion is `B = 2 A₂ M₀/V̄`, and it is temperature-free.
- **`χ` lives on a lattice, and the lattice site is not always the monomer.** The measured PEG/water `χ` sits on a **water-molecule** site, so `v = v₀·(v₀/v_water)·(1 − 2χ)` — **2.010×** the familiar `v₀(1 − 2χ)`. Invisible by inspection; caught only by cross-check against `B₂`, where the right convention agrees to 16 % and the naive one misses by 42 %.
- **`χ ≈ 0.45` for PEG/water has no primary source** — the 0.44 in circulation is *polystyrene in toluene*, quoted for contrast in the very paper that measures PEG at **0.372** (300 K). Use `χ(T) = 1.156 − 235.3/T`, and read `dχ/dT` at the **cloud point**, not at 300 K: they differ by 51 %.
- **PEG in water is only *weakly* good, so order-of-magnitude reasoning about `χ` does not transfer to `v`.** `1 − 2χ = 0.257`, so `Δχ = 1e-3` — unmeasurable by any method — is already 0.74 % of the excluded volume, and one kelvin of cloud-point depression costs 1.35 %.
- **"The theta temperature of PEG in water" is a 16 K band, not 375 K** — 358.7 K (Flory-Huggins), 369 ± 3 K (cloud points), 373.2 K (virial). So `τ` at 300 K is 0.164–0.200, and `C-0002`'s 0.200 is the optimistic end.
- **A `χ` fitted inside a model is a parameter *of that model*, and its theta point is not automatically `½`.** The SCF that produced the circulating "brush `χ ≈ 0.60`" uses unequal segment volumes and puts its **own theta at 0.696**; its fitted 0.789/0.852 therefore say "13–22 % past theta", not "poor solvent by Flory-Huggins". The 0.60 was `1.2 × ½` — a ratio transferred onto the wrong axis. There are two defensible linear transfers (preserve the ratio to theta, or the distance past it) and here they differ by 0.089. **Never compare a fitted `χ` against `½` without first asking where that model puts theta.**
- **A des Cloizeaux or Alexander-de Gennes free energy cannot represent a poor solvent at all**, at any effective monomer length: its amplitude is a positive power of a positive excluded volume, so `χ ≥ ½` is not a large correction to it but outside the family. A grafted `χ` above theta is a claim that a *different* free energy is needed, not a rescaling of this one.
- **A bulk `χ` and a brush `χ` differ by at most 0.053 for this material** — measured by normal compression of grafted PEG *denser* than the Gen-1 window, giving `χ_eff = 0.346–0.424` against a bulk 0.372. Worth −11.4 % to +4.3 % of the layer stiffness. No compression measurement exists *inside* the Gen-1 window, so that bound comes from above and assumes monotonicity in grafting density.

### Brush mechanics

- The layer stiffness is **not a well-posed single number at the resting height**: the de Gennes scaling form has finite stiffness at first contact, the Milner-Witten-Cates SCF form has exactly zero (its pressure vanishes quadratically at `L0`, because the brush's outer edge is diffuse). Always quote a stiffness at a stated compression.
- `k/A = 3 k_BT sigma^(3/2) / L0` is **not** a 3/2 law in the grafting density — `L0` carries `sigma^(1/3)` too, so the equilibrium stiffness goes as `sigma^(7/6)`.
- Mapping the de Gennes two-brush pressure onto a brush against a rigid wall is `D -> 2h`, and the factor of two then **cancels out of both ratios**. Keeping it while reinterpreting `D` as the wall distance understates the pressure by `2^(9/4)` — this is the prefactor confusion the NDI problem definition warns about.
- **Ideal mobile salt exerts exactly no osmotic pressure on a grafted layer.** Ions excluded from the polymer contribute `f = k_BT n_s φ`, strictly linear in `φ`, and `Π = φf′ − f` annihilates a linear term; equivalently the layer's polymer volume per unit area is conserved under compression, so the energy is height-independent. At 10 mM MgCl₂ that cancels a term **3.5× the layer's own osmotic pressure** — not small, exactly zero. Everything a buffer does to a *neutral* layer's mechanics is therefore a `χ`, never an ion count.
- **A grafted layer has no resting height unless you define one.** Only trial-function profiles — the Alexander box, the truncated MWC parabola — terminate; a *solved* profile has a real decaying end distribution and reaches `P = 0` only asymptotically. Quote `L₀` with the load it was defined at, and quote the sensitivity: a hundred-fold change in that load moves `N(L₀)` by 2.5×.
- **And it has no THICKNESS unless you define one either, which is a second convention and worth 2.8× in the chain.** A force-onset `L₀` and a first-moment `2⟨z⟩` are different functionals of the same layer: at the Gen-1 design point they need `N = 62.1` and `N = 175.1` (2.74 against 7.71 kDa). **Read on ONE functional the box and the strong-stretching parabola agree to 0.76 % where their own conventions put them 28 % apart** — a box's `2⟨z⟩` is its own height identically and the parabola's is the Beta constant `1/[(p+1)B(p)]` = 0.7836 of it — so most of `C-0003`'s *"profile uncertainty"* was a convention difference between its own two models. **The gap decomposes as a PRODUCT with THREE factors** (the solved layer's convention, the trial function's own, and the physics), measured rather than preferred: across the grid the factor spreads 1.46× and the difference 3.74×. The physics left over is `1.64–1.95×` and it **falls with grafting density**, 1.92 at the window's dilute edge to 1.22 at its dense one.
- **The first moment is the better-posed thickness and the force onset is the one the DEVICE occupies, and the specification decides which to quote.** Over two decades of the defining load `N` moves 2.494× in the force-onset convention and 1.103× in the first-moment one — 9.3× in log sensitivity — so the moment is the quantity to *measure*. But a layer specified at `2⟨z⟩ = 10 nm` puts its tile at **13.2–18.0 nm**, outside §3's 5–10 nm band at **0 of 61** grid points: §3 specifies a distance between two bodies, so the window belongs in the force-onset convention and both numbers have to travel in a bench order. A bench cannot measure a force onset and cannot buy a thickness.
- **A first moment does not inherit the pressure's convergence order.** `2⟨z⟩` converges at **1.59** in the SCF node spacing and the chain length inverted on it at **1.11**, against 2.08–2.32 for the disjoining pressure on the same solves — because `M = round(h/Δz)` steps the node count with the solved wall height and the moment weights the outer nodes, where an absorbing-wall profile vanishes as `(h−z)²`, most heavily. The eighth instance of *"convergence is a property of the quantity"*.
- **Neither the Alexander box nor strong stretching contains the chain's entropic resistance to confinement.** The box has chain elasticity only as a *pull-back* that reduces the pressure; MWC has none at the wall, because free ends carry no tension. At Gen-1 densities that term is not a correction — against an absorbing wall `φ(h) = 0` and it is the **whole** disjoining pressure. The two models agree with each other to 1 % and are wrong together by 4.6×.
- **A brush criterion built on an *onset* height is not weak, it is vacuous.** `L₀/R₀ ≥ 1` admits **every one** of the 183 points of the Gen-1 sweep, including a layer at `Σ = 0.063` with 22 nm grafting spacing — it measures how far into the coil's tail the threshold sits and cannot fall below one. **Coil overlap `Σ = πR₀²σ ≥ 1` is the only criterion that bounds anything**, and it is also the validity condition of any 1-D mean field. **Four brush criteria have now failed here, each a convention asked to do a measurement's work.**
- **The Alexander-de Gennes unity prefactor is worth a factor of 6.6 in excluded volume.** `L₀ = N a^(5/3) σ^(1/3)` is reproduced exactly by a two-body Alexander box layer at `v = 81 Å³`, against the measured 12.25 Å³. Quote it as a scaling, never as a number.
- **`h ∝ σ^(1/3)` is a two-body result and `h ∝ σ^(5/13)` a des Cloizeaux one; the blob construction is neither** — it is des Cloizeaux minimised against *blob* elasticity rather than Gaussian. The three do not disagree about the interaction, they disagree about the **elasticity**, and which elasticity applies is decided by the thermal-blob count, which is measurable.
- **The layer response is only weakly sensitive to the interaction strength: `k ∝ K^(1/(m+1))` and `N ∝ K^(−1/(m+1))`, exactly**, at fixed height, grafting density and compression ratio — because the chain length a specified height demands moves against the interaction. For `m = 9/4` a 16× change in `K` is a 25 % change in stroke. Do not spend effort narrowing `Π_int` before checking this exponent.
- **The blob-stack height is `L₀/s = (Σ/π)^(5/6)`, identically** — 1.47 blobs at the conventional `Σ = 5` onset, for every polymer, chain length and thickness; a ten-blob stack needs `Σ ≈ 50`. So `Σ ≥ 5` no more delivers a stack of blobs than it delivers semidilute thermodynamics, and the two failures (`CH-0003`, `CH-0001`) are inverse powers of the same `Σ`.

### Load paths and joints

- **The equipartition force in a rigid internal constraint does not exist.** A penalty link's *static* force converges as the penalty stiffens while its *thermal* force grows as `√k_link` — exactly, `√10` per decade over four decades — because a spring in equilibrium stores `½k_BT`. A crossover's fluctuating force is `√(k_BT k_v)` set by its own vertical stiffness (2.78–115.8 pN here), it is present under a *uniform* load, it is identical for every attachment scheme, and it is **already inside any rupture force measured at 300 K**. Adding a broadband thermal RMS to a static share double-counts.
- **A concentration factor and a per-path share live on different cuts, and multiplying one by the other is a category error.** `C-0009`'s 2.3–7.6× multiplies the force *crossing an interface*, not the tension *entering at an attachment*; applied correctly to a 45-path coupling it takes 0.239 pN to 0.150 pN, not 2.22 pN to 5.1–16.9.
- **Which way a tolerance is correlated matters more than how big it is.** A ±10 % attachment-stiffness scatter alternating *across* the helices restores 0.088 pN of crossover force; the same amplitude alternating *along* them restores `3e−11 pN` — exactly zero at any amplitude, because it does not break the across-helix symmetry. **But that zero is a symmetry of EQUAL springs, and on a three-column non-uniform coupling the along-helix index IS the rim/interior index** — there the recommended direction is the one the *flatness* verdict tolerates least, 31.6 % against 69.8 %, so the two channels rank the same build rule oppositely (`CH-0073`). **A build rule derived on a design that fails a requirement cannot be tested against that requirement.**
- **A DNA rupture force is a function of the bonded LENGTH as well as the loading rate.** The 48 pN in circulation is Strunz's **30 bp** number; his own published constants give 18.8 pN at 8 bp, 34.8 at 16 and a 68.1 pN asymptote — so quoting "48 pN" for a staple domain of unstated length is optimistic by up to 2.6×.
- **"Saturating" is not "concave everywhere".** The barrier separation carries an `n`-independent 7 Å offset, so the shear allowable is convex at small `n`: splitting a bond across two duplexes wins above a **14.3 bp** total bonded length (at 100 pN/s) and loses below it. Splitting is not free.
- **Unzip is length-independent and shear is not**, so splitting a bond `m` ways multiplies capacity by exactly `m` in unzip and by `m·A(n/m)/A(n)` in shear. The topology and the geometry are not independent design choices.
- **An entry topology is bounded by cut equilibrium before any matrix runs**: on a `D`-duplex tile no attachment can put less than `1/D` into its worst duplex, and a bond spanning `m` duplexes enters at exactly `1/m` (no crossover sits on the rim). The lattice is only needed to say how close — 4.7 %, everywhere.
- **Spreading a bond along one duplex cannot relieve it** — the duplex still carries the whole tension inboard of the footprint. Relief a lattice shows is load shed past the **first crossover column**, and it vanishes at the phases that put that column beyond the footprint: worst-case, an 8 bp footprint is worth exactly nothing.
- **Bonding across two duplexes relieves the crossovers, it does not load them** — both bonded duplexes move together so the interface between them stops sliding, and the crossover path never becomes binding at any band width.
- **A minimum-length tether is a corner of the design space, not a design.** Its stroke-independent preload is 25–186× what the hold-down needs, and the length that delivers the *wanted* preload is longer than every `L_min`.
- **Evans-Ritchie has no equilibrium plateau**: the rupture force goes negative below ~4 bp at a slow rate. Quote it as a **ratio**, inside the measured loading-rate range, never extrapolated to `r → 0`.

### In-plane load paths

- **The in-plane and out-of-plane concentration factors are different quantities *and* different problems.** `C-0009`'s 2.3–7.6× is a peak over an *equal share* of a reaction the tile **collected** from its foundation over an `ℓ`-patch; a lateral tether collects nothing (the layer's lateral stiffness is exactly zero), so its own tension is the whole load. Applying one as the other is `CH-0021` — and it is ~20× out in the definition alone.
- **A moment applied to a duplex is reacted by the crossovers as an axial *couple***, because they act on the interface line and not on the duplex axis. So equilibrium bounds the **sum** of the member forces on a cut and never the per-member peak: an obliquely pulled tether puts **2.33×** its own tension into one duplex. A tension-chain intuition does not survive an off-axis load.
- **Frame indifference fixes the crossover connector arm at exactly `d/2`; it is not a fitted parameter.** Classical shear lag is the arm = 0 case, which drops `∂v/∂x` from the shear strain and therefore **charges energy to a rigid rotation of the whole sheet**. The dropped term is worth up to **4×** in how much load the attached duplex keeps.
- **A Gen-1 tile is too small to share an in-plane point load at all** — the across-strip sharing length is ≈ 65 nm against a 40 nm footprint. That is why the in-plane transfer ratio is 1 and not 1/15, and it is available for the price of a square root before any lattice is assembled.
- **Sampling a continuous angle on a discrete lattice is not a sweep.** A tether attaches to a duplex, so several nominal angles snap to the same physical design and the intermediate ones do not exist. Sweep the **endpoint pairs** — `C-0015`'s "shapes, not diagonals" in a new place; the complete sweep found placements 1.7× worse than the angle scan.
- **A minimum-length surface-parallel tether's *normal* preload is `n A √(2A/S)` — independent of the stroke.** At the minimum length the tension *is* the allowable, which fixes `δ/L`. 54.9 pN for four tethers in shear geometry, 55 % of the §3 target force, at every stroke; 5.4 pN in unzip geometry at a 2.2× longer tether.

- **The polymer Ginzburg parameter contains no `N`.** `Gi ~ 1/N` is the *blend critical-point* result, where `N` enters through `χ_c`; a solution's is `Gz = √(v/(c b⁶))`, purely local, so a grafted layer carries the **same** fluctuation correction as a bulk solution at the same local `φ`. The translational term grafting removes is separate and additive in the one-loop free energy.
- **The one-loop pressure ratio is `(2√3/π)Gz`, not `Gz`** — 1.1027× — and the free-energy and pressure corrections differ from each other by exactly 2, because `Δf ∝ c^(3/2)`. Three numbers, one physics; name which one you are quoting.
- **A broken expansion can still be bounded, if the term it corrects is not the term that carries the answer.** The polymer loop parameter is 1.30 at the Gen-1 layer, yet destroying the interaction *entirely* costs 9.4 % of `k_brush` — because at an absorbing wall the disjoining pressure is conformational. Ask what the correction multiplies before concluding it is unbounded.
- **`C-0003`'s `k ∝ K^(1/(m+1))` is a property of its two ansatz profiles, not of the layer** — the solved layer measures 0.0647 against 0.3077, 4.75× less sensitive. Same cause as `CH-0010`: the pressure is not the interaction.
- **There are two mean fields in this project and their loop parameters must never be compared** — the electrostatic one (`Ξ ∝ q³`, 123–214 %, corrects `k_es`) and the polymer one (`Gz`, 44–130 %, corrects `k_brush`). They act on the two terms of `k_eff` and neither bounds the other; that the numbers are similar is a coincidence of this material and this geometry.
- **A perturbation to a layer at specified `L₀` is absorbed by the chain length, not by the response** — three instances now (interaction strength, segment length, and `C-0003`'s own exponent). A swollen chain reaches the same height with 14 % fewer monomers, so coil overlap moves 0.9 % where the `α²` identity predicts 15 %.

### Fluctuation budgets

- **A spatially varying fluctuation has no single "at a point" value.** For a plate on a Winkler foundation the point variance is `σ₀²[1 + 3(2x/L)² + 3(2y/L)²]`, so a corner is exactly `√7` noisier than the centre — and the centre is the *quietest* place on the tile, being the fixed point of both tilts. Naming a point fluctuation without naming the point is the same class of error as quoting "the layer stiffness" without naming a compression.
- **The layer confines the tile in one direction only.** A laterally homogeneous grafted layer under a non-adsorbing tile has a translation-invariant free energy, so its lateral restoring stiffness is **exactly zero** — a symmetry statement, not a small number — and equipartition does not apply to that coordinate at all. It also exerts no *upward* force above `L₀`. Any "positional variance" here is a statement about the normal coordinate **under bias**, and must say so.
- **Quote a variance with its bandwidth.** For an overdamped mode the fraction below `f` is `(2/π)arctan(f/f_c)`. With `C-0004`'s drainage corner the Gen-1 tile puts only 0.55–3.1 % of its variance below 1 kHz — 13× in amplitude, more than the entire model bracket. A broadband `σ_RMS` is the `f → ∞` limit and is not the measured quantity.
- **The stroke and the noise use different stiffnesses** — the secant sets the stroke, the tangent at the working point sets the fluctuation, and for this layer they differ by ~3× in stiffness and 1.6× in amplitude.
- The **variance** of an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck coordinate relaxes at `2/τ`, not `1/τ`; dropping the factor gives `√(Dt)` instead of `√(2Dt)` at short times — a `√2` that no dimensional check catches.

### Electrostatics conventions

- **"The Debye length" is three different numbers in this project, and all three are correct in their own place** — 3.93 nm in the bulk buffer at 2 mM MgCl₂; 0.84–1.18 nm in the tile-electrode gap, which is counterion-dominated 3–33× so its ion content is set by the tile's charge and not by the buffer; and 4.5–5.5 nm inside the PEG layer, which excludes 23–48 % of the salt. Substituting one for another is `CH-0004`.
- **MgCl₂ is a 2:1 electrolyte: `I = ½ Σ c_i z_i² = 3c`, not `c`.** A monovalent intuition understates the screening threefold and the Debye length by `√3`.
- **The Manning parameter has two conventions in circulation** — Manning's own `ξ_M = l_B/b` is valency-free with condensation at `q ξ_M > 1`, while Naji et al. fold the valency in as `ξ = q l_B τ`. That is why the same DNA is quoted as both 4.1 and 8.2. State which you mean. The lateral counterion spacing has the same problem: `a_⊥ = sqrt(q/σ_s)` against the Wigner-Seitz `sqrt(q/(π σ_s))`, a factor of `√π` apart, and only the latter makes `Γ = sqrt(Ξ/2)` exact.
- **`Ξ ∝ q³` and `μ_GC ∝ 1/q`.** Divalent Mg²⁺ at a DNA surface is a *different problem* from monovalent Na⁺ at the same surface, not a rescaling of it — `Ξ` goes from 3.0 to 24.0, and the mean-field error at a 7 nm gap from 36 % to 163 %.
- **MgCl₂ is 2:1, and the two signs of surface charge do not screen equally.** The 2:1 first integral is `(y'/κ)² = (e^{−2y} + 2e^{y} − 3)/3` — not even in `y`, so there is no `sinh` form and no symmetric closed form applies. Its saturated far-field amplitude is `12 − 6√3` at a **negative** wall and exactly `6` at a **positive** one, a ratio of exactly `2 + √3`. Both are needed here: the tile is negative and the electrode positive.
- **A diffuse-layer potential is not an applied bias.** They differ by the compact-layer drop, and because the electrode charge is exponential in `ψ_d` while the compact term is linear in it, the compact layer takes 66 % of 0.1 V and 88 % of 2 V. Comparing a `ψ_d` threshold against a §3 bias without the Stern series is `CH-0007` — it made a 1.2× exceedance look like 10×.
- **A charge-saturated surface makes its own charge ambiguity irrelevant.** Three readings of the tile's gap-facing charge spanning a factor of three give `σ_eff` within 7 %. Check for saturation *before* spending an iteration resolving a charge model.
- **Finite ion size (Bikerman) *raises* the electrostatic force, by up to 56 % here** — counterions that cannot pack at the wall screen from further out. Point-ion PB is a **lower** bound on `|F_es|`, not an upper one.
- **Parametrise a biased gap by the diffuse-layer drop, not the applied bias.** One Poisson-Boltzmann solve gives the force *and* the bias that produced it; going the other way costs ~34 solves of Stern-series inversion. A factor of 35 wherever the bias is an output rather than an input.
- **A finite charged plate's edge *adds* force, it does not lose it.** The intuition that a rim "loses field lines" is right and the conclusion drawn from it is wrong: fringing raises a capacitor's capacitance, and at the Gen-1 gaps the 40 nm tile carries **+14.7 %** more total force than a 1-D pressure over its footprint — a sub-Debye **1.65 nm collar** on every side, scaling as `1/L` (+25.8 % at 20 nm). The sign reverses only at strong screening and wide gaps, and the crossing is inside §3.
- **The lateral decay length of a load perturbation in a slit is not the Debye length.** It is the transverse eigenvalue, `q₀² ≥ κ² + (π/2h)²` for a potentiostatic electrode and a fixed-charge tile, so the edge width is 0.62–0.84 `λ_D` at 2 mM and it **narrows as the gap closes**. Cheap, closed form, and a rigorous ceiling within linear theory.
- **"This boundary exerts no force" and "this boundary's charge does not matter" are different statements.** The vertical traction on a vertical wall is `ε E_z E_x` and `E_x` there is fixed by the wall's own Neumann condition, so an uncharged rim contributes *exactly* zero — while its charge still moves the edge load profile by 1.85×.
- **Read `Ξ` from the duplex cylinder charge density, not the projected one.** The projected density gives a PB contact density 89× past close packing; the high projected values describe the far field, which is where PB works anyway.
- **Two unbonded duplexes in mM Mg²⁺ have NO equilibrium separation** — repulsive at every separation, on four independent measured methods, and the array swells without bound as the load goes to zero. So a hard-body exclusion width between them is a **threshold on an energy**, not a distance, and it moves **3.93 nm per e-fold** of the threshold at the loose end (the Debye length) and **0.24 nm per e-fold** at the tight end (the measured hydration law). **A `k_BT` criterion is the criterion for two FREE bodies** and gives 8.8 nm here; both bodies in a plan model are covalently rooted to the same sheet, so the **fold** pays, not `k_BT`.
- **The host sheet is a CALIBRATION of what a crossover can afford, and it is a measured object.** Holding two of its own duplexes at the SAXS 2.69 nm costs 0.80 `k_BT`/nm, 32 `k_BT` per 40 nm interface and **8.0 `k_BT` per crossover column** — and origami folds. Anything cheaper than that is affordable by construction; the disputed plan gap costs 4.9.
- **DNA–DNA force curves are published in INTERAXIAL coordinates and that is the convention-free axis.** Converting to a surface separation forces a choice between the field's 2.0 nm hard diameter and `T-71`'s measured 1.8173 nm phosphate contact — 0.183 nm apart, which was **41 %** of the quantity `T-139` was resolving. The **charge** radius and the **hard** radius are different numbers inside one calculation and must be carried separately.
- **A hexagonal osmotic-stress array converts to a pair force EXACTLY: `f_∥(d) = Π(d)·d/√3`**, from `Π = −∂F/∂A` with `F = 3g(d)` and `A = (√3/2)d²`. It is the one step that can silently carry a wrong lattice factor — difference the pair energy numerically and assert it returns the `Π` it was built from.
- **Two duplexes END TO END are a different pair problem from two side by side, and the closed forms differ by 15× at the same separation.** Crossed: `2πτ²l_B k_BT e^(−κD)/κ`. Coaxial: `τ²l_B k_BT[e^(−κg)/κ − g·E₁(κg)]`, which is **finite at contact** because `g E₁ → 0`. So a *collinear* gap in a plan model is not a steric exclusion at all; what it must prevent is a blunt-end **stacking bond** (−2.6 kcal/mol per helix, established origami motif), whose whole range is **0.5–1.3 nm**.
- **An unretarded DLVO ALWAYS has a secondary minimum**, because every repulsive term is an exponential and Lifshitz is a power law. Here it is `0.006 k_BT` at 37 nm — report it with its depth and its separation, never as an equilibrium; `C-0021`'s *"a stable equilibrium is not a confinement"* applies verbatim.
- **Mg²⁺ does not condense duplex DNA at any concentration, and that empirical fact is the ONLY bound this project has on strong-coupling correlation attraction.** `Ξ = 17–24` at a DNA surface is exactly where mean-field PB has the wrong **sign** for like-charged rods; no theory bounds it here and a measurement does.

### DNA-origami structural parameters

- **A duplex END has exactly two strand termini, so a joint at the end of a duplex has at most TWO covalent links and a lever arm bounded by the phosphate radius, 1.0 nm.** Any base couple is `2 k_s a²` with `a ≤ r_P`, so a stiffness quoted with a lever arm taken from the *sheet* (the 2.69 nm interhelical distance) rather than from the *part* is over by the square of the ratio — 3.34× in the case that mattered. It is a count, and no force field can add a third backbone.
- **Two links lie on a chord, so a two-link joint restrains ONE axis and the orthogonal one keeps only the bonds' own hinges.** That is not a choice between a favourable and an unfavourable orientation — both are simultaneously true of the same joint — and **a column buckles about its softest axis**, so the free one governs.
- **The phosphate radius in B-DNA is 10 Å, which IS the duplex's steric radius** — the 2 nm diameter is the phosphate backbone, not a shell outside it. The 8.9–9.4 Å fibre numbers are the other end of a bracket, not a correction. **But that 10 Å is a cited round number and this repository MEASURED it: 0.9086 ± 0.0665 nm** (`T-71`, 13 084 crystallographic linkages, in `MeasuredBackbone`), so steric contact is **1.817 nm** and the round 2.0 nm is 9.1 % high. Use the measured value wherever a *margin* depends on it — and note what it buys: the origami weave's measured 18.5 Å minimum clears backbone contact by **0.35 σ**, so **the weave minimum IS the steric floor** and no groove interdigitation has to be invoked.
- **The intrastrand phosphodiester step is a WINDOW, 0.60–0.70 nm (C3′-endo to C2′-endo), not a reach.** A phosphate pair too *close* cannot be bonded either. Minimising a bare distance in a junction-closure search parks the optimum on the van der Waals floor and reports a clash as a link.
- **And that window has NO primary source — its chain ends at two textbooks — while the measurement is 0.607 / 0.664 nm.** Measured here on 13 084 crystallographic linkages: the ordering and the pucker coupling hold, but the C2′-endo end is **5.1 % shorter** than the 0.70 in circulation. Every closure search in this repository has been searching a window wider at the top than the backbone is.
- **A phosphate-distance criterion is not merely incomplete, it is ANTI-correlated with torsion feasibility.** Minimising a *window residual* is satisfied anywhere inside the window, so a shortest-gap tie-break drives the optimum to the **edge** — and the edge is exactly where no populated backbone exists. Of 3 546 placements that close on distance, **47.7 % close at no torsion whatever**, and the feasible ones sit in the window's *interior*. Search on the torsion criterion; do not filter by it afterwards.
- **The cheap torsion bound is a REACH interval and it is a proof, not a search.** With ideal bonds and the two intervening angles, the chain `O3′–P–O5′–C5′` spans `[0.269, 0.385] nm` (`[0.223, 0.409]` at three measured σ), a closed form in the single torsion `α`. A link whose donor `O3′` and acceptor `C5′` fall outside it closes at **no torsion**, in microseconds — which is why an oxDNA or all-atom minimisation is the wrong *first* spend: a minimiser reports a local minimum where a reach bound reports an impossibility.
- **Reapplying one measured nucleotide at its own fitted helical screw does NOT reproduce a real dinucleotide** — the reconstructed `O3′···P` is out by 0.0145 nm, i.e. **7.6 bond-length standard deviations**. A local helical axis fitted from coordinates is an approximation and at a covalent bond's tolerance it is not a small one. Carry the **actual successor residue**; a free limiting case built from a copy calibrates the copy.
- **A k-means conformer class cannot serve as a "populated region" test.** The diffuse classes of a DNA backbone survey have 99th-percentile radii above **150°**, so *"inside the nearest class"* admits almost anything — a baseline landed 133° from its own class and was called populated. Use a **marginal ten-degree occupancy histogram** per torsion instead; it is what lets a verdict say *"β = 27° lies in a bin holding zero of 15 457 observed residues"*.
- **Averaging crystallographic coordinates in a fitted frame CONTRACTS every internal bond** — the mean of points scattered on a sphere lies inside it — so an averaged nucleotide template had a `C5′–O5′` bond 3 % short, which is not a molecule. Emit the population **medoid**: one real measured residue, whose internal covalent geometry is a molecule's.
- **`δ` carries the sugar pucker and is not an independent variable.** C3′-endo (`0 ≤ P ≤ 36°`) has `δ ≈ 82–86°`; C2′-endo (`144 ≤ P ≤ 190°`) has `δ ≈ 130–143°`. Pinning a link's P···P distance pins the pucker and therefore pins `δ` with it — never sweep the two as if they were separate.
- **The Svozil/NAR B-DNA torsion table publishes 95 % CONFIDENCE INTERVALS ON THE MEAN, not spreads.** *"α = 299.0 ± 0.9°"* read as a tolerance is wrong by ~10×; back-converting with `CI = 1.96 σ/√N` gives population SDs of 6–12°.
- **A flat duplex end on a round duplex is a LINE contact, and it is one-sided.** It blocks rocking along the line to first order, leaves ~30° across it, and can react only `N·R` of moment before it lifts. Never credit it as a rotational restraint without comparing that capacity.
- **The crossover spacing is 32 bp, not 16 bp, for a single-layer Rothemund sheet.** Crossovers recur every 1.5 turns (16 bp) along a helix but *alternate between its two neighbours*, so a given adjacent pair is linked every 32 bp; honeycomb is 21 bp per interface. Using the per-helix number where the per-interface one belongs doubles the across-helix flexural rigidity.
- **A square-lattice helix has FOUR crossover azimuths at 8 bp and a single-layer sheet occupies TWO, so the sheet's own crossovers are NOT the inventory of junction sites its lattice offers.** `8 bp × 33.75°/bp = 270°` exactly, so the two unoccupied azimuths point **out of the sheet plane** — Ke et al. (*JACS* **131**:15903) state it verbatim, planes i/iii ⊥ ii/iv. A 15-duplex 40 nm tile offers **161–176** sites and builds **49–56**: it occupies **under a third** of its own lattice at every phase, and 1/3 is the asymptote (four azimuths, the two in-plane ones shared with a neighbour). Any budget written on the built crossovers is a budget on a third of the lattice.
- **Every upward site of a single-layer square-lattice sheet is the SAME helical phase of its OWN duplex — 24 bp from that duplex's `NORTH` plane — because adjacent rows' duplexes are phase-shifted by exactly the 16 bp their sites are offset by** (`(k − 2b) mod 4`). So a question about how an element **registers** on the host's backbone is **one** question over a whole array, not one per station, and the placed count is **quantised at 0 or N**. A lattice congruence that fixes the *shape* of an answer is worth more than a bound that brackets its value.
- **A seven-column sheet is a NON-UNIFORM lattice by DESIGN, and there the two `D_⊥` readings must disagree.** Seven columns split **4/3** between the two crossover parities, so seven of a 15-duplex sheet's fourteen interfaces carry three crossovers and seven carry four: the **smeared** rigidity, linear in the count, loses `49/56 = 7/8`, while the **series** one — a harmonic mean, which is how a sheet actually bends across its helices — loses `42/49 = 6/7`. Exactly **48/49** apart, and the smeared reading is the optimistic one. The same 4/3 split makes the sheet **3.59×** likelier to lose *every* crossover on some interface under measured staple incorporation, which takes the series `D_⊥` to **exactly zero**. `C-0054`'s *"75 % spendable"* is an eight-column number; at seven it is 71.4 %.
- **A crossover PLANE lands on the row end modulo 8 bp and is a COLUMN only modulo 16 bp, so "an end plane has an even index" is true at half the phases that have one.** At a 112 bp row the end plane exists at phases 0, 8, 16 and 24 and is a column only at 8 and 24; at 0 and 16 its index is **odd**, so admitting the row-end crossover adds **fifteen upward stations and no columns** (45 → 60) instead of the reverse. A lattice statement quantified over *"any phase"* has to be checked at every residue of the **coarser** lattice it silently assumes — `CH-0118`, and the ninth *"quote it with the state it was read at"* here.
- **The register departure from a design twist is LINEAR in the base-pair offset, so an unoccupied site can be less strained than an occupied one.** At the preferred 10.5 bp/turn the 8 bp out-of-plane site is off by 4.286° and the sheet's own next in-plane crossover, 16 bp away, by 8.571° — exactly 2×. "Unused" is not evidence of "unusable"; check the offset before assuming the phase forbids it.
- **A crossover 8 bp from another forces an 8 nt staple domain, and that is a published YIELD cost.** Ke et al.: *"some staple breaks must be implemented between crossovers 8 bp apart … We observed significantly lower yield … Introducing these breaks may be destabilizing"* — and they raised the yield of their 8 × 8 block by **omitting** crossovers. The cost of a denser crossover pattern is folding yield, not geometry.
- **A scaffold SEAM is a parity on a tree, not a fabrication convention.** Crossovers join only *adjacent* duplexes, so a single-layer sheet's row-adjacency graph is the path `P_D`, a **tree** — and a closed walk on a tree traverses every edge an **even** number of times. A **fully folded circular** scaffold therefore gives every row **two** segments, which is exactly Rothemund's seam; a **linear** scaffold needs only a Hamiltonian path (`P_D` has 2, and **zero** cycles, brute-forced at every `D` from 3 to 12), and a circular scaffold left partly unfolded closes through its own remainder. **A seam needs BOTH premises and dropping either removes it** — which is why Rothemund's 26-helix square *"had no vertical reversals … required a linear scaffold"* and his rectangles do not.
- **A seamless raster quantises the tile's WIDTH at 32 bp, not at the rise.** A boustrophedon has only *progressive* scaffold crossovers, so Rothemund's *"the distance between successive scaffold crossovers must be an odd number of half turns"* binds the **row length**: at 1.5-turn (16 bp) spacing the admissible widths are the **odd multiples of 16 bp** — 16, 48, 80, 112, 144 — and a nominal 40.0 nm (117.6 bp) is not among them. The nearest buildable tile is **38.08 nm**. A specified dimension is not automatically a drawable one.
- **Rothemund's own STAGGERED seam is not a remedy for a station registration.** Solved exactly over the three-row coupling a weave model implies, the best staggered seam on a 40 nm tile still takes **5 of 34** stations off their weave node against a straight seam's **6–12**. The cost is a property of a tile **3.68 weave periods wide carrying 34 stations**, not of the seam's straightness, and no contour through it misses them all.
- **The many-tie rigid second layer has a published precedent and the single-tie arm does not, which is the opposite way round from this programme's design bias.** A duplex crossovered to a sheet at an out-of-plane azimuth *and tied at many sites* is the elementary step of square-lattice multilayer origami (Ke et al. 2009); the undemonstrated motif is the one **free at a single crossover**. So a coupling built as a second layer buys back the precedent that `C-0055`/`C-0061`'s arm spends — at the price of a **two-layer body §3 does not describe**, which is a specification question rather than a calculation.
- **Every published DNA-origami hinge is an ssDNA connection, not a crossover.** A 2023 mechanics review says so in as many words (*"a nanoscale hinge realized using ssDNA connections"*, `PMC10395309`); 62 recorded queries find no crossover-rooted flexure hinge, in plane or out of it. `n k_θ` as a hinge spring is this programme's own construct and should be flagged as such wherever it is priced.
- `10.67 bp/turn` is the **square** lattice and `10.5 bp/turn` the **honeycomb**, not the other way round.
- **Interhelical distance is measured, not designed** — 2.69 nm single-layer, 2.73 square, 2.54 honeycomb (SAXS, Fischer et al. 2016). The ~3.0 nm that circulates is Rothemund's *inferred* 1 nm gap; CanDo's 2.25 nm is an assumed helix diameter. Do not substitute one for another.
- **Those numbers are BRAGG LATTICE CONSTANTS, not local separations, and the same paper's SI carries their WIDTH.** The measured interhelical distance is a *deterministic sawtooth* — **18.5 Å at a crossover to 36 Å midway** (Bai et al., *PNAS* 109:20012, cryo-EM; confirmed by all-atom MD and by oxDNA on a 2D tile) — so the minimum is **inside** the 2.0 nm steric diameter this project asserts, and a hard-body plan model using 2.69 nm as an exclusion width samples neither end. Fischer's own SI Tables S5/S6 give the **single-layer sheet** a fitted lattice width `w_a` = 2.5 Å on 27.41 Å (**9.1 %**) and a Lorentzian `B/q₀` of **15.2 %**, against **2.9 %** for the multilayer brick: **the single-layer sheet is the worst-ordered origami anyone has measured**, and Kube et al. (2020) could not solve one at all, *"due to excessive conformational heterogeneity"*.
- **The weave is a TRIANGULAR wave whose extrema are the crossover planes, and the out-of-plane roots are the ODD planes — so every upward station sits on a NODE.** Snodin et al. (*NAR* 47:1585, oxDNA on a 2D tile) state the phase verbatim: minima at the interface's own crossovers, maxima where the *adjacent* pair has its crossover, period 32 bp, *"'triangular wave' character … the intervening sections basically straight"*. A duplex's crossover planes are `k ≡ 2b` and `k ≡ 2b+2 (mod 4)` — **even** — and its `EAST`/`WEST` sites are `k ≡ 2b±1` — **odd for every duplex**. So the interhelical distance at an out-of-plane root is the lattice constant **exactly**, at every phase and at **every amplitude**: the whole 1.2–1.75 nm amplitude disagreement between cryo-EM, all-atom MD and oxDNA has **coefficient zero** there. **Check a measurement's PHASE against the lattice before pricing its amplitude** — a deterministic pattern whose phase you know is not a tolerance. And the parity is a design variable: an element of an **even** number of 8 bp planes puts both ends on nodes, an odd one (the 24 bp arm) puts its tip on an antinode.
- **A plan model's `d` is THREE quantities and only one is an interhelical distance**: the host's **row pitch** (two crossover-*bonded* duplexes, *across* the helices — the weave's own quantity), a free body's **girth** (one body, steric), and the **collinear clearance** charged *along* the helices between two *unbonded* bodies. Writing one symbol for all three is how a 0.04 nm change in one constant comes out worth 12 arms or 4 depending on which role it is applied to — and how a measured across-helix sawtooth got read through an along-helix margin (`CH-0088`). Before substituting a measurement into an exclusion width, ask which **axis** it is on and whether the two bodies are **bonded**.
- **A per-domain register offset is not the register error a structure CARRIES.** Every 16 bp domain of a square-lattice sheet is undertwisted by the same 8.571° (33.75°/bp design against B-DNA's 34.286°), *same sign*, so the error **accumulates** along a duplex — Rothemund says so in as many words (*"the helical domains between crossovers are slightly overtwisted or undertwisted"*) — and what limits it is the duplex's own torsion, not the next crossover. The smeared energy `∫[½C(u′−Δω)² + ½(k_θ/p)u²]` gives a boundary layer `λ = √(Cp/k_θ)` with the **natural** end condition `u′ = Δω`, so a free row end keeps `Δω λ tanh(L/2λ)`. At Gen-1 that is `λ = 12–24 nm` against a **19 nm half-row**: the tile is too small for its own duplexes to untwist their way out, the relief is only 17–43 %, and a row end carries **17–25°** where the per-domain ladder says 8.6–17.1°. Quote the accumulated value, never the per-domain one.
- **Two independent sign alternations can CANCEL, and then the favourable distribution is not available at all.** Rothemund's glide symmetry flips the crossover type with the interface parity (`(−1)^b`, and `C-0015`'s parity rule makes column parity *be* interface parity), and a boustrophedon's raster turns alternate ends, so the row-end crossover of interface `b` sits where the odd field `u` is `(−1)^b u_max`. The two compose to `+1` at **every** interface: the physical map is the **uniform** one, which is the adverse one. Each alternation alone would have been favourable. Compose the symmetries before choosing a distribution.
- **A missing staple is a DROPOUT, not a scatter, and it has been measured.** Incorporation over all 168 staples of a Rothemund rectangle is **48 % at the edges to 95 % in the centre, mean 84 %** (Strauss et al., *Nat. Commun.* 9:1600, DNA-PAINT at single-staple resolution). A missing staple removes a load path rather than perturbing it, so the population is two-valued and `σ_rel = √(f/(1−f))` — 43.6 % at the mean, 104 % at the edges — and the mean is *also* a 16 % shortfall on any mandate written as a **sum**. Convert a relative-scatter threshold to an incorporation before calling it generous.
- **The only measured thermal-versus-fabrication split in the field is one paper and it is 2.1–3.6× in amplitude** (Dietz, Douglas & Shih, *Science* 325:725: 5.2–9.0° observed bend-angle s.d. against a 2.5° thermal prediction, the excess attributed to defects). An equipartition σ is therefore a **lower** bound on an as-built scatter, and that is the multiplier a tolerance model is entitled to.
- **A margin below 0.34 nm cannot be corrected, only removed.** DNA quantises every length at the rise, so a clearance smaller than one base pair is below the resolution of the design language: there is no shorter increment to trade with, and no scatter measurement can make it recoverable.
- **CanDo treats crossovers as rigid, and says so.** Fine for a multilayer bundle, where that degree of freedom is geometrically frustrated; wrong for a single-layer sheet, where it is the *only* across-helix compliance. Its `EI = 230 pN·nm²` is a model input, not a measurement — it implies `L_p = 55.5 nm` against ~40–47 nm measured.
- **A seamless raster quantises the tile's WIDTH, and the quantised width then SELECTS the crossover phase.** The only buildable seamless row length near §3's 40.0 nm is 112 bp = **38.08 nm**, which is `7 × 5.44` **exactly** where 40.0 nm is 7.35 — so the row-end scaffold crossover, the one that turns the raster, is a lattice point only at `phase ≡ −L/2 (mod 16 bp)`, i.e. **phases 8 and 24**, which are exactly `C-0063`'s two centro-symmetric phases. The **ten** eight-column phases of a 40 nm tile collapse to those **two**. The routing and the phase stop being independent choices, and the station lattice at them is *bit-identical* to the 40 nm one.
- **A numerical guard becomes a physical assertion the moment the lattice lands on it.** `CrossoverLayout.EDGE_MARGIN = 0.05 nm` exists so a column cannot seed a zero-length beam element, and at 40.0 nm no base-pair phase brings a column within 0.28 nm of the edge, so it is inert. At 38.08 nm it **deletes two of eight columns at exactly the two phases the design wants**, and the flatness verdict moves 0.0621 → 0.1684, across `T-5b`'s 0.10. Sweep the guard (here 0.05 nm / half a rise / one rise: 0.32 % spread) rather than assuming it is still inert at the geometry you are running.
- **A plan budget is a MINIMUM of two bounds, and only one of them carries the tile width.** `C-0069`'s `pitch − d = 8.19 nm` is the **inboard** bound and contains no tile dimension; the **outboard** bound is `edgeX/2 − outermost root` and contains no interhelical distance. They cross at `edgeX = 2(2p − d) = 38.14 nm`, and the only buildable seamless width, 38.08 nm, falls **0.176 base pairs** below it — so there the tile edge owns the budget (8.16 nm, exactly 24 rises) and `C-0085`'s widening of the *inboard* bound to 8.84 nm buys nothing at all. At 40.0 nm the outboard bound is slack by 0.93 nm, which is why nine claims wrote the budget as a lattice constant.
- **The crossover column *phase* has period 32 bp, not 16 bp, and it is quantised to base pairs.** A layout chooses the *phase* of the column lattice and the count follows (a 40 nm tile fits 8 columns at 10 of the 32 phases and 7 at the other 22). A shift by one column pitch leaves every column position unchanged and hands every interface **the other parity's** columns — a physically different sheet. Sweeping `[0, 16 bp)` covers half the design space while looking complete.
- **Centro-symmetry is a property of the phase, not of the sheet** — it holds exactly when (columns + duplexes) is odd, and 22 of the 32 phases of a 40 nm tile have **no** symmetry at all. Do not generalise a symmetry asserted on one lattice.
- **REFLECTING an out-of-plane array moves it to the OTHER FACE of the sheet.** A helix's two out-of-plane azimuths are a **half-pitch** (16 bp) apart, so a reflection of a lattice of pitch `p` maps a row's `EAST` sites exactly onto its `WEST` ones — `k ≡ 2r+3 → 2r+1 (mod 4)`, identically, every row. A mirror image of an *upward* array is a *downward* one, i.e. arms hanging into the grafted layer. "Mirror the odd rows" is not a free relabelling; check the azimuth, not the column.
- **A placement's own phase and its HOST's phase are ONE variable**, because the same 8 bp plane lattice carries both: the sheet's columns are the planes at even `k`, an upward root is a plane at `k ≡ 2r+3 (mod 4)`. Reading an array placed at one phase on a host built at another is worth **38.9 %** of the dishing here — and the two differ by exactly the 7-column/8-column split.
- **Which stations a coupling enters at is worth more than how its stiffness is distributed.** On the same 34 upward roots, sweeping the row phases takes the dishing 0.4156 → **0.0706** with *equal* springs, past what `C-0058`'s 5:1 distribution reaches on a 3 × 15 grid (0.0753) — and on the swept placement the rim rule **reverses sign** (0.2214 at ×5). A distribution rule is a property of a station set, never of a tile.
- **A hinge of `n` crossovers is bounded by COLLINEAR INTERFACE, not by the tile's crossover inventory**: they must share one interface to add in parallel, so they need `(n − 1) × 32 bp`. A 40 nm tile carries **four**, at every one of the 32 phases (three on the other parity at 22 of them), and sixteen would need **163 nm**, 4.08 tiles. The maximum coincides with the ten centro-symmetric phases, both being the eight-column ones. Count a hinge before pricing it — `C-0023`, `C-0029` and `C-0034` all took sixteen as given.
- **`k_θ` is a DIHEDRAL spring about a line running ALONG the helices**, so a fold *across* them is not a crossover hinge at all — it is resisted by the duplexes' own `EI`. A transverse line of crossovers on 15 duplexes has seven members and none of them restrains the axis such a fold turns about. Check the axis before counting the members.
- **Parallel hinge lines compose in SERIES, and the outboard ones sit on short lever arms**: for a raft on `m` equally spaced lines of `n_i` each, `n_eff = n_i·3(2m − 1)/(m(2m + 1))`, exactly `n_i` at `m = 1`. Sixteen crossovers as a 4 × 4 fan are worth **2.33** of hinge — 14.6 % of their own count — so "we can find sixteen crossovers" is not "we have a sixteen-crossover hinge". Its continuum control is `2m(2m+1)/(2m−1)²`, 6.00 at one interface and 1.47 at four, **always above one**: the lattice fan is the softer of the two.
- **The peak per-load-path force is governed by the attachment's distance to the nearest crossover, and the duplex shear runs the opposite way.** Across the cell the crossover force falls 1.4× while the duplex shear rises 1.8×, so the two optima are at opposite corners; the crossover wins only because it is judged against 10–15 pN unzip and the duplex against the 65 pN nicked ceiling.
- **One attachment row per duplex makes the per-load-path force exactly zero** — every beam carries the identical load, so no interface transmits anything. Same symmetry as "a uniform load dishes nothing", and just as fragile: any load non-uniformity restores it in proportion.
- **A crossover-hinged arm is CUT OUT of its host sheet, not added to it** — its tip is a duplex end and its root must be *doubly* nicked from the rest of its own row, because a single nick is a clamp. So an array of them spends duplex **length** as well as crossovers, and **buries** further crossovers under itself: 43 arms of 9.13 nm take 65 % of a 40 nm sheet's duplex length, spend 43 crossovers and bury 13, which is 56 of 56. A crossover *inventory* ledger contains neither term and over-reports the buildable count by 1.7×.
- **A hinge can only be rooted where a crossover is, and a row sees only its own two interfaces.** Those carry opposite parities, so an interior row's roots sit at 16 bp = 5.44 nm and an **edge** row's at 32 bp = 10.88 nm — worth exactly one element per edge row, and it does not dilute with tile size. **Inventory is not placement**: 49–56 crossovers against 45 demanded looks tight and admits 43.
- **Search attachment schemes over grid *shapes*, not over counts.** The sheet is 25.6× stiffer along the helices than across them, so the square diagonal is the wrong slice: flatness needs 45 attachments as 3 × 15, against 64 as 8 × 8.
- **A ROOTED element has a direction and a centred one does not** — sweep `[0, 2π)`, not `[0, π)`. It is worth a factor of two in the feasible-orientation count and, more usefully, an extra element wherever a cluster of lattice sites is narrower than one element: one runs each way out of it.
- **Whenever a lattice effect is claimed, run the continuum plate beside it and quote the excess.** Two candidate design rules here survived the lattice and died against the plate — it feels a grid moving toward a free edge just as much, and that was most of the effect.
- **A discretisation is not automatically a relaxation.** Replacing a continuum plate by the beam-and-hinge lattice it approximates makes the tile *softer* under a point load entering it (12–38 %) and thermally (11–20 %), but *stiffer* under a point reaction and a smooth load (1–16 %) — a 2 nm duplex cannot bend across its own diameter and the continuum lets it. Do not assume a lattice is the compliant one.
- **The lattice-over-plate excess is a property of the LOAD PATH, not of the tile, and it changes sign inside one study**: −8.1 % at a uniform 45-path coupling and **+23.9 %** at the same 45 paths carrying a rim-stiffened distribution. Quote the excess with the distribution it was read at, and never transfer one measured at another.
- **A discreteness criterion must pair lengths in the same direction.** `ℓ_⊥/p` compares an across-helix bending length with an along-helix hinge spacing. Matched, they are `ℓ_∥/p` and `ℓ_⊥/d`, differing from `ℓ_⊥/p` by more than 2×. Better, quote the number of crossovers inside an anchor's influence patch — 2–8 here — which needs no convention at all.
- **INVERT a discreteness criterion before quoting it, because one of them is degenerate.** Depleting a lattice makes every criterion an exact power of the retained count (`ℓ_⊥/d ∝ N^(1/4)`, `ℓ_∥/p_eff ∝ N`, patch `∝ N^(5/4)`), so each closes for the count it crosses one at. Inverted, `ℓ_∥/p` demands **67.2** crossovers on a sheet that owns 56 — it reads "invalid" at *zero* consumption too and therefore carries **no information** about depletion; `ℓ_⊥/d` crosses only past severance. Only the patch count crosses inside the design region, at **37.3 of 56 spent**, which is *five crossovers before the sheet even disconnects*.
- **`ℓ_⊥/d` cannot tell crossover DEPLETION from a stiffer FOUNDATION** — it depends only on `D_⊥/k_f`, so retaining a quarter of the crossovers is *exactly* a fourfold `k_f` for it, to the last digit, while the other two criteria separate the same pair of states by exactly `2√2`. A layer stiffness and a hinge count are not interchangeable and this criterion says they are.
- **"Spread over the interfaces" does not spread over the COLUMNS.** `C-0054`'s connectivity-optimal round robin is optimal for the pigeonhole and its column tie-break takes the lowest available site, so at the ceiling all fourteen survivors sit in the two lowest columns. Same count, same areal density, **1.68×** in the distance from an anchor to the nearest surviving crossover. Where the survivors sit is a design variable; sweep it or bracket it.
- **A continuum density and an integer census stop being the same statement below one element per patch**, and that is discreteness in its purest form: 0.694 crossovers per anchor patch is 0 or 1 depending on where the anchor lands, and 28 of 45 stations have none at all. No smeared rigidity can express it.
- **A plan overlap with a member that has to reach the TILE is level-independent, so the SECTION decides and the PLAN answers.** A tie-down's clear column runs from the tile's own top face to whatever plane it hangs from, and therefore contains *every* slab above the tile — stacking, re-ordering and a larger body are all unavailable. Check that containment before pricing an area: 0.664 of the footprint settled nothing.
- **Two arrays on one face compete for a REGISTRATION, not for room.** 34 arms leave **108** places a 2.69 nm tie could stand against the 45 demanded, and yet 26 of `C-0015`'s 45 grid stations are unusable and **no rigid translation** of a two- or three-column grid clears every row. An area bound says yes and a capacity count says yes; only the lattice says no.
- **Displacing a coupling ALONG the helices is nearly free; across them it is not.** Moving 45 tie stations by up to 4.33 nm to dodge an arm array costs **1.7 %** of the dishing, because `C-0026`'s one-row-per-duplex registration is an *across*-helix statement and the escape was entirely *along*.
- **A rotating arm's plan projection is a COSINE, so its swept envelope is the REST footprint.** `√(L² − s²)` shrinks with the stroke, so a static plan view is conservative at every stroke and the clearance is worst at zero — and the same arithmetic refuses a stroke longer than the arm, which is how a kinematic ceiling shows up in a plan view.
- **A variable an upstream claim reports as free may be forced by the boundary.** The 34 arm senses `C-0063` calls a free variable are forced on **8 of 15 rows** — a row rooted at `±16.32 nm` cannot point its arm off the tile — so the whole exhaustive freedom is worth 30 → 26 clashes of 45. Enumerate it before spending anything on it.
- **A load-path contour average is not a peak.** A rigid anchor is carried by its **two nearest crossovers and essentially nothing else**, so spreading its reaction over the paths on an `ℓ`-contour understates the peak by 2.3–7.6×. Relatedly: a Rothemund sheet is **centro-symmetric, not mirror-symmetric**, because crossovers alternate between a helix's two neighbours — a plate has the full rectangular group and cannot represent this.
- **The crossover count and phase are design variables, not convergence parameters.** Seven crossover columns across a 40 nm tile instead of eight moves the peak per-load-path force by 19 %, and the anchor's placement within the unit cell by another 30 %. Report them; do not average them away.
- **An INITIAL STRESS is a load, not a stiffness, and that decides both its cost and its trap.** A crossover prestrain `θ₀` makes the hinge store `½ k_θ(Δφ − θ₀)²`, whose quadratic term is unchanged — so no entry of the stiffness matrix moves, the field is **linear** in `θ₀`, one solve fixes the whole axis and the triangle inequality gives a rigorous ceiling. The trap is the other side of the same fact: a term that lives on the **structure** and enters **every** load case contaminates every *influence function* computed from that structure. `δw_j` under a unit load at `k` becomes that influence **plus** the prestrain's own response, the Woodbury compliance matrix stops being a compliance, and it fails as a non-positive Cholesky pivot — loudly at 1 rad, and **silently at the 4–17° that matter**. Take the free field on the prestrained lattice and the influences on `withoutPrestrain`; the influences are then prestrain-independent and one bank serves the whole sweep. The same trap bit a *diagnostic* in the same study: the standing uniform-load falsifier read on a prestrained lattice reported **0.833** where the load-only reading is `2.1e−7`.
- **`CLAUDE.md`'s best falsifier does NOT transfer to an eigenstrain.** *"A uniform load on a uniform Winkler foundation produces no dishing at all"* is true because a rigid translation equilibrates it. A **uniform prestrain** is an eigenstrain, and the state that relaxes every hinge and every vertical link at once is a **cylinder of curvature `θ₀/d`**: the free 38.08 nm tile goes 0.299 → 0.638 of the stroke under a uniform 17.14° on all 56 crossovers, and the sagitta `κL²/12` predicts it. Asserting the zero would have reported a correct solver as broken.
- **A row-end crossover's PRESTRAIN can move a verdict where its stiffness cannot, and the reason is that a load need not be small to be admissible.** Destroying all 14 row-end dihedral springs is worth 3.0 percentage points of margin (`C-0099`); building them at the **32 bp per-interface register**, 17.14°, takes `C-0090`'s recommended placement to **0.1013** and past `T-5b`'s 0.10. The threshold is **15.45°** and the lattice's own register ladder straddles it (8.571° at the 16 bp column pitch). Re-optimising the placement recovers it — 0.0711 in the adverse sign — but the argmin **moves**, so the recommended *design*, not just its value, depends on an unmeasured parameter.
- **A crossover is TWO elements and only one of them is `D_⊥`.** The dihedral spring `k_θ` carries the across-helix rigidity; the vertical link is a *constraint* tying two duplex surfaces together and carries no rigidity at all — and a continuum plate has **no parameter for it**. So scaling `D_⊥` is not a model of removing crossovers: over the same range the smeared plate's fluctuation moves 31 % and the lattice's **117 %**. `C-0010`'s *"a 2× change in `D_⊥` is worth 2.5 %"* is right and is the wrong channel.
- **A bracket between two MODELS is not a bracket on a parameter, and the check is a term-by-term decomposition.** `C-0090`'s two end-of-row readings differ in **three** things — the dihedral spring, the vertical link and a mesh node — and the 2.71× between them is **97.40 % the link, 2.85 % the spring, −0.25 % the node**. Sweeping the only *elastic* one of the three over its whole range, to exactly zero, moves the answer **1.05×**. Subtract the models term by term before reading the interval between them as the range of one parameter; here it turned an open verdict risk into 3.0 percentage points of margin, and it cost no solve.
- **Scaling a PENALTY is a step, not a ramp.** A crossover's vertical link is a constraint carried by a penalty *whose value the answer must not depend on* — so an eighth of it still enforces the constraint: the same lattice reads 0.0651 at `s = 0.125` and **0.1686** at `s = 0` exactly. A bisection on such a channel locates a **discontinuity**, not a threshold, and the only two physical states of a constraint are present and absent. A covalent tie is therefore a **binary**, and asking how stiff it is, is asking the wrong question.
- **A crossover cannot be a hinge and an interface at once, and the constraint that follows is TOPOLOGICAL, not elastic.** A hinge line is crossovers sharing one interface *and one pair of bodies*, so what is outboard of a turning hinge is not the sheet. The interfaces form a **path graph** on the duplexes, so a connected sheet needs one retained crossover on **each** of its `D − 1` interfaces: the hinge budget is `4(D−1) − (D−1)`, i.e. **75 % at every tile size**, 42 of 56 here. Spending 42 costs 11 % of the flatness; spending three more costs 92 %, because the step is at **severance**, not at consumption.
- **Across-helix bending is fourteen hinge lines in SERIES, so `D_⊥` is a harmonic mean and one empty interface annihilates it.** `L_y k_θ/(L_x Σ 1/n_i)` against the imposed-field `k_θ d² N/A`; they agree on a uniform lattice to exactly `(D/(D−1))²` and part company completely on a depleted one — 0.667 pN·nm and an anisotropy of 128 where the truth is zero and unbounded.
- **The lattice/plate sign rule reverses at severance.** `C-0009`'s rule (the lattice is *stiffer* under a smooth load and a point reaction) holds while the sheet is connected — 0.87 at the connectivity ceiling — and inverts to **1.62** one crossover past it, because the plate cannot represent a severed interface. *"A discretisation is not automatically a relaxation"* is true on both sides and the sign is a property of the topology.
- **The rigid limit of a tile is a stiff SHEET, not a stiff foundation.** Stiffening a Winkler foundation makes the tile *conform* to the load, which is the opposite of rigid, and a cut-equilibrium identity written for a rigid tile then diverges rather than converging. Take the limit on `EI`, `GJ` and `k_θ`.
- **A flatness count minimised under a uniform load is set by the tolerance and nothing else**, because a free tile on a uniform foundation dishes exactly zero and the objective tends to zero at large count. Under the *solved* electrostatic load the same criterion saturates at 0.149 of the stroke between 45 and 225 attachments and never reaches 10 %. Quote a flatness count with its load case — the fifth instance of this discipline, after stiffness-with-a-compression, variance-with-a-bandwidth, rupture-force-with-a-loading-rate and `k_es`-with-a-gap.
- **An attachment coupling can be a NET DISHING SOURCE, and the sign flips at an attachment pitch of one Winkler bending length.** Below three columns on a 40 nm tile the sag a sparse coupling adds between its own attachments exceeds the rim dishing it removes: under the solved load the *uncoupled* tile dishes 0.308 of the stroke and a 1 × 15 coupling dishes 0.695. **Always run the uncoupled tile as the reference** — a count sweep that starts above the break-even (as 45 → 225 did) cannot see that the axis reverses.
- **A DROPOUT IS AN INCREASE IN THE ATTACHMENT PITCH, so a fabrication yield converts into a DENSITY requirement in one division and with no solve.** Surviving `j` consecutive absences in a row needs `columns ≥ (j + 1)·edgeX/ℓ`; at the measured staple incorporation the 90th-percentile longest run is **3**, so the Gen-1 tile needs **13 columns — 195 paths** against the **34** its plan ceiling admits, **5.7×** short. That is a floor, not the answer: a denser array has more places for a long run, so the fixed point of the requirement is above 13. **The count, not the placement and not the distribution, is what fabrication charges on** — the 90th-percentile dishing is monotone in the path count (0.8522 → 0.5327 over 15 → 90) and six times the paths buys only 1.60×.
- **A reachable dishing floor is an ORACLE bound: it optimises over FORCE vectors and a design chooses a STIFFNESS vector once, before it is folded.** Restricted to a dropout realisation's survivors it is still a rigorous per-realisation lower bound over every distribution — and it costs `O(n³)` per realisation from one precomputed Gram matrix rather than `O(n²N)`, which is the whole difference between a bound you can put under a Monte Carlo and one you cannot. But the gap to the best **fixed** distribution is **34–255×** and it *widens* with the path count, so the floor can **exclude** and can never **admit**. Two claims read a small floor as evidence that a distribution exists; it is not.
- **The reach of one attachment is `ℓ = (4EI/k_f d)^(1/4)`, and it is four operations that predict a flatness answer AND its best repair.** 12.83 nm along the helices against 5.71 nm across, so a 2.69 nm row pitch is 0.47 `ℓ` and a one-column 40 nm pitch is 3.12 — the 25.6× rigidity anisotropy seen through a **fourth root**, which is what makes `C-0015`'s "shapes, not counts" a closed form: three columns is the last count whose pitch still falls inside one `ℓ`. The same length also locates the optimum of an alternating stagger, to 6 %.
- **Alternating an attachment's STATION across the helices is the same symmetry break as alternating its STIFFNESS, and it is FIRST order.** The *reaction* is second order — a tile's bow under a central column is even, so `w'(0) = 0` — but a crossover measures the **relative deflection of two adjacent duplexes**, and two duplexes propped at `+s/2` and `−s/2` have mirror-image *shapes* differing at `O(s)`. An 8 bp connectivity stagger therefore restores 0.389 pN under a *uniform* load, 1.9× the entire solved edge effect. Do not argue a positional perturbation away as second order without asking what the quantity is a difference **between**.
- **An optimised placement is a CANCELLATION, and a cancellation has no tolerance to a missing term.** Removing exactly **one** of `C-0063`'s 34 exhaustively-swept roots takes the dishing from 0.0706 to **0.5010** of the stroke — **7.1×**, and 5× past `T-5b`'s convention — while one of `C-0058`'s 45 takes 0.0753 to 0.3060. `n` solves, no sampling, and it bounds the whole fabrication distribution's tail from below. **The ranking reverses with it**: the denser 45-path grid loses *less* per missing path than the 34-root array, so `C-0063`'s equal-spring advantage — better at zero defects, 0.0706 against 0.0753 — inverts under the measured staple dropout (0.6391 against 0.4893 at the 90th percentile). **A design rule read off a zero-defect solve can invert under the only defect statistics anybody has measured**, and the single-path removal is the cheap bound that says so before any Monte Carlo. It is `CLAUDE.md`'s own *"an attachment coupling can be a NET DISHING SOURCE"* seen from the other side: a dropout **is** an increase in the attachment pitch, at random places.
- **A boundary-layer measurement does not transfer between two tile sizes.** A 40 × 40.35 nm tile has **1.85×** the perimeter per unit area of the 87 × 65 nm Rothemund rectangle staple incorporation was measured on, so the same measured layer gives a mean incorporation of **0.759–0.790** here against the measured **0.840**. A measured *mean* is a property of the object it was measured on; only the *profile* transfers, and it transfers in nm.
- **A placement is tuned to a LOAD FIELD, not to a tile, so a coupling flat at one layer height can be a dishing SOURCE at another.** The 34 upward roots that hold the tile flat under a 10 nm layer's solved collar — 0.0706 at the design state and 0.0789–0.0896 over the whole stroke of all three buffers — dish **1.2–1.7× worse than no coupling at all** at both states of a **5 nm** layer, whose free-tile dishing is 4.8× smaller: there is less to correct and the coupling's own sag at 34 discrete points dominates. The sixth instance of *quote it with the state it is read at*, now read as **the range it is read over**.
- **The crossover phase is selected by the LAYER, not only by the sheet.** `C-0015`'s ten eight-column phases are the *candidates*; which one a design takes is a function of the load field. Under a 10 nm layer's solved collar the flat 34-root placements live at **phase 24** (0.0789 over the device's whole stroke) and under a 5 nm layer's **nothing at phase 24 clears at all** (best of 198 288 is 0.1169) while **phase 8** does (0.0895) — and that placement dishes 0.2416 against the 10 nm layer.
- **An operating RANGE is not automatically a set of compatible states.** `C-0064`'s device partition says *which* states one design must serve and nothing about whether they can be served together: the two ends of the 5 nm device's own stroke have **anti-parallel** free-tile dishing fields (cosine −0.943) where every 10 nm device's two ends run +0.997. Run the cosine before the optimiser — it costs one pass over two precomputed fields.
- **A staggered attachment only has to stay on the tile; a staggered FLEXURE has to stay on the body.** A flexure is a beam centred on its own tie, so the half-stagger is capped at `edgeX/2 − span/2` (9.28 nm at the Gen-1 span), not at `edgeX/2` — which is why the unconstrained flatness optimum of a stagger overhangs the edge and returns half of what it appears to.
- **A DNA rupture force without a loading rate is not a material constant** — the same origami class moves from ~42 pN at 5.5 pN/s to ~75 pN at 1.8e5 pN/s. The problem definition's 35–60 pN band is a *whole-cross-section* number, not a per-load-path allowable; per path use duplex shear (~48–65 pN) or unzip (10–15 pN), with 65 pN a hard ceiling because every origami helix is nicked.
- **A sweep of operating points is not an operating RANGE, and `C-0022`'s five headline states are FOUR DEVICES.** Three are the rest states of three different *buffers* and two are the rest and *held* states of a different *layer height* — the 2 nm state is `C-0022`'s own *"held at 3 nm stroke"* row of the 5 nm device and no state of the 10 nm one. A device traverses one buffer, one layer and one bias, `L₀` → `L₀ − s`. Asking one distribution to be flat at all five is a **portfolio** duty; over each device's own range a flat distribution exists (0.0372–0.0619 against 0.1247 over the five). **Ask which device a state belongs to before intersecting states.**
- **Two operating states can be incompatible by SIGN, and the discriminant may already be in an upstream table.** The 2 nm state is the only one of `C-0022`'s 21 whose finite tile carries *less* total force than a 1-D pressure over its footprint (−3.91 % against +4.9…+19.2 %), so its free-tile dishing field is **anti-parallel** to every other state's (cosine −0.943 to −1.000, exactly −1.000 against 10 mM) and a distribution that flattens an edge *enhancement* deepens an edge *deficit*. **Compute the cosine between two states' free responses before running any minimax** — it costs one pass over precomputed fields and says whether one correction can serve both.
- **Cyclic coordinate descent on a MAX stalls on the kink, and the loss is a factor of 2.5, not a percent.** A max of smooth functions is non-differentiable along its switching surfaces, and a coordinate method halts where no *single* coordinate descends although a combination does. Replacing it with log-sum-exp smoothing + continuation + analytic gradients moved a *single-state* peak-dishing optimum from 0.0544 to 0.0214 at the same 45 paths. **A minimax value reported from a coordinate descent is not evidence of a floor.**
- **Projection onto a constrained family is not optimisation within it, and here the gap is 24.9 %.** The 45-parameter robust optimum quantised optimally onto two stiffness levels is *worse* than the best two-level design found by searching the two-level family directly (0.1002 against 0.0753). Quantise a design only to *price* it; never to *find* it.
- **The influence functions of a set of attachment stations do not depend on the load.** So one multi-state surrogate costs `n + S` load cases where a per-state one costs `S(n + 1)` — 50 solves against 230 at five states — and the whole minimax becomes affordable. The station influence matrix and the sampled dishing fields are properties of the structure; only the free response is per state.

### Actuator coupling

- **A stroke is a root of a force balance, never a force divided by a stiffness.** Three of the six layer models have *exactly zero* stiffness at `L₀`, so `F/k(L₀)` is infinite for half of them and a large overestimate for the rest.
- **The blocking force and the stroke live at opposite ends of one curve and are never delivered together.** The characteristic is `W(s) = |F_es(L₀−s,V)| − P(L₀−s)·A`; the blocking force is `W(0)`, the free stroke is its root, and `dW/dh = k_eff` exactly. Because `k_eff` can be negative, `W(0)` is **not** the largest force the actuator delivers — at the 10 nm point it understates it by 6–20×.
- **`k_es < 0` is a property of the force decaying with the gap, not an axiom.** `|F_es(h)|` peaks and then falls toward a sign change at 0.55–1.58 nm; past the peak the electrostatics *stiffens* the layer. Quote the sign with the gap it applies to.
- **A bias ceiling must be quoted with the load it was evaluated at.** The 0.02–0.1 V "usable window" is a property of the *unloaded* actuator, which snaps to near-contact; the tile *held* at the §6 target sits at a 2–7 nm gap and `φ ≤ 0.09`, inside every upstream validity range. Same class of error as quoting "the layer stiffness" without a compression, or `k_es`'s sign without a gap — the third instance in this project.
- **An output coupling has two requirements, not one, and the binding one contains no physics.** Stability fixes a *lower bound*, `k_c > |k_eff|`; **placement** fixes the *value*, because the force delivered to a load over a stroke is `k_c·Δs` — so §3's own 100 pN and 3 nm give `33.333 pN/nm` by arithmetic. A table of `|k_eff|` at arbitrary biases maps the stability boundary; it becomes a design requirement only once the bias is fixed by the acceptance clause.
- **Placement is written on the coupling's SECANT and stability on its TANGENT**, so a strain-stiffening element discharges both with one part and the whole `tangent/secant` ratio is free stability margin at zero placement cost. The third appearance of the secant/tangent split here.
- **The stiffness is rarely the constraint; the compliance is.** Forty-five DNA duplexes in tension are 148× *too stiff* for the Gen-1 coupling, and what closes the task is a 10–19 nt ssDNA spacer carrying 99.6 % of the load path. Size a coupling by what it must *give*, not by what it must resist.
- **`k_lat/k_norm ≤ 1` is not binding when the two requirements differ by 72×.** Normal stabilisation needs 33.3 pN/nm and lateral confinement 0.46, so an anchor sized on the normal condition delivers lateral confinement with 70× to spare while one sized laterally delivers 0.4 % of the normal. They want the **same** anchors; only the order of the design differs.
- **A pull-in bias cannot be bisected for — it is a *discontinuity* in the bias and a smooth *maximum* in the stroke.** Parametrise the equilibrium path by the stroke, `V_eq(s)`, and take `max_s V_eq(s)`; differentiating the balance at `V′(s) = 0` gives `k_c + k_eff = 0`, so the argmax *is* the tangency point and the two routes to it are independent checks.
- **A stiffness margin is not a bias margin, and here the gap is 10–40×.** `V(s)` is flat at a fold, so a 19–42 % reserve in `k_c/|k_eff|` is 0.7–3.2 % in bias. Quote the margin on the axis the design is controlled on.
- **A ceiling below the pull-in bias is not sufficient: the target stroke must also lie on the stable side of the fold.** Two different tests — at 7 nm / 10 mM the fold sits at 1.92–2.68 nm while §3 asks for 3 nm.
- **A ceiling belongs to a `(bias, load line)` pair.** The same layer, field and geometry give no pull-in at all unloaded (49 of 54 states), a fold at 11 of 54 coupled, and *no stable compressed equilibrium at any bias* under a dead load. Quote which load line a maximum usable bias was read on.
- **And so does the LIST of candidate ceilings, not only their values.** `C-0018`'s three — pull-in, `φ = 0.2`, the point-ion boundary — are exhaustive only for a load line defined at every stroke the layer admits, which an affine mandate and a midspan flexure are and a **rotating arm is not**: an inextensible element has a contour, a shooting solve has a branch, an entropic strand has a contour and a strut has an Euler load. Inheriting the list unexamined quotes a margin over a stroke the element model does not describe — **up to 3.74×** at exactly the headline states here. Build the candidate list from the load line's **domain**, and carry both readings.
- **A load-line substitution at a fold is exactly two corrections, and the free one usually decides.** The slope change `Δk_c` at the baseline fold costs one evaluation of each law and no field solve; the level change `ΔR` needs a re-solve. Because the baseline coupled tangent vanishes there, the sign of `Δk_c` predicts which way the fold moves — right at **11 of 11** gradable states here, including **5 where `ΔR` ran the other way**. It is 24× cheaper than one path point and it runs before anything is compiled into a sweep.
- **A convergence axis cannot be read at a state where the quantity does not exist.** Substituting the recommended element removes the fold at `C-0018`'s own binding state, so a mesh/bracket sweep there converges on `null` and silently reports nothing. Pick the axis's state *after* the sweep, from the states where the quantity survives.
- **`C-0050`'s dead-load stroke is a §3-LOAD bound, not a reachability bound.** It correctly excludes `C-0022`'s 2 nm state from a 10 nm device (8 nm demanded against 7.42 nm anywhere in the window), and applied to the **5 nm** device that *owns* that state it excludes it too (3 nm against 1.53 nm) — because that state is held by its own solved **0.368 V**, a force above §3's 100 pN. Use it to say a state is off a device's *duty*, never to say a biased actuator cannot reach it.
- **A multiplier on a force reaches the stiffness only where the operating point is not force-pinned.** At a held operating point the force balance fixes `|F_es| = 100 pN + P(g)A`, and `k_es = −|F_es|/ℓ` identically — so an enhancement of the *level* of the force is absorbed entirely into the bias and contributes **exactly zero** to `k_es`. Only `d ln μ/dh` survives, and it runs the *favourable* way. The fourth instance here of a quantity quoted at a state the device does not occupy.
- **At a force-pinned fold the whole effect of a force multiplier is `|F_es| d ln μ/dh`, so its SIGN needs no computation.** The baseline coupled tangent vanishes at the fold by construction, and a multiplier on a pinned force moves only the decay rate, `1/ℓ → 1/ℓ − d ln μ/dh`. A positive gradient therefore moves *every* fold to a deeper stroke, at every model and every load line, before any re-solve — `+2.60` to `+4.99 pN/nm` here.
- **A one-signed correction to a force is NOT a one-signed correction to a bias margin.** A margin is a ratio of two biases read at two *different* gaps — the fold gap and the operating gap — and a multiplier `μ(h)` lowers each by about `1/√μ` at its own gap. So the pull-in margin moves with the sign of `μ(h_fold) − μ(h_operating)`, i.e. with the sign of `3 nm − s_fold`: at 10 nm / 2 mM (fold deeper than §3's stroke) the same collar **raises** it 1.4 %, at 7 nm / 10 mM (fold shallower) it **lowers** it 0.9–3.5 %. Correct a ratio in both of its arguments, or it is not corrected at all.
- **Two corrections of the same size and opposite sign were published in separate claims hours apart.** One degraded the 10 nm coupling margin 7 % and the other improved it 12 %; each claim's own bracket was right and neither could carry the other. Never quote a margin corrected on one channel without checking whether the other channel's correction exists yet.
- **A hinge-and-arm flexure's arm is capped by its own placement condition at `(c·n·EI/k_target)^(1/3)`**, because the arm's bending is in *series* with the hinge — 9.77 nm for a cantilever arm at 45 paths and the Gen-1 mandate, i.e. **below §3's desired 10 nm stroke at any hinge count**. The cap contains its own remedy: the end-condition factor `c`, 3 → 12, is worth 1.59× in the cube root. A *stiffer* arm does not help — it buys stiffness where the stroke needs length.
- **`δ = r sin θ`, not `r θ`, and the restoring lever is `r cos θ`.** A rotating-arm coupling placed on a small-rotation law at a stroke comparable to its arm is understated: at 47° the exact tangent/secant is 1.549 against a claimed 1.000. Check the rotation angle before quoting a hinge flexure as "exactly linear".
- **A stroke is a COMPRESSION, so it is bounded by the layer's own resting height, and no coupling can move that.** `s = L₀ − h < L₀` identically, and §3 caps `L₀` at 10 nm — so its *"~10 nm desired"* clause asks a 10 nm layer for `h = 0`. The delivered stroke is monotone *decreasing* in the coupling stiffness, so the **free** stroke is the supremum over every coupling anybody could design. Check the coordinate before searching the catalogue: five claims searched it first.
- **A ceiling written as a multiple of a mandate inherits the mandate's stroke.** `C-0023`'s 40 pN/nm is `1.2 × (100 pN / 3 nm)`; the same construction at the *desired* clause is **12**, not 40. Reading it at a larger stroke is not the conservative choice, it is the wrong clause's number — and 3.33× too generous.
- **The acceptance stack has no ceiling on a coupling TANGENT at all**: `C-0017` is an equality on the secant and a floor on the tangent, and `C-0018`'s fold gets *better* as the tangent stiffens. The one genuine ceiling is the per-path allowable, which is a bound on a **force**, so as a stiffness it is `n·allowable/s` and **tightens as `1/s`** where a declared tolerance stays put — 150 → 45 pN/nm at 45 paths, 50 → 15 at 15.
- **Every requirement on a coupling law is owed over the strokes the device TRAVERSES, `[0, s*]` — the ceiling and the floor are one question.** A device placed at 3 nm never occupies the 4.556 nm stroke `CH-0042`'s tangent minimum is taken at; read at the placement stroke the same element clears **4 of 6** model stability floors where the prescribed-range minimum clears **none**.
- **A monotonicity argument does not survive the coupling.** `C-0001` could bisect on an interval because the layer's pressure is strictly decreasing in `h`; the coupled characteristic is not monotone, so scan for the first sign change and bisect on *that* bracket.
- **A nonlinear load line moves a fold in its STROKE far more than in its bias, and the two are different tests.** Substituting `C-0030`'s softening coupling for the affine mandate at 10 nm / 2 mM drops the pull-in bias by 0.7–1.8 % — a rounding error — while the fold's own stroke walks back 0.6–1.0 nm, **through §3's 3 nm target**. Placement makes the operating bias **bit-identical** across load lines (`0.0` at 144 of 144), so every margin difference between couplings lives in the ceiling; read it on the stroke as well as the bias.
- **A tangent minimum taken over a range that includes ZERO stroke measures the interval, not the element.** At `s = 0` the reaction, the layer load and the bias are all zero, so the stability requirement is identically zero, and a strain-stiffening flexure's membrane term has not switched on — its `[0, 10]` minimum is its bending stiffness alone (23.5 pN/nm for an element whose tangent over the used range is 44.8). **An extremum silently imports its interval's endpoints**; minimise `k_tangent − |k_eff|`, whose zero *is* the fold, or state the range. Seventh instance of a quantity not well posed without the state it is read at.

### Anchoring and lateral confinement

- **An anchor's orientation decides everything and its material almost nothing.** For any flexible link crossing the layer, `k_lat/k_norm` is the *secant over the tangent* of its force-extension law, which convexity bounds at **1** — so a through-layer path costs at least as much normal stiffness as it buys laterally. A rigid rod is not covered by that bound and does 40–160× worse (`cEI/(SL²)` = 0.006–0.025), while the same duplex laid *in* the surface has the ratio the other way up by four orders of magnitude.
- **A strut standing under the tile is destabilised by the load it exists to resist** — it carries the actuation force in compression, buckles at 5.7–22.7 pN against a 25–100 pN duty, and a column at its Euler load has *exactly zero* lateral stiffness: `k(P) = k₀(1 − P/P_c)`.
- **An ssDNA tether is not "nothing at zero tension".** The geometry stretches it to the layer height whether or not it is taut, and there `F/L` *equals* the chain's own entropic constant — 0.124 pN/nm at a 10 nm gap, 27 % of the whole `A1.1` bound from one tether. The design rule is a contour **ceiling**, `L_c b ≤ 3N k_BT/k_req` (81 nt for four tethers), not "short and stiff".
- **The ssDNA Kuhn length is a 2× method-systematic bracket, not a number** — 1.34–1.41 nm from 10–40 pN force spectroscopy against 2.10–2.84 nm from zero-force scattering. A ~1 pN tether needs the zero-force end. The contour per nucleotide travels with the elastic model too (0.57 nm/nt extensible, 0.65–0.70 inextensible); mixing them double-counts the extension.
- **Yaw and translation are the same condition for anchors on the budget radius, exactly** — margins stand as `(r_anchor/r_budget)²`, so anchors *inside* the point the budget is written at make yaw binding, and one central anchor leaves yaw entirely free. Rotating in-plane tethers from radial to tangential is worth 638–2551× in yaw at no cost.
- **Over-stiffening an anchor is not free**: the per-anchor thermal force is `√(k_BT k)/N`, so a 120×-margin scheme puts 29 pN on a load path where the minimum design puts 2.6 pN.
- **A surface-parallel tether converts stroke into tension geometrically** — it must stretch `δ²/2L`, so `L_min = δ√(Sn/2A)`, linear in the stroke: 28 nm for a 3 nm stroke, 93 nm for 10 nm. Lateral confinement and the desired stroke are incompatible at a fixed **footprint**, not in physics.

### Zero bias and dispersion forces

- **A stable equilibrium is not a confinement.** A hold-down whose force falls faster than `1/h` has a *convergent* potential and therefore a **finite** well — van der Waals here is 0.2–5.7 `k_BT` — so the tile escapes it and every moment of the distribution is a property of the integration domain, not of the physics. Quote the escape barrier beside any zero-bias variance; only a hold-down whose force *rises* as the tile lifts (an entropic tether) confines.
- **Above `L₀` the confining potential is LINEAR, so the requirement is a force, not a stiffness.** `⟨h − L₀⟩ = k_BT/F` exactly, giving `F ≥ k_BT/3 nm = 1.381 pN` — the same statement as `k ≥ k_BT/σ² = 0.4602 pN/nm`, one power of the bound away. Equipartition understates the zero-bias amplitude by up to 2.6×, because the potential is harmonic below the rest height and linear above it.
- **A taut flexible link pulls its two ends together, so the ground point fixes the sign before any arithmetic.** Grounded on the substrate it holds the tile down; grounded on a lever above it holds the tile up; only a **two-sided** element (one that carries compression) can be preloaded either way. An ssDNA spacer is not one — which is why the compliance that closed `T-16` is exactly what opened `T-13`.
- **`1 zJ = 10⁻²¹ J = 1 pN·nm` exactly**, so a whole van der Waals calculation runs in this project's units with one conversion function and no stray factors.
- **The Hamaker constant of DNA quoted as `10⁻²⁰ J` is an author's explicit overestimate reused as a measurement**, and the `2 × 10⁻²⁰ J` in the AFM literature is a *protein* value substituted knowingly. The only Lifshitz computation across water gives **4.33–5.90 zJ**, and **no planar `A_DNA` exists** — every published value is cylinder-cylinder.
- **Two Hamaker combining relations are in circulation and they are not interchangeable.** The vacuum form `(√A₁₁ − √A₃₃)(√A₂₂ − √A₃₃)` reconstructs a medium interaction from vacuum constants; the across-water form `√(A₁w₁·A₂w₂)` mixes constants already across water, is validated to 2 %, and always *overestimates*. Use the second for numbers; the first is useful only as a **sign** diagnostic, being the only one that can go negative.
- **The zero-frequency Hamaker term is `(3/4)ζ(3)k_BT = 3.734 zJ`; the familiar `(3/4)k_BT` is the `s = 1` truncation and is 20 % low.** Its *share* is not what a symmetric constant suggests either: 1.5 % of `A_Au|w|Au` but **10–25 %** of a DNA-electrode cross constant, because the small half of a geometric mean dominates the ratio.
- **§3 does not say what the electrode is made of, and that is worth 2.6×** in the one hold-down that cannot be designed away — more than the DNA constant (1.17×), retardation, or the polymer in the gap. And zero *applied* bias is not zero *charge*: a contact potential of 0.9–5.1 mV supplies the whole thermal-scale hold-down. Some uncertainties are specification gaps, not modelling ones, and a better calculation of the wrong material is not an improvement.
- **A stroke quoted on `s = L₀ − h` is measured from a height the tile never occupies.** The zero-bias rest sits `d` below `L₀`, and `d` depends entirely on what resists the descent: 0.05–2.36 nm against the layer alone, but only 0.07–0.38 nm against the layer *and* a 33 pN/nm coupling.

### Joints and end conditions

- **A body attached at ONE point and otherwise free adds EXACTLY ZERO stiffness there** — its rigid-body motions span everything a single-point attachment can impose, so the Schur complement vanishes term by term. Same class as "a uniform load on a uniform foundation dishes exactly zero", and it is why 34 duplexes covering 46 % of the Gen-1 tile's plan change nothing in the grillage. **Attaching a body is not the same as stiffening a structure, and the tie COUNT is the whole question.**
- **Two ties on a straight host still add no bending, at any member rigidity, because two points determine a line.** A lever tied at two crossovers adds a **torsion bar** and nothing else (two hinges in series with its own `GJ/L`); its own `EI` is engaged only at **three** ties. A "one tie versus tied" bracket has three steps, not two, and the middle one is nearly empty.
- **An off-diagonal is not a compliance, it is a kinematic SUPPLY, and a bound on it says nothing about the sign of what it does.** A cantilever standoff's `C12` makes the beam's own end moment tilt the head *inward*, so the joint **supplies** draw-in — `Φδ` per end, **first order in the stroke where the arc-length demand is second order**, and 3.09× larger than it at the Gen-1 design. Two claims bounded it and both argued it "softens the joint"; against a *net* demand the coupled joint is **2.06× stiffer**, and the effect that moves the answer is not a compliance at all.
- **A first-order supply against a second-order demand cannot have a single sign.** `Φδ` is odd where `e(δ)` is even, so a coupled flexure's law is **signed but not odd** — 1.9–3.9× between its two limbs — and the sign of every consequence is set by **which body carries the standoffs**. That is worth a whole design window, and no upstream claim asks the question.
- **A sagging beam pulls its supports together, exactly as a cable does, so the standoff length is also a CLEARANCE.** The mounting sense that supplies the draw-in is the one in which the midspan descends toward its own supports' ground: travel is `ℓ − d`, 5.31 nm on an 8 nm standoff. The two mountings trade a compliance window against a geometric stroke ceiling.
- **`c` carries the stroke as well as the span.** Under a coupled joint the bending *coefficient* is unchanged to the last digit, but the reaction acquires a term proportional to the axial force, so the *effective* end condition runs 139 → 124 → 92 over `δ = 0 → 3 → 10 nm`. The sixth instance here of a quantity that is not well posed without the state it is read at.
- **`t/s < 1` is a stability DEBT, not the absence of a bonus, and the tangent can have an interior minimum inside the operating range.** Once a coupling strain-softens, stability must be read on `min_s k_tangent(s)` — 22.88 pN/nm at a 4.55 nm stroke here — and not on the tangent at the working point.
- **A "free design choice" in an assembly of two bodies is usually a kinematic identity waiting to be written down.** A midspan-loaded flexure's deflection **is** the change in its two bodies' separation, so `dδ/ds = (v_base − v_driven)/n` — exactly `±1`, containing no length, and a **product of two binaries**: which body carries the standoffs *and* which way they point out of it. Naming one of the two predicts nothing (each body appears on both sides of the split). Before filing a sign as a specification gap, differentiate the chain.
- **A standoff in TENSION does not buckle, and half of any mounting sweep puts it there.** The flexure's end shear acts along the standoff's own axis, so compression ⟺ the midspan sags toward the base ⟺ the favourable draw-in supply. Charging an Euler load against `|end shear|` prices a stability failure that does not exist in that configuration — and hides that the *favourable* mounting is the one that has to buckle, because the compression **is** the supply.
- **§3's effort point is a constraint on the coupling's own height budget, not just a consistency check on the stack.** Its `~20–25 nm` band is exactly as wide as its 5–10 nm layer-height range, which forces a **constant** attachment height and fixes it at **5 nm**. A coupling stacked *between* the two bodies cannot put its effort point closer to the tile than its own standoff length, so 5 nm is a ceiling on that length at the 10 nm layer — where the whole design window lives. The number sat unused in `ActuatorGeometry` for two iterations.
- **A joint's stiffness is never its own member's stiffness — it is that member in series with its GROUND.** A standoff quoted as `EI/ℓ`, `3EI/ℓ³`, `S/ℓ` retains only **32.0 / 13.6 / 32.0 %** of those on a crossover base, and its Euler load 16.4 %. Quote every joint constant with the ground it assumes — the fifth instance of this discipline, and the first where the omitted qualifier turned a reserve into the binding constraint.
- **A pinned base under a sway column is a MECHANISM, not a weaker strut: `P_c = 0` exactly.** The two-spring eigenvalue `sin u(u² − ρ_bρ_h) − cos u(ρ_b+ρ_h)u = 0` has all four textbook `K` factors as corners, and the `ρ_b = ρ_h = 0` corner is in no "factor of 4" bracket. A clamped-base `K = 2` is not the conservative reading of a column whose base is a crossover.
- **A nicked continuation cannot turn a corner.** "A single nick is a clamp" and "a double nick is a crossover" are both statements about a duplex continuing along its **own axis**; a nick preserves the helix axis, so the stiffest joint in the catalogue is structurally unavailable as a 90° base.
- **A misalignment FLOOR is a minimum over the placement coordinate, and an ARRAY pins that coordinate.** `C-0059`'s 6.0° base floor is the best a 90° junction pair reaches *anywhere* in a 32 bp sweep; a truss in an array stands at a station the upward lattice fixes, and there the same feasible set delivers **18.0°** at a 9 bp row and **57.0°** at the 10 bp row `C-0062` recommends — past the 45° at which the base cannot be represented at all. The sheet's own crossover phase cannot repair it, because the phase moves the station **and** the host's backbone together. The seventh instance of *"a quantity is not well posed without the state it is read at"*, and the first where the state is a **lattice coordinate** rather than a load.
- **An argmin selected on a coordinate orthogonal to the answer manufactures its own sensitivity.** `C-0057` found the single junction's strain swinging **7×** across a 0.01 nm change in the phosphate radius, which reads as a physical sensitivity and is not one — the radius was moving *which placement the distance search returns*. Re-ranking the same feasible set on the quantity actually wanted makes the verdict stable at 1.00, 0.90 **and** 0.8901 nm. **A large sensitivity to a convention is evidence about the objective before it is evidence about the physics.**
- **The 33.74°/bp azimuthal quantum belongs to the SHEET's phosphate lattice, not to a free-standing standoff's base chord.** A standoff is a *separate* duplex, so nothing fixes its helical phase and its rotation about its own axis is **continuous** — the chord can be laid exactly on any axis the closure window admits, and the "worst phase costs 8.4 % of the couple" allowance is not a debt (`CH-0056`). The quantum still binds *where a link may land*; it never binds *which way the chord points*. It would attach to the chord only for a phase-**inherited** standoff, i.e. a nicked continuation, and that motif cannot turn a corner.
- **The chord's azimuth is continuous and the FEASIBLE subset of it is not, so "the alignment is free" holds at ONE junction and fails at two.** `CH-0056` is about the chord; **torsion feasibility is a relation between two bodies**, so it depends on the standoff's azimuth *relative to the seat's phosphate lattice*. A single junction can therefore be simultaneously torsion-closing and *exactly* aligned (chord 90.0°, `cos²ψ = 1.0000`), while a pair — which must find two closing placements at a **fixed separation**, and only 18 of 90 axial positions carry one at all — inherits whatever chords those positions offer: 6°–69° depending on the separation, with `C-0042`'s recommended 7 bp the **worst** of its own band. Never carry a one-junction alignment result to a multi-junction assembly.
- **A misalignment past 45° is not a degraded design point, it is outside the model.** `C-0037`'s `TwoLinkBase` carries a *restrained* axis and a *free* one; beyond a half right angle the two have **exchanged**, so a search that can return `ψ > π/4` needs a guard rather than a number. A 69° base is a `NOT REPRESENTABLE`, not a 12.8 % couple.
- **But a body with TWO junctions has a quantised RELATIVE azimuth, and that is a different quantity on the same body.** A truss leg carries a junction at each end, so its two terminal chords differ by `m × 33.74°` folded modulo `π` — the design chooses a **length**, not two azimuths, and rotating the body trades one misalignment for the other **one for one**, so only the *difference* is quantised. `CH-0056` is completed rather than contradicted: one junction inherits no phase, two share one body. A 21 bp leg (`C-0048`'s recommended 7.00 nm) is 78.5° from the azimuth pair that design asks for, and it is on the wrong side at 10.67, 10.5 **and** 10.4 bp/turn.
- **A seat's rim has TWO directions once the seat is short.** `seatContactLength` excludes a *lateral* rim seat; a 13 bp crossbar has an **axial** rim too, and `ceil((w + 2R)/rise)` leaves exactly **0.02 nm** of overhang per end at **every** row width — `2R = 2.00 nm` is 5.88 rises and the ceiling buys 6. And `ceil` returns a **length**, not the demand: "13 bp = 4.38 nm" conflates them, 13 bp being 4.42.
- **A capsule is not a duplex with a flat end.** Approximating two half-infinite cylinders by their axis segments plus a radius rounds each flat end face into a hemisphere and reports a **clash** (−0.15 nm) exactly where the real bodies clear (+0.25 nm). Both are convex — a cylinder intersected with a half-space — so **alternating projection** gives the exact closest pair in a dozen lines, each projection being componentwise because the cylinder and the half-space constrain orthogonal coordinates.
- **A seat HEIGHT is not a seat: a flat duplex end resting off a cylinder's top line has a line contact `2√(R_s² − y_c²)` that collapses to a POINT at the rim**, independently of the cylinder's own radius. An unbounded closure search parks its optimum exactly there — a standoff balanced on an edge, legal by face height and a mechanism in fact. Bound the lateral seat, and quote the contact length beside it.
- **Aligning a two-terminus chord can cost the phosphodiester WINDOW even when it costs no couple.** The aligned pair's binding link sits at **0.697 nm**, the C2′-endo end of the measured `[0.60, 0.70]` step, where the residual-minimising single junction sits at **0.600**, the C3′-endo end — and pricing the centring at 5.7° of alignment per 0.05 nm does not move it. A downstream torsion check must be run at the pucker the *alignment* demands, not at the one the *closure search* happened to report.
- **Two layouts identical in one plane need not be identical in the other, and a buckling verdict reads the MINIMUM.** A cross row at 6 and at 8 bp is bit-identical in the loaded plane — same span, tangent, coupling factor, supply ratio and critical load — and 1.32× apart in the free one, which is the plane the truss exists to restrain. "The spacing is free" is a statement about one plane; check both before adopting it.
- **A buckling duty must come from the element's own law, never from the mandate secant.** A strain-stiffening coupling placed at 3 nm delivers 1.27–1.70× more at 10 nm than `k·s`, and the two coincide *exactly* at the placement point — so the error is invisible where it is checked. Better still, quote the **stroke at which the member buckles**, which needs no margin convention.
- **A multi-crossover base reacts a moment as a COUPLE, and a couple has an axis.** Two crossovers to adjacent duplexes give 261.2 pN·nm/rad laid *across* the beam and 27.06 laid *along* it — 9.65×, same staples, same axial support, and the difference between a design and a mechanism.
- **A beam-end condition is a CONTINUUM and its two textbook values are limits: `c(ρ) = 192(ρ+2)/(ρ+8)`, `ρ = k_θL/EI`, exactly 48 at 0 and 192 at ∞, 96 at `ρ = 4`.** `ρ` carries the **span**, so the same joint is nearer a pin on a short beam and nearer a clamp on a long one — `c` is not a constant of the joint. Every origami joint that can *support* a beam lands at `c = 83–192`; the near-pinned ones are the ones that cannot.
- **A joint has THREE stiffnesses and a dead band, not two.** A beam end transmits shear (both directions, if the coupling is two-sided), axial force and moment. Sizing a joint on the axial one alone is how `C-0023`'s two-nucleotide hinge got proposed and `CH-0031` had to remove it.
- **An isotropic element cannot be stiff across and soft along — `k_⊥/k_axial ≡ 1` for any flexible link, and for any covalent tie on a softened bond.** So a single strand pays its whole axial slack back as a transverse **dead band** of the same size (1.30 nm at 2 nt, 43 % of the §3 stroke). The escape is the one that escaped the axial trade-off one level up: **bending has a direction**. A duplex standing *normal* to the sheet is `S/ℓ` across and `3EI/ℓ³` along — anisotropy `Sℓ²/(3EI)`, 102× at 8 nm, set by a length.
- **A joint has TWO load paths on the same links, and quoting the counting theorem for one of them is not quoting it.** `C-0037` fixes a leg head's *axial* tie at `2 k_bond,s` "by `C-0029`'s counting theorem" and takes the same two links' **rotation** as infinite; it is at most `2k_bond,θ + 2k_bond,s r_P²` = 78.24 on one axis and 13.53 on the other. That is not a series correction to the couple — it is a **ceiling** on the head restraint any couple can deliver, so widening the frame stops helping (a free plane pinned at 6.20 pN at *every* row width to 16 bp).
- **Two legs cannot both be seated on a duplex laid across their row, ever** — the steric floor makes them `≥ 2R` apart and a flat end face contacts a seat only within `R` of its axis, so the two conditions are one length read twice and they exclude each other. Any "the flexure itself is the cap" geometry dies on that count, before a solve. A cap must run **along** the row, which makes it a **third duplex** with a length, a bending, a torsion and a **height of one radius**.
- **A rigid offset on a tip flexibility is a unit-determinant congruence `T C Tᵀ`, `T = [[1, e], [0, 1]]`**: `C22` is untouched, `C12` gains `e C22`, `C11` gains `2e C12 + e²C22`. So a cap's own radius **raises** the off-diagonal that supplies the draw-in and costs `−½ P e φ²` of geometric softening in the buckling problem. A series spring has no height; a body does.
- **A double nick IS a crossover, and a single nick is a clamp.** One intact backbone is not a softened bond; it carries the duplex's own `B/a` and `S/a`, so a nicked continuation is clamped *and* held. Cut both backbones at the same base pair and what is left is two softened bonds in parallel — a crossover, to the last digit.
- **The arc-length excess of a partially restrained beam is `g(β) δ²/L` with `g = (2.4 − 1.25β + β²/6)/(1 − β/4)²`, `β = 3ρ/(ρ+2)` — 2.4 at BOTH end conditions and exactly 9/4 at `ρ = 8`.** The endpoint coincidence is what makes the interior look uninteresting; 2.4 is a ceiling, not a constant.
- **A "bracket" needs its two errors to be OPPOSED, and two corrections to one baseline usually are not.** `C-0034`'s series and BVP readings looked like a bracket because one is shorter than the other, but both correct the *same* linear two-spring beam and both **stiffen** it — a restrained far end relieves the hinge, and the arc shortens the span — so their exact composition lands **outside** them, 12.72 nm against 11.03–12.50. Ask what common baseline two readings correct before calling their span a bracket.
- **Once a stroke is a large fraction of a member's own CONTOUR, no linear reading of it is a percent-level approximation.** `E5`'s arm is capped at 13.65 nm by its own placement condition, so §3's 10 nm stroke is ≥ 73 % of it everywhere; the exact `t/s` runs 1.002 → 1.093 → 3.778 over the stroke and the tangent at 10 nm is 264 pN/nm where the linear reading says 36.8. The same statement that closed the standoff branch, on a member that carries no axial load at all.
- **An inextensible member has no axially-HELD reading at all.** Holding two ends at their original axial separation while offsetting them transversally demands a chord longer than the contour, so `C-0023`'s free/held binary is not a design choice for a rod — the held branch is a *different constitutive assumption*, and its cost is a strain bound `ε ≥ √(1+(δ/L)²) − 1`, which for `E5` at the desired stroke is 299 pN, 4.6× the nicked ceiling. The **free** reading is the favourable one; check whether the unfavourable one even exists before pricing it.
- **A per-path allowable read at the *acceptable* stroke is not read at the *desired* one.** A coupling at §3's mandate delivers 333 pN at a 10 nm stroke, so the 10 pN unzip allowable alone needs ≥ 34 load paths — a fourth and tightest route to `C-0015`'s 45, and none of the first three noticed it.
- **Invert a length-dependent allowable, do not just evaluate it.** `CH-0029`'s ladder read backwards says what bonded length a load *demands*: 4 bp for a good design, 100 bp for a bad one, and **nothing at all above the 68.1 pN loading-rate-free saturation** — where "past the 65 pN ceiling" understates the failure.
- **A cantilever/guided end pair is its OWN continuum, and it is not `C-0025`'s: `c(ρ) = 12(1+ρ)/(4+ρ)`, exactly 3 at a free far end, 12 at a guided one, 6 at `ρ = 2`.** The two textbook values are again a factor of four apart, but the function is different because the end pair is — never reuse a `c(ρ)` across boundary-value problems. And the whole two-spring beam is `c(ρ_n, ρ_f) = 12(ρ_nρ_f + ρ_n + ρ_f)/(ρ_nρ_f + 4ρ_n + 4ρ_f + 12)`, whose `(0,0)` corner is **0** — a mechanism, not a weak beam.
- **Because `ρ = kL/EI` carries the LENGTH, a cap of the form `(c·n·EI/k)^(1/3)` is a FIXED POINT, not a formula** — a longer beam buys its own end restraint. Evaluating it at an asserted `c` is what `C-0029` did; solving `r = (c(k_far r/EI)·n·EI/k)^(1/3)` is what turns the question into a **link count**: two links at a duplex end clear §3's desired stroke on every axis, one link is exactly the cantilever and does not.
- **A guide RELIEVES the hinge, so a series composition and a guided end condition are not independent.** `1/k = r²/(nk_θ) + r³/(cEI)` charges the hinge the whole tip moment `Fr`, which is true only when the far end carries none — it reproduces the two-spring BVP **exactly at `ρ_f = 0` and nowhere else**. Changing `c` from 3 to 12 inside it is a category error worth **1.64× of over-placement** (`CH-0044`); the far end's own moment is `Fr ρ/(2(1+ρ))`, rising to `Fr/2`.
- **The same counting theorem can close one branch and leave another open, and the difference is the LOAD PATH.** Two links on a chord restrain one axis at a duplex end wherever it appears; that is fatal to a standoff (*a column buckles about its softest axis*) and harmless to a transverse-loaded arm, which carries no compression. Ask what the free axis carries before transferring a verdict.
- **A chord at a duplex end is a diameter of that duplex's own cross-section, so its azimuth is a DESIGN VARIABLE** — quantised at 33.74°/bp on the square lattice, worst misalignment half a quantum, `cos²` cost ≤ 8.4 %. Where the joint is at the end of the member being bent, the favourable orientation is always available for the price of choosing a base pair.
- **A conservation identity derived on a FREE coordinate becomes an INEQUALITY the moment that coordinate is pinned, and always in the costly direction.** `C-0052`'s `ψ_base + ψ_cap = |m τ − 90°|` holds for a rotation the designer *chooses*; a base pinned by the host's backbone need not take the reducing sign, so the true statement is `≥`, overspending by up to `2|δ|` — **1.80×** at the Gen-1 truss. Ask whether a conserved sum was derived under a minimisation before carrying it into a constrained design.
- **A two-legged body on one helix has TWO pinned azimuths, not one, and the DIFFERENCE is what a shared length cannot touch.** One leg length rotates both cap chords by the same `m τ`, so `fold(cap_A − cap_B) = fold(δ_A − δ_B)` at every length and the worse end is floored at half of it. A design table carrying *"the"* base misalignment of a pair (the worse of two) has projected away the only part of the geometry that is invariant under the free variable.
- **When an array's instances share one helical phase class, a per-instance design variable is a per-*part* variable and the array clause is free.** The 34 truss stations are one class, so the 68 leg bases are two classes of 34 and *"one leg length for 34 instances"* is *"one leg length for two legs"* — a counting argument that settles the question before any junction is solved. Check the class census before pricing a global-versus-per-instance trade.

### Coupling topology

- **DNA's compliance comes in exactly two kinds and they have opposite sidedness: entropic compliance only *pulls*, bending compliance is *signed*.** An element loaded along its own axis must choose — a duplex is `S/L` (220 pN/nm at 5 nm, two-sided, 297× too stiff) and a single strand carries no compression at all, with nothing in between. Loaded transverse to its axis, or through a hinge, the same duplex is both, because `c EI/L³` and `k_θ/r²` are as small as the designer makes `L` and `r`. Every coupling element in this programme before `C-0023` was loaded axially.
- **A confinement requirement is a force or a stiffness depending on the *topology* of what confines, and the two differ by exactly one power of the position bound.** `F_req = k_req·σ` identically: a one-sided hold-down gives a linear potential (`k_BT/σ = 1.38 pN`), a two-sided one a quadratic potential (`k_BT/σ² = 0.46 pN/nm`). The same 33.3 pN/nm coupling is 4 % short of the first and 72× past the second. Never quote a positional requirement without saying which potential it assumes.
- **A preload on a two-sided element is a mounting offset, i.e. a LENGTH, and DNA quantises it at 0.34 nm.** `F_down = F_t q/(δ_t − q)`, so the thermal-scale hold-down asks for 0.041 nm — an eighth of a base-pair rise — while the smallest offset a design can build delivers 12.8 pN, 9.3× too much. A preload that cannot be set is an argument for not needing one.
- **A clearance between two duplex END FACES is an AXIAL length, so it is a base-pair COUNT — and so is any margin built out of it.** `C-0069`'s knife-edge `p − d − L = 0.0256 nm` is not a small clearance, it is the **residue** of subtracting a *transverse* SAXS lattice constant (2.69 nm = **7.912** rises) and an elastica root (8.16439 nm = **24.013** rises) from an integer 32 bp pitch. Written on the lattice the identity is `M = (32 − N_d − N_L)` rises: the standing design closes at **exactly zero**, quantising the convention *up* to 8 rises makes it **negative** and drops the array 34 → 30, and a properly chosen clearance gives **2 whole rises**, which is quotable where 0.0256 nm was not. **Before calling a margin unquotably small, check that every term of it is a length the design language can draw.**
- **An anti-stacking remedy and a duplex-end JOINT compete for the same two strand termini.** All three of Rothemund's measured remedies — omit the terminal staple, add a 4-T hairpin, add a 4-T tail — append to or remove a **terminus**, and `C-0029`'s counting theorem says a duplex end has exactly **two**, both of which `C-0034`'s `A2` joint already spends. So at a jointed end the clearance is not a *backup* for the end chemistry, it is the **only** control; the escape is a staple break one base pair inboard, which is a nick (free by `C-0025`) and an 8 bp domain (a yield cost by Ke et al.).
- **A preload is a LENGTH and a stiffness is a POWER of a length, so the same 0.34 nm quantum is a trap for one and not for the other.** The preload above asked for 0.041 nm against a 0.34 nm quantum and lost 8.3×; a per-path stiffness of the same design is set by an 87 bp span, so one base pair is `3 × 0.34/29.6 = 3.2 %` of it — and across the whole catalogue 1.0–19.1 %, against a flat ratio window a factor of 5.7 wide. **Before declaring a quantised design variable unbuildable, ask what power of the quantum the requirement is.**
- **A mandate written on a SUM is settable `n` times more finely than any one path.** Rounding two stiffness *levels* independently misses `C-0017`'s equality by up to 5.44 % — a placement error, not a rounding nuisance — and moving individual paths by **one** base pair recovers it to `1.3e−4`. The price is 3–4 distinct staple lengths instead of two. **A two-level design rule is not two staple lengths.**
- **A transverse beam with axially restrained ends is a cable, not a spring, at 10 % deflection.** The membrane term is cubic and invisible in any linearised budget: it triples the tangent, doubles the required span, and takes the beam's own axial tension past the 65 pN nicked ceiling at a 10 nm stroke. Whether the ends can draw in — `2.4 δ²/L`, about 2.6 base pairs here — is the binding design choice, and it is a joint design, not a measurement.
- **The path count of a distributed coupling is set by the per-path allowable, not by the stiffness, and more paths make each element LONGER.** At 8 paths §3's 100 pN alone is 12.5 pN per path, past the 10 pN unzip allowable, before any concentration factor; and a flexure span goes as `n^(1/3)`.
- **A stability margin read at a HELD gap is not the same quantity as a fold margin on a MOVING equilibrium.** The held gap's `|k_eff|` rises as `V^1.9–2.8` here while `C-0018`'s fold implies `V^11–25` — most of a pull-in fold's steepness is kinematic. Quote which one you mean, and never report the two as a reproduction of each other.
- **A budget that enters a placement only as a PRODUCT makes the trade that spends it degenerate.** A rigid-armed array of `n` flexures on `h` crossovers each places at `θ tan θ = k_target δ²/(n h k_θ)`, so every split of a fixed crossover inventory places the *identical* arm — 5.387 nm at all eight splits of `n·h = 56`. **What breaks such a tie is whichever restraint is PER-ELEMENT rather than per-budget**: `C-0034`'s far anchorage is 5.78 crossovers' worth *per flexure*, so the arm grows with the path count and "fewer, longer" is the wrong direction. Ask which of a design's restraints scale with the count before assuming a two-variable sweep has two variables.
- **A per-path floor derived from a placement is a property of the CONVENTION, not of the material.** The 34-path floor three claims quote is `k_c δ/A = 33.333 × 10/10`; placed on §3's desired clause instead (`k_c = 100/10`) the same allowable gives **10**. A path count quoted without the placement it was read on is the sixth instance here of "quote a stiffness with its compression" — applied to the acceptance clause rather than to the model.
- **Placement and stability are the same inequality read on opposite sides, so together they cap the STROKE.** `k_c = F/δ` and `k_c > |k_eff|` give `δ ≤ F/|k_eff|` — **3.58–4.27 nm at §3's 100 pN and the 10 nm layer in 2 mM** — a bound on the stroke that names no coupling element at all. Check it before pricing any element against a desired stroke.

- **`C-0017`'s mandate is an equality on a SUM, and WHERE the sum's compliance lives is a topology choice worth an order of magnitude.** An array of independent paths is `Σᵢ series(tᵢ, gᵢ)` and spends the mandate at every station — 0.98 pN/nm each at 34 paths; a **shared body** is `series(Σᵢ tᵢ, g)` and spends it once, in a **rigid-body mode of the tile**, which the dishing projector annihilates by construction. The ties are then capped by the per-path *force* instead (3.33 pN/nm at 10 pN and 3 nm): **3.4× more local support at 34 paths and 9.0× at 90**, and the ratio *grows* with the path count where the array's shrinks. It is a division, not a mechanics.
- **A shared body is an ADDITIVE COMPLIANCE in series with the ties and nothing else** — `(T⁻¹ + M + F_b) f = w_free`, `F_b = Φ H⁻¹ Φᵀ` — so the array is its `F_b = 0` corner, a whole change of topology is **one term in an existing Woodbury bank**, and every number the array published reproduces bit for bit. Condensed, the coupling the tile feels is `K_c = T − TΦ(ΦᵀTΦ + H)⁻¹ΦᵀT`: symmetric, positive **semi**-definite, and **not diagonal**, which is the whole of the difference.
- **A FREE rigid body tied at `n` points contributes `T − TA(AᵀTA)⁻¹AᵀT`, of rank `n − 3`** — one formula for three separately recorded facts (*one tie adds exactly zero*, *two points determine a line*, *the arm's own `EI` is engaged only at three ties*), and it also says what the fourth tie buys. It carries **no net force and no net moment**, so it supports only the *non-affine* part of the station displacement, which is exactly what dishing measures — and under a **uniform** load it therefore applies exactly zero tie force, a falsifier as sharp as *"a uniform load on a uniform foundation dishes exactly zero"*.
- **A plan-length bound `(c EI/k)^(1/3)` inherits the PATH COUNT the mandate was divided by.** `CH-0081`'s *"22.414 nm, a bound on the family"* is that expression at `k = 33.3333/34`; the same family supplying the same mandate from one to six ground elements is **4.36–7.92 nm**, inside the same 8.19 nm budget, because the span goes as `n^(1/3)` and `34^(1/3) = 3.24`. A bound quoted on a family must carry the count.
- **The standoff's sway and the flexure's draw-in are the SAME coordinate.** So a bending standoff cannot be made to release the draw-in without being made to buckle in it, and the "held-head" buckling reading is not available to any design that needs the release. Two requirements in direct opposition on one degree of freedom.
- **But that identity is about ONE coordinate, and a two-link base fails on the ORTHOGONAL one.** A frame couple `k_a Σd_i²` is a **rank-one tensor on the leg offsets**, so `Σx² + Σy² = w²/2` is a conserved budget the azimuth spends, and a two-leg row laid across the loaded plane adds **exactly zero** to it — 6.71× in critical load for 23 % of a draw-in supply that had 3.75× of margin. The cost is then the leg **count** (two flexibilities in parallel), not the leg **spacing**: cross rows at 2.04, 2.72 and 4.08 nm are bit-identical in the loaded plane. **Ask which axis a failure is on before pricing a remedy against an identity.**
- **An array's plan view is decided by its VERTICAL members, not by its beams, and their clash is LEVEL-INDEPENDENT.** A standoff runs from its ground up to its own beam plane and a tie runs from that plane down to the driven body, so any two vertical members of a multi-level array share a height range whatever levels their beams sit at. Stacking therefore cannot separate them: a 2.59× area shortfall against three available beam planes is still unrealisable. **The array does not fail to pack, it fails to stand up** — check the legs before pricing the levels.
- **Two collinear beams need `span + d`, not `span`** — their standoff feet sit on the beam **ends**, so beams laid end to end put two standoffs in the same place. The extra duplex is invisible to a body-versus-body overlap test and to every area budget.
- **The attachment grid's across-helix pitch IS one duplex, so a beam array on it is tangent at zero tilt and interferes at any other** (`2.69 cos θ < 2.69`), and its along-helix pitch is *under* the span — two lattice facts that meet nowhere. The 45-path flexure array is unrealisable at **0 of 720 orientations, at any level count and on any body**; the tile carries **15**. And the one layout that works is feasible on a set of **measure zero** in the angle — which is a design rather than a defect only because the sheet's own helix direction supplies it exactly. **A lattice can hold a tolerance of zero.**
- **A row of tie holes on the attachment grid is not `n` holes, it is a SLOT, and it severs the sheet.** The grid's row pitch is exactly the duplex pitch, so a column of ties removes a whole line of material: a 3 × 15 grid cuts every duplex into four pieces and leaves **18 disconnected components** at every one of the 32 crossover phases. **Quoting a perforation as an area answers the wrong question** — solve the connectivity. The cure is 8 bp of stagger, and it is free because "one attachment row per duplex" fixes the rows and says nothing about the positions along them.
- **An off-square member row is eccentrically loaded by the head moment**, which reacts partly as an axial **couple**, so the outermost member carries more than its share: the per-member check fails at 12 of 12 azimuths below 90° even where the *total* margin is ample. The mean is never the design number — `C-0020`'s "equilibrium bounds the sum on a cut, never the per-member peak", one level up.
- **A bending element's plan length is `(c EI/k)^(1/3)` and `c` is set by its TOPOLOGY, not by the designer** — 48–192 supported twice and loaded at midspan, at most 12 supported once and loaded at the far end. The two families are **2.74×** apart before any lattice is consulted, so a *packing* negative on a midspan-loaded flexure is an **end-condition** statement wearing a plan view. Check `c` before running a packer.
- **The only out-of-plane compliance DNA has is AXIAL.** A member standing normal to the sheet and loaded normal to it is loaded along its own axis — `S/k`, **1122 nm** at the Gen-1 per-path stiffness, 112× the whole envelope — and to bend it instead the load must be transverse, which puts it back in the plan. **There is no "out of the plane" escape from a plan-packing problem**, and looking for one costs an iteration.
- **A fold trades the slack a lattice has for the slack it does not**: it halves the along-row demand and doubles the across-row one, and on a single-layer sheet the across-row pitch **is** one duplex. Folding therefore *shrinks* the plan budget rather than enlarging it — 28 of 34 at the contour where the straight element places 34.
- **An element can be short enough to place and too short to work.** A compliance window has a **floor** as well as a ceiling: below `1.5 ×` the stroke a lever turns past 42° and its chord draw-in is a large fraction of its own length, so the exact elastica refuses it outright. A solver refusal there is a **result**, not an error — catch it and report the window.
- **A mandate written on a SUM needs `placed/n` as a COLUMN of any sensitivity table**, because the load is carried by what is *built*: a row that places 18 of 34 delivers `18/34 = 0.529×` the mandate, so a plan failure **is** a stiffness failure of exactly the same size. Three of the eight rows of `C-0069`'s Deliverable 5 fail it and only one of the three is a path-count row.
- **A cap that saturates the count is an IDENTITY, not a constraint.** Thirty roots on fifteen duplexes at two per row is `2 × 15 = 30`, so every row carries two, the search family is a product of per-row 2-subsets, and an exhaustive centro-symmetric enumeration falls from 361 584 candidates to **34 992**. Check whether a "constraint" has any freedom left in it before writing a search.

### Design-window synthesis

- **A flatness, a stiffness distribution and a load-path count are all quoted on a STATION SET, and a station set is a placement claim's output, not a convention.** The Gen-1 flat design (0.075 of the stroke) lives on `C-0015`'s 3 × 15 *grid*; on the only station sets a placement claim supplies — 15 or 34 — the same family reaches 0.605 and 0.165. **The sixth "quote it with the state it was read at" in this project, and the state is a set of coordinates.**
- **Check a placement for CENTRO-SYMMETRY before optimising anything on it.** A greedy per-row scheduler fills from one end and points every member the same way; `C-0055`'s 34 arm roots have their centroid 8.8 nm off a 40 nm tile's centre, and reflecting alternate rows — free, same lattice — bought more flatness than a whole non-uniform-stiffness rule did.
- **Not every constraint resolves in the axis the window is drawn on.** Three of the five axes this programme discovered — flatness count, usable bias, output-coupling stiffness — are height-level or topological, not functions of grafting density. They cannot *narrow* a window, only *close a height*, and a constraint that cannot narrow is **invisible to an intersection**. Check which axis each constraint lives on before intersecting.
- **A window width is a ratio, and a wide window is not a tolerance.** The 10 nm window is 22× wide in `σ`, and its two ends differ by 4.7× in grafting spacing and 2× in stroke — they are different devices. Report the width as a ratio, and report what changes across it.
- **A window gains an axis when a constraint is discovered and loses one when a constraint is DISCHARGED, and an intersection records neither.** The lateral-confinement footprint axis left the window entirely when the in-plane factor became 1 and the in-plane tethers left the design. Check for *removals* as well as additions before re-intersecting.
- **A claim's consequences are not confined to the task it was written for.** `C-0023` was filed against `T-13` and is worth **four grid steps** of the 10 nm design window, because removing the substrate tethers removes 92 % of the hold-down whose preload was being charged against the stroke.
- **A correction can be quoted against a stack that has already left the design.** Check *which configuration* a challenge's bracket was evaluated on, in the same way a stiffness is checked against its compression.
- **The upper edge of this window is a DEAD-LOAD stroke, so no electrostatic correction can move it.** Ask which clause owns an edge before propagating anything to it; it settles most candidate movers for free.
- **A negative result is strongest when the failing element is the one that PASSES.** The cleanest row in `T-108`'s catalogue clears every coupling-side predicate at the desired stroke and fails only on the reach — which is what proves the binding constraint is not the coupling. A table of failures cannot make that argument; one pass can.
- **An upstream bracket upheld at one design point is not upheld at all of them.** `CH-0010` upheld `C-0003`'s *response* numbers at 10 nm; at 5 nm the solved layer falls 1.22× outside the same bracket. When a synthesis transfers a claim's verdicts, re-check the licence **at every shared point**, not at the one the challenge happened to examine.
- **A mandate written on a SUM is not a mandate on each term, and reading it as one cost this programme its only flat design.** `C-0017`'s 33.3333 pN/nm is a placement equality on the *whole* coupling; five claims shared it equally between the paths without ever saying so. Freeing the distribution takes 45 attachments to 0.075 of the stroke where 225 equal ones saturate at 0.149. **A saturation measured inside one family is not a floor of the object** — before calling a limit irreducible, name the family it was swept in.
- **A flatness verdict needs an OPERATING STATE as well as a load case.** A distribution tuned at one of `C-0022`'s states is 2.6× *worse than uniform* at another, and the uniform coupling is already flat at the two compressed states while failing at all three 10 nm ones. The sixth instance here of a quantity that is not well posed without the state it is read at.
- **A window that does not move is evidence of nothing until you count how many of the new constraints are functions of its own axis.** Twenty claims and twenty challenges moved zero of six edges, and the reason is that **exactly one of the twenty is `σ`-resolved at all** — the rest are counts, plan layouts, elastica geometries, height-level actuator states and specification questions. Report the *census* beside the null result; without it a stationary window reads as a defended one.
- **A ceiling on a volume fraction must be quoted with the LAYER MODEL it was read on**, the sixth instance of this discipline here. At the 10 nm window's upper edge `C-0003`'s trial-function models sit at `φ = 0.151–0.285` and `C-0011`'s solved layer at **0.0686** — 4.15×, and they disagree about whether a `φ ≤ 0.2` ceiling *exists*. `φ = Nσv₀/h` identically and `T-1d` emits it, so checking costs one multiplication.
- **At a fold the composition of two corrections is EXACT, not first order**, because the baseline coupled tangent vanishes there by construction (`k_c + k_brush + k_es = 0` is what located it). So every perturbation enters as an increment and the *sign of the sum* gives the direction the fold moves for the price of one evaluation of each law — against a re-solve of the whole equilibrium path. Use it before paying for the relocated fold; it is the cheap bound that a two-channel disagreement actually needs.
- **A discharge is invisible to whoever files the removal.** Two of the six specification questions this programme carries to NDI (`T-95`, `T-102`) stopped applying the moment `CH-0081`/`C-0069` removed the flexure-and-tie branch from the output role, and neither claim noticed — because a claim that removes a branch is not looking at the questions that branch raised. **When a branch is killed, sweep its open questions**, not only the window's axes.
- **And it is invisible to the QUEUE as well as to the deliverable — the third instance, and the register was the last place to learn.** `C-0071` discharged `T-95` and `T-102` in iteration 14; `TASKS.md` carried both as `TODO`, in their rows *and* in *Open questions for Kazik*, three iterations later. **Strike a discharged item, never delete it** — keep the analysis and mark it, because a list that only ever grows is not a record and a list that silently shrinks is worse.
- **A status vocabulary GROWS, and every word your checker does not know is silently read as OPEN.** `SESSION-PROMPT.md` declares `DONE` and `KILLED`; iteration 14 coined `ANSWERED` and iteration 17 `DISCHARGED` — the latter for a status that is neither *answered* nor *abandoned* but **stopped applying**, which is a distinction this project needed and had no word for. The failure direction is the costly one: an unknown word reads OPEN, so a *closed* task keeps reading open, which is precisely the drift the checker exists to catch. Add the word with a test the day the queue coins it.
- **Count the INDEPENDENT margins, not the rows.** `C-0071`'s recommendation has four `NONE` bands in three different units owned by three different claims, and they are **one arithmetic** — `pitch − d − L = 0.0256 nm`; the two joint ceilings are the two coordinates of the same `c ≤ 2.3417` inequality. A ledger that lists them separately overstates the diversification of the exposure and hands a downstream tolerance task two targets where there is one.
- **A two-factor move has a TOTAL and an INTERACTION, never "an X term and a Y term".** Splitting a move across a 2 × 2 by measuring one factor and *subtracting* gives one of two orderings; the totals agree identically and the splits need not. On the count/phase move this programme recommends, count-first reads +12.86 %/−19.27 % and phase-first −11.48 %/+2.93 %, and on a search-free family the phase term **changes sign** between the orderings — while over the whole 32-phase grid the interaction carries **more** of the variation (9.79 %) than the phase main effect does (7.84 %). **The interaction costs four graded cells and the identity is exact, so measure it before quoting a split**; and quote the total, because that is what a recommendation between two endpoints actually rests on.
- **A count effect measured at one lattice phase is not a count effect.** The same reduction is adverse at 27 of 32 crossover phases and **favourable at 5**, spanning −4.6 to +12.2 %, where the anchor rule at one phase is worth 2.0 points of that 16.8-point spread. Sweep the phase before generalising a count, a distribution or a placement result off it.
- **A sensitivity whose alternative value is already in print is not a sensitivity, it is an UNRESOLVED READING.** Two of the five results that would remove `C-0071`'s recommendation are the square lattice's 2.73 nm and `α = 1.2` — both inside brackets their own sources publish. Classify a failure route by whether its reversal is hypothetical before quoting how many routes there are.
- **A MAXIMUM over a family read at one member of it is not a maximum, and the cheap form is usually a bisection.** A placement's plan ceiling is a `min` over *independent* rows, so `max over placements` is `sup{L : Σ_r cap_r(L) ≥ n}` — exact, closed, and needing no solve. `C-0072` reads it at its own reduction rule and understates the escape it discovered by **1.31×** at 30 roots, **3.62×** at 22 and **1.78×** at 15. The eighth *quote it with the state it is read at*, and the state is again a **placement**.
- **The equal-spring advantage belongs to a COUNT as well as to a layer.** Dropping `C-0063`'s 34 upward roots to 30 loses it outright — **0 of 32 phases** clear `T-5b`'s 0.10 with equal springs, best 0.1667 against 0.0789 — while a **2.06** peak-ratio distribution at the unchanged total recovers it (0.0682). What a sparser coupling spends is not the flatness, it is the **uniformity**; `CH-0080` is the same statement read on a layer height.
- **Selecting a placement on the EQUAL-SPRING objective is selecting on the wrong quantity once a distribution is free.** The *worse* equal-spring placement (0.2424 against 0.1726) is the better one under a minimax (0.0682 against 0.0648) **and** keeps 4.2× the plan margin. Rank on the objective the design will be built to, not on the one that is cheap to evaluate.

### Poroelastic transport

- **The layer's hydrodynamic screening length is not known to better than a factor of 6.** Segment-scale models give `√k ≈ 0.9 nm`, the measurement-anchored `k = ξ²` gives 5.6 nm, and at `φ/φ# ≈ 1` the blob is two thirds of the coil — so these describe the same object. Bracket it and quote from the slow end; never a single number.
- **Squeeze-out under a tile is a footprint problem, not a thickness problem.** `τ = ηG/(kMf)`: the thickness cancels except through the Brinkman wall correction, and `τ ∝ L²` in the tile edge. Lateral and vertical drainage cross over at `L = 3.4 h`, so at 40 x 40 nm on a 10 nm layer neither path is obviously dominant.
- **Use the Brinkman transmissivity `T = k h [1 − (2√k/h) tanh(h/2√k)]`, never plain Darcy `k h`.** It contains the free-film Reynolds squeeze-film limit a layer with `sqrt(k) ~ h` degrades to; plain Darcy overstates drainage by 5x there. Poroelastic drainage and lubrication squeeze film are the same expression at two ends — they are **not** additive channels.

- The box ships **no compiler toolchain at all** — `g++`, `make`, `cmake`, numpy and scipy were all absent and had to be installed. Anything that builds from source (oxDNA, for leaf `A1.2`) needs them first.
- **For an authoritative full-suite run while other agents are working, use `tools/verify.sh`** (`--committed` to test `HEAD` instead of the working tree). At four or more concurrent agents `-PbuildDirectory` is **not sufficient** — the Gradle project lock, `~/.gradle` and the Kotlin daemon are still shared and the incremental compiler's session state races, giving `NoClassDefFoundError` on classes nobody touched. One agent lost fourteen consecutive attempts to this. A copy of the tree has its own project lock and runs clean first time. **Redirect its output to a file rather than piping it through `tail`** — Gradle prints each failure with a full stack trace, so `| tail -30` shows the *last* failing test name and the build result and silently discards the other two, and the snapshot directory the report lives in is deleted on exit. A five-minute run has to be repeated to learn what failed.
- **A sibling breakage is not always a *test* file.** A concurrent agent's half-written `coupling` **main** source failed `compileKotlin` for the whole project, and `--drop coupling` was unusable because `anchoring` imports it. Snapshot the tree and delete the single offending **file**. This has now happened twice; `--drop-file` takes a **main** path as happily as a test one, and a study `main` is the safest thing to drop because nothing imports it.
- **When the broken sibling main source is one your OWN package depends on, restore it to `HEAD` in the snapshot instead of dropping it.** `--drop-file` deletes, and deleting `structure/OrigamiGrillage.kt` takes `structure.Gen1Tile`'s package with it, which `window`, `anchoring` and `coupling` all import. `git show HEAD:<path> > "$target/<path>"` inside a `tools/snapshot.sh` snapshot tests *more* than a drop does and touches nothing in the shared tree — but the sibling's **new** files that depend on their unfinished version must then be dropped too, so the two techniques compose rather than substitute.
- **The set of files needing `--drop-file` GROWS during a long iteration.** A run that was green at the start of an iteration can fail at the end because a sibling has since begun a new file — three accumulated in one iteration here, discovered one at a time because `compileKotlin` stops at the first broken source. Re-read the error list on every failure rather than assuming the previous drop set is still sufficient.
- **Any snapshot's copy-back must be scoped to the file the run produced.** A snapshot is a view of the *past* of every other file in the tree, so copying back "everything that differs" reverts concurrent agents' fresh output. This bit **twice in one iteration**: in `tools/study.sh`, and again in a private runner that repeated the mistake after the fix.
- **The Kotlin daemon OOMs under four or more concurrent agents even with 9 GB free**, reporting `Not enough memory to run compilation`. Append `kotlin.daemon.jvmargs=-Xmx3g` to the *snapshot's* `gradle.properties` — a `-D` on the Gradle command line does not reach the daemon.
- **`--drop <pkg>` removes a package's MAIN sources too, so it cannot be used when your own package depends on the broken one.** `anchoring` imports `structure.Gen1Tile`, so a sibling's half-written `structure` *test* file cannot be worked around with `--drop structure`. Copy the tree and delete the offending test **file**; the whole-package drop is the wrong granularity for a dependency.
- **`drop_packages` deletes, and `tools/{verify,study}.sh --drop` is the only supported way to call it.** It now refuses any target containing `.git`, because an agent called it with the checkout root and removed a package from the *working tree* while another agent was writing into it. And dropping one package cascades: `--drop anchoring` breaks `coupling`, which imports it.
- **The Kotlin daemon reports its OOM two ways** — `OutOfMemoryError: Java heap space` and `Back-end (JVM) Internal error: Couldn't transform method node` on a file that compiled seconds earlier. A fourth member of the `NoClassDefFoundError` family, and the same cure.
- **Under a load average above ~10 the Kotlin daemon OOMs during `compileKotlin`** (`GC overhead limit exceeded`; `in-process` merely moves it into the Gradle daemon). `-Dkotlin.daemon.jvmargs=-Xmx3g` fixes it — it is contention, not a broken build.
- **When more than one agent works this checkout, pass `-PbuildDirectory=<dir>` to every Gradle command.** Concurrent runs otherwise race on `build/test-results` and fail with `EOFException` or `NoSuchFileException` on the in-progress results binary — a harness failure that reads exactly like a broken test. `build-*/` is git-ignored. Under heavy load a run can still die with `Test process encountered an unexpected problem`; that is contention, so retry rather than "fixing" a test.

## Known gotchas

- **A quadrature limit SNAPPED to a mesh node is a hidden discretisation, and it can move further than the physics being measured.** `fitEdgeTaper` advances its rim standoff to the first sample at or beyond 1 nm, and `PoissonBoltzmannEdge` grades its lateral mesh over `[0, a]` — so **the tile's own half-width chooses the standoff**, 1.0345 nm at `a = 20.00` against 1.0273 at 19.04, and the integrand there is the **peak** of the edge enhancement, 1.88× the interior load. The smooth/rim **partition** therefore moved **2.15 %** between two tile widths where their **sum** — the global momentum flux — moved **0.040 %**; the partition alone is worth **5.2×** what re-solving the physics is worth on the downstream flatness. Three signatures, each removed by interpolating the limit instead of snapping it: the departure does not converge (6.8 / 12.9 / 2.2 / 5.6 % over refinements 1/2/3/4, a scatter *larger* than itself), the half-width sweep is not monotone, and the exact-limit sweep is. **When a decomposition is a bookkeeping choice, quote the SUM** — and check whether any consumer reads one term alone.
- **A departure at matched refinement cancels everything two solves SHARE and nothing the swept parameter does to the MESH.** It divides out the model, the ion statistics, `C-0005`'s 123–214 % mean-field bracket, the rim-charge convention and the corner cut-off — which is what makes a 0.04 % answer quotable inside a 214 % error bar — and it divides out **nothing** from a grading whose node positions are proportional to the variable being swept. *"Matched refinement"* is not *"the same mesh"*.
- **An AMPLIFICATION is a ratio and it MIS-RANKS ACROSS TOPOLOGIES.** A coupling that improves its own zero-defect baseline by 24× while improving its worst single removal by 2.7× reports the *worst* fragility ratio in the corpus (20.96 against an array's 2.06–7.10) and is absolutely the *least* fragile design in it. Declare a falsifier on the absolute quantity the tolerance is written on, never on a ratio to a baseline the change under test moves.
- **A convergence DEPARTURE is a difference of two nearly equal solves, so it is the field a JIT recompilation moves — and `RESULT_ABSOLUTE_FLOOR` cannot catch it.** Two runs of one study agreed on every number in a 100 kB result file and disagreed in the 11th decimal of one departure (`3.19469867e−11` against `3.19472365e−11`), because the floor is a claim in the **locked units** and a departure between two dimensionless ratios is not in them (`P-18`). Emit convergence departures at **two significant digits**, which is what `CLAUDE.md` already asks for and what nothing was enforcing.
- **Rayleigh-Ritz truncation OVERESTIMATES a body's stiffness, so the basis order is a declared convergence axis whenever a stiff body is the favourable case.** Here the rigid limit and a four-layer brick differ by 2.8× in dishing (0.0355 against 0.1002) and the Ritz degree settles to `3e−11` from degree 3 on: the gap is physical and the truncation is not what makes it.
- **`+` binds tighter than `.format()` INSIDE A RECORD FIELD too, and there the failure is silent until somebody reads the JSON.** Two `settles` strings reached a committed result file carrying raw `%.4f` and `%.0f`, where the same mistake in a *findings* line would have been read out loud at the end of the run. Parenthesise the whole concatenation, and grep the emitted file for `%\.[0-9][dfsg]` before quoting it.
- **A solver's bracketing strategy can be written into published claims as PHYSICS, in more than one vocabulary, and no claim can see another's copy.** `C-0039`'s blind doubling force ladder appears three times in three files: as *"the arm folds before reaching it"* in a placement table, as an *"element model branch end"* in a ceiling taxonomy that a whole challenge was then raised about, and as a refusal note in a reach catalogue. One repair removed all three, and the **only** thing that found them was re-running every consumer and diffing it — no reading of any one record could have.
- **A repair must leave the defect MEASURABLE.** `C-0092`'s evidence is a *measurement* of the ladder's artefact, so repairing it in place would have made a standing claim uncheckable and moved 53 of 1053 fields of its own result file. Retain the old strategy behind a named opt-in used by exactly the one reproduction that needs it — twenty lines against replacing one unfalsifiable number with another. And check the cost before assuming the safer strategy is dearer: the continuation is **190 RK4 sweeps against the ladder's 209**, because each rung starts where the last one ended.
- **Correcting one argument of a `min` moves only the rows that argument owns, and that is a containment, not a tolerance.** 96 fold rows were owed a re-read at a corrected element domain; 54 carried a load line with no element in it and 36 had a layer-owned ceiling, so `min(a, b) = a` excluded 90 of them for free and the outstanding set was **six**. One pass over the upstream result file against 96 field solves — and **re-running all 108 anyway is what turns the argument into a measurement** (0 of 90 moved).
- **A moved STRING is not necessarily a moved decision, and here it was 40 % of them.** A result file is rounded at the serialisation boundary and a number emitted *inside prose* is not, so a diff classifier must strip digits before calling a prose change a verdict change: **23 of 57** apparent decisions were `Double.toString()` moving in its sixteenth digit inside a sentence.
- **A per-path force placed by a mandate is a TIE against its own per-path allowable.** `C-0017` places 100 pN over 10 paths, so `forcePerPathAtWorking` is exactly `10.0` against the 10 pN unzip allowable and which side of the comparison it falls on is decided by the last ulp: two of `C-0046`'s sixty binding-constraint **lists** change under any upstream perturbation while **no emitted number of either row moves at all**. Compare a placed quantity against its own allowable with an absolute tolerance, or the file reports noise as a finding.
- **A dependency census of this repository cannot be produced by reading, and the shortfall is a THIRD of the graph, not an edge.** `C-0073` grepped `File("gpd/results/` and reported one reader of `T-1d`; a derived census (`tools/result-reader-census.py`, `P-22`) finds **41 direct read edges and 20 transitive ones**, all 20 assembled from a directory in the caller and a name in the helper. **The grep is a strict lower bound and the derivation is asserted to be a superset of it** — a literal the grep finds that the census does not attribute is a derivation bug, never a discovery. And the convention that would have made the graph self-maintaining is used by **2 of 72 studies**: measure a convention before resting a test on it.
- **A static call graph over FILES is not a conservative approximation, it is noise, because Kotlin scopes names per file.** Package `window` declares `ledger`, `array`, `reader` and `scalar` **privately in several files at once**, so at file granularity `DesignWindowStudy` — which reads three result files — comes out reading **thirteen**. Attribute at **declaration** granularity, resolve a name in its own file first, and treat a `private` top-level declaration as invisible outside it. Over-inclusion is the safe direction only until it swamps the signal.
- **The cost of re-emitting a rounding site is a statement about its OUTPUTS, and the two directions rank oppositely.** `window/` reads the most (`CH-0092`) and is the **cheapest** of the three over-printed sites to re-emit — nothing outside `window/` reads `T-2`, `T-25` or `T-118`. Its hazard is not size but **ORDER**: `T-118` reads `T-25`, and running the second against a stale first is exactly the failure that left a reproduction residual at `8.79e−7` for an iteration. A per-site digit count cannot show a sequence.
- **A seam deletes an extremum, and a deleted extremum takes the node with it.** `C-0076`'s congruence — every station on a weave node, independently of the amplitude — is a property of a **seamless** sheet. At any even plane every duplex participates in exactly one interface crossover, so removing one junction column removes exactly **one pull event from every duplex**: each is then straight at `±Δ/4` over four planes and every interface is pinned at `mean ± Δ/2`. On a 40 nm tile (3.68 weave periods, 34 stations) **there is no seam position that touches nothing**. And *"opens up"* is the favourable half — the other parity **closes** by the same amount, which is what takes an across-row clearance negative.
- **A numerical guard's own justification is a statement about a STATE, and it expires when the state moves.** `CrossoverLayout.EDGE_MARGIN = 0.05 nm` exists so that a column cannot seed a zero-length beam element, and its KDoc certifies it inert: *"far below the 0.28 nm closest approach any base-pair phase makes on a 40 nm tile, so it never decides a column count that the physics does not already decide."* True at 40.0 nm and **false at 38.08 nm**, where the tile is an exact whole number of column pitches — there the guard deletes **two of eight** columns at exactly the two phases the design wants, and it is worth **0.0621 against 0.1684** in the flatness, i.e. inside `T-5b` against outside it. The guard was right; the sentence beside it was the assertion. **Re-read a tolerance constant's justification whenever the geometry it was written against changes** — it is *"quote it with the state it is read at"* applied to a `const val`.
- **"Bit-identical" is not assertable on a SOLVED FIELD of this lattice, and it is on the load vector.** Two solves of two *identically constructed* `OrigamiGrillage` objects in one JVM differ by ~4 ulp (`1.2e−12` relative on a well-conditioned point-load dishing) — the JIT recompiling a hot reduction between the two calls, which `CLAUDE.md` already records for re-runs and which is just as true **within** a run. So a gate written as *"an empty option map is bit-identical to the unmodified lattice"* must be taken on `assembleLoad`, where the arithmetic is a fixed-order scatter-add, and the solved field asserted at `1e−10`. Choosing a *well-conditioned* load case matters too: under a bare uniform pressure the dishing is its own `1e−11` conditioning noise, so the same comparison is a test of nothing.
- **A SECANT of a peak is not the peak of the unit response, and a bound written from one is not the bound named from the other.** `C-0104`'s `dishingPerRadian` is `|D(1 rad) − D(0)|` = 0.1404, and its prose calls the ceiling built on it a triangle inequality; the triangle inequality needs `max|B|` = 0.1883 and gives a threshold of **11.52°** where the secant gives 15.45°. Peak dishing is a max of affine functions and therefore **convex**, so a chord from zero lies above it and the secant bound survives — by an argument the claim does not make. Whenever a "rigorous ceiling" is quoted, check which functional of the unit response it was built from.
- **A search's PATH is not reproducible even when its answer is, and the path is what a diagnostic field records.** Rounding at the serialisation boundary does not save an evaluation count, a sweep count or a *"which start won"* label: a last-ulp difference in the objective (the JIT recompiling a hot reduction mid-run) flips a strict comparison and the descent reaches the same optimum by another route. Three runs here agreed on every objective to nine significant digits and disagreed on all three diagnostics. Emit the answer and a **two-significant-digit** convergence measure; emit nothing that counts steps. The same family as the argmin trap, one level further out.
- **A WALL-CLOCK TIMING is the extreme case of that rule and it belongs in the console, never in the result file.** *"Measure the cost of the inner loop before choosing the budget of the outer one"* is a research-practice rule and the measurement is worth making — but two runs of one study here agreed on all 108 folds and all 65 reproductions and differed on **exactly** the three millisecond fields. A timing is *less* reproducible than a step count, not more. Print it, quote it in the claim with its spread over runs, and emit only the deterministic half: the solve **count** per unit of work.
- **A finite difference of a law has a TIGHTER domain than the law.** The recommended arm's reaction closes 0.0008 nm further out than its tangent, because the tangent is a forward difference of the same solve — so *"the largest stroke the element answers at"* is two different numbers, and a path's ceiling must take the smaller. Cheap to check, invisible if you only probe the primary quantity.
- **A WALL CLOCK in a result file is a step counter by another name.** `T-148`'s two independent runs agreed on all 2 037 other lines and differed on exactly one: a `runtimeSeconds` parameter. `CLAUDE.md`'s own *"emit the answer and a convergence measure; emit nothing that counts steps"* covers it and it was still emitted — and one such field makes the whole file permanently un-diffable, which is the check the rounding layer exists to enable. Put the timing in the console log, never in the JSON.
- **A DECISION must be rounded COARSER than the number it is taken on, and nine significant digits is not coarse enough.** A backtracking Armijo test, an "is this iterate better" test and a choice between starts are all `Double` comparisons a last-ulp jitter can flip, and one flipped step moves a descent into a neighbouring basin — 0.14 % in an answer whose inputs agreed to `1e-15`. At nine digits the chance a pair straddles a quantisation boundary is ~`1e-6` per comparison, and a search that takes `~1e6` of them therefore flips **every run**: two runs of one study differing only in an *unused local* disagreed in the sixth digit throughout. Decide at **six** significant digits (~`1e-9` per comparison) with the earlier candidate winning ties, and emit at nine. Reproducibility is a property of the decision precision, not of the output precision.
- **A descent on an optimal MANIFOLD has no isolated answer to be reproducible about, and no amount of rounding supplies one.** Where the active constraints are fewer than the free directions — 2 against 44 in a two-state minimax over 45 stiffnesses — the optimal set is a manifold, so the terminal point is whatever the trajectory reached. Rounding the *decisions* stabilises which branch is taken (`1e-3` → `1e-6` in the objective here) and does **not** stabilise the point; **Polak-Ribière is the amplifier**, its `beta` numerator `g·(g − g_prev)` cancelling catastrophically once the iteration settles so an ulp becomes an `O(1)` direction change. Periodic restarts, a small-difference restart and a lattice snap on the iterate took a diff from 1 599 lines to 28. **Report the residual rather than asserting byte-identity** — and check what depends on the *point* rather than on the *value*: a 45-parameter optimum's per-path spread and its quantisability onto two levels were **not** stable across equally optimal members, which is a result about the design space and not a defect.
- **`roundForResult` THROWS on an exact zero when its absolute floor is zero** — it takes `log10(|value|)` and hands `roundToLong` a `NaN`, reporting `Cannot round NaN value` from inside the descent that called it. A *decision* precision is a statement about **relative** precision, so the floor cannot be used to cure it; pass `0.0` and every non-finite value straight through. The case is not exotic: it is what a falsifier test whose objective is exactly zero at its target constructs on purpose.
- **A descent's approach to a KINEMATIC limit is first order in the stiffness scale and its prefactor is a CONDITIONING, so the naive convergence test fails from the first rung.** `K_c(sT)/s → T − TΦ(ΦᵀTΦ)⁻¹ΦᵀT` as `s → ∞`, but the `O(1/s)` term carries `S⁻¹HS⁻¹` with `S = ΦᵀTΦ`, so at four nearly collinear stations the departure is still **52 %** at `s = 100` and a decade of scale buys 4.7× rather than 10×. Assert that `s × departure` **settles**, never that the ratio is ten.
- **An out-of-sample percentile descent can LOSE, and where it does is informative.** `C-0089` measured 1.30–1.61× for a 90-parameter percentile descent on an array, because an array divides `C-0017`'s mandate and a distribution redistributes a scarce budget. The same machinery on a shared body — whose stiff ties are already at a kinematic limit — has nothing to win and chases the training sample's noise instead. **Emit the in-sample objective beside the out-of-sample one**, or an over-fit is inferred rather than seen.
- **`com.xemantic.kotlin.test.assert` refuses `<=` and `>=` inside its argument**, with `'operator' modifier is required on 'fun <T> Comparable<T>.compareTo(other: T): Int'` pointing at the comparison and not at the import. Write `<` and `>` (tighten the tolerance by an ulp if it matters); every existing test in the tree already does, which is why it looks like a convention rather than a constraint.
- **Kotlin's `String.format` with one placeholder too many does not fail where the placeholder is — it fails at the FIRST argument of the wrong type**, `IllegalFormatConversionException: d != java.lang.String`, hundreds of characters downstream. In a long concatenated finding string, count the placeholders against the argument list before spending the run; here it cost 17 minutes of completed computation at the last line before the JSON was written. **Build the result and write the JSON before formatting any prose.**
- **A descent over a superset can return a WORSE point than a descent over the subset it contains.** An unconstrained optimisation started elsewhere came back above the same problem's constrained optimum, which reads as a bug in the constraint and is not one. Enforce the containment by construction — run the constrained problem first and seed the unconstrained one with its answer — and then their agreeing is a *result* (here: the per-path ceiling costs nothing at 45 paths) rather than a coincidence.
- **A central difference divides by the SEPARATION of its two samples, and `2·step` is right only when `step` is the half-step.** Reading `step` off the wrong axis makes the gradient exactly half, and **no dimensional check catches it** — the quantity has the right units either way. Name the separation once in a function; a hand-written `/(2 * step)` at three call sites is three chances to get it wrong.
- **A gradient converges more slowly than the quantity it differences, and it has to be checked separately.** Refining the 2-D edge mesh 2 → 3 → 4 moved `μ` by 6.4e−4 and `d ln μ/dh` by **5.1e−3** at refinement 2, and 1.8e−4 against 1.1e−3 at refinement 3 — 6–8× worse, from the same solves. "Convergence is a property of the quantity" applies to derived quantities too, and a derivative is the one most likely to be quietly under-resolved.
- **A safeguarded SECANT root-finder that exits only on bracket width STALLS, and returns the midpoint of a bracket whose root sits at one end.** Once the residual is locally linear the first secant step is already exact, the retained endpoint stops moving, and `high − low` never falls below the tolerance. This is `CLAUDE.md`'s own *"an unreachable convergence tolerance is silent"* in the one place where it is silently **wrong** rather than merely slow: bisection's exit criterion is not a secant method's. Exit on the iteration's own **step**, `|x_{k+1} − x_k|`, with the bracket-width test kept beside it.
- **An elastica's shooting residual is not monotone in the shooting parameter, and NEITHER end of the assumed bracket is safe.** Past a right angle the tip force's moment arm reverses; the residual at a zero near-end rotation can then come out *positive*, which is the opposite of the small-load case. Scan geometrically for the **first sign change** and bisect on that bracket; where there is none, refuse rather than return a root off the small-rotation branch. Carry `max_s|φ|` as a field so the reader can see which branch a state is on.
- **A validity ceiling can fail to exist rather than being small.** At the top of `C-0027`'s 10 nm `σ` window the layer already sits past `C-0002`'s `φ = 0.2` at **zero** compression, so `L₀ − Nσv₀/φ_max` is negative. Emit `null` and record the fact; a clamped zero reads as a layer with no usable stroke rather than as a layer outside its own equation of state.
- **A root-finder handed a target the function never reaches should return `null`, and the `null` is a VERDICT.** `exclusionWidthAtEnergy` above the barrier value means *"the affordable width is below the model floor"*, which is the answer; `require`-ing the target into range turns a result into a crash 400 lines downstream.
- **A feature-locating scan that starts one step above its floor misses a feature inside the first interval, and it reports that as absence.** A DLVO barrier 0.02 nm above the model floor was found at a 0.002 nm scan step and *not found* at 0.02 — a `null` that reads as *"the energy is monotone"*. Start the scan at the floor plus an epsilon, never at the floor plus the step.
- **A residual sliver is TRIM, not a disconnected component.** Cutting an element out of a lattice at the site nearest the edge leaves a 0.28 nm stub that a design simply does not build; counting it as a piece reported a placement artefact as a structural failure and inflated a component count by 40 %. Discard residual pieces below a physical scale (one duplex diameter) and report the discards separately — the same discipline as *"a load-path contour average is not a peak"*.
- **A packing verdict is a proof only when a CONSTRUCTION meets an independent upper bound.** Report both: a greedy placement alone is a lower bound and a per-row maximum ignoring the sharing constraint is an upper one, and where they coincide — 43 here — no better placement exists and no cleverer packer need be written.
- **A "weak spring" regularisation of a mechanism is a spring to GROUND, so it breaks the very symmetry the falsifier tests.** An armed tile under a *uniform* load must dish exactly zero and dishes `O(ε)` instead, because the `ε`-springs act as vanishingly weak anchors. The cure is not a smaller `ε` but to assert the **rate**: linear convergence over four decades is the numerical signature of an exact zero, where a genuine added stiffness would converge to a non-zero limit.
- **A private DOF layout can be reconstructed from a class's PUBLIC API and asserted against its own basis vector**, which is how to extend a shared main source without editing it when other agents are in the tree. `basisAt` at a node returns exactly `1.0` at that node's own `w` — nothing forces the reconstruction to be right, and that one line is what makes it falsifiable.
- **A strict `>` between two quantities that can be EQUAL BY CONSTRUCTION reports a floating-point tie as a finding.** Two independently computed misalignment floors coincided at exactly 24° at one row pitch and the bare comparison emitted `true`; the fix is an absolute tolerance at the decision point, not rounding at the serialisation boundary — the same family as *"comparing two quantities that are both meant to be zero relatively compares their noise"*, and it survives rounding because a boolean is not a rounded double.
- **A scaling exponent fitted through PER-POINT OPTIMA is not an exponent in the swept variable, and the two can differ by a sign.** A search's slack varies with what is being swept, so a sequence of upper bounds carries no slope: on one lattice, one phase, one topology and one ensemble, `C-0103`'s redundancy exponent is **−0.740** through a family **nested by construction** and **+0.061** through placement-**searched** subsets of the same 53 sites. Fit a scaling law through a nested family, or measure the slack (an oracle floor over the found value) at every point before reading a slope through it — `CH-0119`.
- **A difference of two nearby cells must be read on a PAIRED sample, and a stochastic sweep should share one stream restricted per cell rather than draw one per cell.** `C-0089` draws a Bernoulli dropout ensemble per station set, so a 34-vs-30 comparison mixes a design difference with two independent samples' noise; `C-0103` draws one stream over the whole 53-site inventory and restricts it, and the 34 → 30 difference then sits **32×** above its own convergence departure. Common random numbers cost one function and are what make a 13 % effect readable at all.
- **A toy-sample smoke run tests the plumbing and must NOT be read for a falsifier verdict.** At 150 realisations `T-163`'s monotonicity falsifier `F1` fired on one of two chains; at the declared 10 000 it did not fire on either, and nothing but the sample count changed. Run the smoke pass for the `String.format` placeholders, the JSON schema and the wall time — never for the answer.
- **A verdict that is not MONOTONE in a swept variable has no threshold, and sweeping it finer finds more alternation rather than less.** `C-0070`'s lateral seat passes at 4 of 11 sampled values with the failures interleaved: it is a **register**, not a tolerance, so a scatter model on it is meaningless and a bisection on it is a fiction. Check monotonicity before quoting a threshold — the same discipline as `C-0015`'s *"shapes, not diagonals"*.
- **Two claims can publish the same number twice without either noticing, because a margin is a REGROUPING.** `C-0069`'s *"budget minus arm"* `(p − d) − L` and `C-0066`'s *"tip gap minus duplex"* `(p − L) − d` are one subtraction and one 0.0256 nm. Before treating two thin margins as independent exposures, expand both and compare.
- **A boundary maximum is not a stationary point.** Reporting a tangency residual `k_c + k_eff` at a fold located at the *end* of a branch gives `6e13` where the interior folds give `9e−6` — and the scale to divide by is the sum of the *magnitudes* of the stiffnesses, since two of three load lines have `k_c = 0`.
- **A golden-section maximum is floored by the noise of the search underneath it, not by its own bracket.** With a bias bisection at relative bracket `t`, the stroke at a fold is resolvable only to about `λ√(2t)`; tightening the outer tolerance alone changes nothing.
- **Sampling a continuum field at the lattice's own nodes can silently break a conservation law.** Every non-uniform cosine mode integrates to zero over a duplex's *tributary strip*, so the strip integral makes the member forces sum to the applied load identically; evaluating on the duplex axes instead aliases, and the "total" came out 130 % of the applied force for a soft crossover. Integrate over the tributary, do not sample the axis.
- **A cut-equilibrium diagnostic can be degraded by the very physics it is asked to measure.** `shearAcrossInterface` integrates `k_f w` over panels that straddle the tributary strips; across a *severed* interface the reconstructed deflection is genuinely discontinuous, a Gauss-Legendre panel spanning the jump misreads the foundation reaction, and the identity that holds to `1e−6` on an intact sheet is out by 0.06 pN on a broken one. It is not a solver error. Take the conservation gate on the **global** balance — applied load against foundation plus anchors — which is exact even on a sheet in fifteen pieces, and assert the cut identity only where it is valid.
- **The "global balance" is only exact on a field the two quadratures integrate identically.** A consistent element load vector (one Gauss rule per element and per tributary strip) and `integrateOverFootprint` (a panel-refined rule over the whole tile) agree to machine precision on a *uniform* pressure and differ by **0.07 %** on `edgeCollarPressure`'s 1 nm rim term, because neither resolves a sub-panel feature the same way. Take the conservation gate on uniform-plus-point-loads, where it is a `1e−9` statement, and assert the collar profile at the `1e−3` its own quadrature supports.
- **`kotlinx.serialization` refuses `NaN`** (`JsonEncodingException: Unexpected special floating-point value`), so a ratio field guarding a zero denominator must emit a sentinel, not `Double.NaN` — and where both quantities are meant to be near zero the right fix is to compare **absolutely** and carry no ratio at all.
- viktor rejects empty arrays already on construction — `DoubleArray(0).asF64Array()` throws `IllegalArgumentException: empty arrays not supported` — so an empty `F64Array` cannot exist, and guarding a function against one is dead code.
- `L₀ = N a^(5/3) σ^(1/3)` evaluated in floating point does not land on a round number even when the inputs are chosen so it should — `0.125.pow(1.0/3.0)` is `0.5000000000000001`. Do not assert an exact equilibrium height, and do not embed one in a `require` message that a test then matches literally; interpolate the computed value instead.
- **The contact-value theorem is the worst-conditioned way to evaluate a disjoining pressure at a large gap.** The first integral is constant so any node is exact in principle, but not in floating point: at a 30 nm gap the pressure is four orders of magnitude below the two terms it is the difference of, and a 1e-6 profile error becomes a 4 % force error. Evaluate at the node minimising `|Π_osm| + |Maxwell|`, and report the spread over the *core* of the gap — the full spread measures the conditioning of the diagnostic, not the accuracy of the answer.
- **A re-entrant corner's traction is mesh-divergent, not mesh-dependent.** A 90° wedge gives `r^(−2/3)`, and one more lateral derivative takes it past integrability on a five-point stencil: refining 1 → 2 → 4 took the rim-node load through 10.8, 32.5, 90.8 pN/nm² while everything else converged at second order. Cut it off at a physical scale (a duplex radius) and recover the residual from a global momentum balance.
- **The contact-value theorem is even worse in two dimensions, and its cure costs a term.** The first integral is no longer constant — `∂_z T_zz = −∂_x T_zx` — so moving off the wall to the well-conditioned interior node requires carrying the integrated shear stress out to the wall. Worth it: the contact route is 248 % wrong where the interior route is 0.03 % wrong.
- **Grade the direction your preconditioner solves exactly as hard as you like, and the other direction as little as you can.** A line-relaxation preconditioner removes the `z` grading from the condition number entirely; the `x` grading lands in it undiminished, and a `tanh` `β = 6` there (a 3.6e4 spacing ratio) stops conjugate gradients converging at all.
- **`y = 0` is the wrong far-field datum next to a biased electrode.** Use the electrode's own single-plate profile: the zero datum fabricates a boundary layer along the last mesh column whose induced charge exceeded the tile's own by an order of magnitude, and it is invisible in the force. The charge balance is what catches it.
- **A sign error in a Newton right-hand side converges — to the reflection of the answer.** Assembling `A = −J` so the system is SPD makes `J δ = −F` read `A δ = +F`; negating in both places leaves a solver that runs its full iteration cap with the correction pinned at the damping ceiling, which reads as a conditioning problem and is not one.
- **A Gouy-Chapman length of 0.09 nm across a 30 nm gap needs a *graded* mesh, not a fine one.** A uniform mesh that under-resolves the wall leaves an *absolute* error in the surface potential, which enters the pressure as a constant offset and destroys any decay-length measurement at large separation. `tanh` grading at both walls keeps the scheme second order.
- ~~**`bracketedRoot` can evaluate outside its own bracket.**~~ **REPAIRED (`P-15`, `C-0031`), and it was two defects.** The reported one: a sign test written on a *product* underflows to `−0.0` (step test → bracket lost → escape) or `+0.0` (entry test → a bracket with no root accepted). **Never write a sign test as a product** — compare signs, and carry the endpoint's sign as a flag *separate* from its residual, because the Illinois halving mutates the magnitude of a value whose endpoint has not moved. It needs both residuals below `1.5e-154`, so it is **not reachable at this project's scales**; a direct probe of the unrepaired routine on residuals spanning 30 decades escapes zero times. The unreported one, active at **every** call: **the Illinois halving was unconditional.** Dowell & Jarratt halve only in their case (ii), when the *older* endpoint is retained; halving every step deflates both residuals once the estimate alternates sides, they interpolate to the midpoint, and the method silently becomes bisection while still paying for a secant — 52 evaluations against bisection's ~52 on `x²−2`, where correct Illinois takes **11**. The answer is correct to the last ulp either way, which is why it survived.
- **A DOUBLING LADDER DOES NOT REPORT A BRANCH END; IT REPORTS HAVING LOST THE BRANCH.** `TwoSpringElastica.forceForDisplacement` doubles a tip force until the stroke reaches its target and `stateAtForce` brackets the shooting parameter by doubling from a seed; both are exact while the far-end residual has **one** root, and on the Gen-1 arm a second appears at ~100 pN, three decades of force **below** the right angle the KDoc warns about. `C-0084` read the resulting throw as a branch end at **7.9197 nm**; continued by marching the near-end rotation and taking the first sign change in the force above the previous one, the same arm answers to **8.1611 nm** with `max_s|φ|` at 0.999997 of `π/2` and still below it. The ceiling-side twin of the search-floor trap below.
- **An inextensible member's reach is bounded by an INTEGRAL OF A BOUNDED FUNCTION, and that bound holds on every branch.** `δ = ∫₀^L sin φ ds < L`, strictly, because `φ ≡ π/2` contradicts `EI φ′(0) = k_n φ(0)`. It costs nothing, it needs no convergence parameter, and it turned *"does a fold exist at a larger stroke"* — open on an unbounded axis — into a **0.2447 nm** window before any code ran. Look for the bounded-integral bound before enumerating branches.
- **A taxonomy gap's price is set by whichever candidate is SECOND, not by the last one on the list.** `CH-0099` priced a missing ceiling at 2.57–3.74× by comparing against `CH-0007`'s 1.0 V; with the boundary corrected the movement is **1.0007–1.1620×** at four states, where `C-0002`'s `φ = 0.2` steps in, and 2.84–3.34× at the other four. Reprice a taxonomy gap against the next candidate, not against the default.
- **A search floor written as a multiple of the working stroke silently excludes designs whose working stroke IS the target.** `elasticaArmForStiffness` brackets from `1.5 δ`, which is 15 nm when the placement is read at §3's desired 10 nm, and it refuses arms of 11.4–13.8 nm that exist. Walk **down** from the long end instead: the assembled secant is monotone in the arm, but a short arm at a large stroke *folds* and the shooting solve throws rather than returning a number, so a fixed floor is both too high for the design and too low for the solver.
- **An escape from a bracket is always a lost bracket, and that is a theorem, not a diagnosis.** A secant through two opposite-signed ordinates is a *convex combination* of its endpoints, so while the bracket holds an escape is arithmetically impossible. Any evaluation outside `[low, high]` therefore proves the sign test misfired — which is how `S-143`'s observation became `C-0031`'s cause without reproducing the run.
- **A bracket WALK and the root finder it feeds must share ONE residual function, evaluated at exactly the abscissae that are passed on.** Spelling the walk `achieved(x) < target` and the root `ln(achieved(x)/target)` is a sign test written two ways: `achieved` can exceed `target` by one ulp while the quotient rounds to `1.0` and its logarithm comes out `−3.3e−16`, so the walk certifies a bracket whose upper endpoint `bracketedRoot`'s entry `require` then rejects. Same family as *"never write a sign test as a product"* and **reachable at every scale** rather than only below `1.5e−154`. It bites hardest where the seed IS the answer — an Alexander box has `2⟨z⟩ = L` exactly, so all 183 of its first-moment inversions land on the tie — and **a three-point check passed while the 61-point sweep did not**: whether the ulp falls the fatal way is a property of the point.
- **A quantity solved inside an accessor must be hoisted out of any quadrature that calls it.** `StrongStretchingLayer.volumeFractionAt` re-runs the coverage-conserving Newton solve for `λ` on **every** call, so a 512-panel first-moment quadrature written on the public accessor is ~500× slower than one that recovers `λ` once — three minutes against milliseconds, and it turned a unit test into a timeout. The recovery is exact and needs no new API: the strong-stretching potential is `λ − Az²`, so `μ(φ(0))` inverts straight back to `λ`, `exchangeChemicalPotential` and `volumeFractionAtChemicalPotential` being each other's inverse.
- **A defect that is invisible in the answer is invisible to every check written on the answer.** Three instances now: `S-95`'s copy-back reverted a result file and "no number moved"; `S-166`'s rounding made an *argmin* irreproducible while every number was identical; and `C-0031`'s solver returned roots correct to the last ulp while doing 3–5× the work it was selected for. **Check the thing the component was chosen for** — here the evaluation count, asserted as a test with a bound that fails if it degenerates back.
- **An absolute zero-floor is NOT inherited by what is computed from the floored value, and a reciprocal derivation amplifies exactly what the floor was hiding.** `roundForResult`'s `RESULT_ABSOLUTE_FLOOR = 1e-9` emits a rounding-level `layerStiffness` as exactly `0.0` — honest — while `√(k_BT/k)` derived from it **escapes the floor because it is large**, so `T-8`'s committed file asserted `"layerStiffness": 0.0` and `"unconstrainedPistonRms": 1172864.7` on adjacent lines: not implausible but arithmetically impossible. Floor the **derived** field on its own physical criterion (here, that a linearised fluctuation must stay inside the layer it fluctuates against), never on a sign test against the floored quantity — `stiffness > 0.0` is a sign test on a quantity `C-0003` says is exactly zero, so the noise decides it. Found by `P-15`; **two of three cases were already wrong in `HEAD`** and the solver repair only changed which.
- **Round a result file to the precision the answer is DETERMINED to, not to a fixed digit count.** The tree rounds to nine significant digits; `SelfConsistentFieldLayer.heightAtPressure` carries `HEIGHT_TOLERANCE = 1e-6`, so anything downstream of a solved height is printed three digits past what it is pinned to. A byte-for-byte re-run diff then certifies that **nothing perturbed the iteration** — a property of the *path* — and is silently read as a property of the *answer*: repairing `bracketedRoot` moved 589 fields of `T-1f` by a median `9.0e-7`, i.e. by exactly `HEIGHT_TOLERANCE`, with **no verdict and no quoted figure changing**. (`CH-0043`.)
- **The cheapest place to evaluate an SCF layer is never its own floor.** At the saturation height the layer is a melt, the node spacing collapses to `h/24`, the contour step count goes as `1/Δz²`, and the self-consistent iteration does not converge — so one guard evaluation runs the full 8000-iteration cap over a 10⁵-step contour and burns ninety minutes without throwing. Diagnose a "slow" study with `jcmd <pid> Thread.print` before assuming it is contention.
- **An SCF window edge is not grid-converged where a stiffness is.** The 10 nm stroke edge moves **23.4 %** between `Δz = 0.4` and `0.2 nm` while `d ln k/d ln K` moves 0.5 %. Convergence is a property of the quantity, not of the solve.
- **An unreachable convergence tolerance is silent: the loop returns the right answer, having run its full iteration cap every time.** A residual test like `|f(x) − target| ≤ 1e-15 × target` sits below the noise floor of any quadrature of ~10³ terms. Exit on the **bracket width** instead. Combined with an `equilibriumHeight()` recomputed inside a per-call validity check, this turned a 3-minute sweep into one that did not finish — memoise pure per-object functions that a `require` calls.
- `pkill -f <pattern>` **kills your own shell** when the pattern appears in the compound command you are running. Match on the JVM path, or use the PID.
- **And on a shared checkout `pkill -f` kills your NEIGHBOURS.** `tools/study.sh` and `tools/verify.sh` snapshot into `plenty-of-room-study.<pid>` / `plenty-of-room-verify.<pid>`, so any pattern matching the prefix matches every concurrent agent's run — one such command destroyed a sibling's nine-minute study. **Never pattern-kill a snapshot directory; take the PID from `pgrep -af` and kill that one PID.**
- **The same self-match makes a `pgrep -f` wait loop IMMORTAL, and silently.** `until ... ! pgrep -f "plenty-of-room-study.<pid>"; do sleep; done` never exits, because the loop's own command line contains the pattern and `pgrep -f` matches it — so the poll succeeds forever while the study it was watching finished minutes ago. It looks like a study that never ends. Wait on the **result file** (`until [ -f gpd/results/<T>-*.json ]`) or on the log's own success line, never on `pgrep -f` of a string you are yourself running.
- **A convention shift evaluated by moving the GRID measures the grid, not the convention.** Re-gridding a feasible-set sweep around a shifted datum resamples a set that lives on a continuum, and the shifted samples are a different set of points: the first attempt at *"which strand defines the station"* reported **no feasible placement at all** where the same shift applied to the **offsets** leaves every one of them. Move the datum in the *results*, never in the sampling.
- **A per-position candidate CAP is a ranking, and a ranking is not monotone under refinement.** With `C-0059`'s cap of 4 best-aligned candidates, a finer azimuth grid can *lose* a closing position that a coarser one found, because the closer falls out of the top four. Uncapped, the coarse azimuth set is a subset of the fine one and monotonicity is exact — so test convergence **uncapped**, and treat every capped *"does not close"* as weaker than a *"closes"*.
- **Rounding at the serialisation boundary does not make a result file reproducible if it contains an *argmin*.** An index is not a rounded double: where a sweep is flat, two entries tie to the last unit in the last place and the minimum returned depends on summation order, so a re-run diffs in one integer while every number is identical. Round at the decision point too, with the index as tie-break.
- **Set a result file's rounding by the largest movement a change of PATH can produce, never by the largest error the MODEL carries.** For the SCF layer those are `5.6e−7` and `9.9e−5` — two decades apart and in the counter-intuitive order, the model being *less accurate* than the solver is *reproducible*. Rounding to the model's accuracy blinds the diff to a real change of the model; rounding to the solver's reproducibility makes it sensitive to exactly that. The accuracy figure belongs in a validity range, not in a rounding rule.
- **There is no tree-wide digit count: there are SIX independent rounding implementations**, and two of them disagree on the absolute floor (`1e-9` against `1e-12`). "The tree rounds to nine significant digits" was a description, not a design — a change to one site silently leaves the other five. Conversely, a *per-provenance* rule needs no new mechanism.
- **`RESULT_ABSOLUTE_FLOOR` is a claim about UNITS and it does not travel.** It is documented as a magnitude *in the locked units* (`1e-9 pN`), and a **dimensionless** quantity is not in those units at all: `P-18`'s first run emitted its own measured `3.3e−13` movement, and the declared SCF field tolerance `1e−11` in its own parameter block, as `0.0`. Pass a per-call floor whenever a study emits ratios or tolerances. Same shape as `C-0031`'s floored `layerStiffness` beside an unfloored `√(k_BT/k)`.
- **A "two significant digits" rule on a convergence departure is a rule about DEPARTURES, and a study emits more than one kind.** `C-0093` found the trap on its convergence axis and cured it there; its **reproduction** departures — the same difference of two nearly equal numbers, equally dimensionless, equally beyond `RESULT_ABSOLUTE_FLOOR` — stayed at nine digits, and two runs of identical code then agreed on 1 484 of 1 484 numeric fields and disagreed in exactly those two (`1.06411397e−9` against `1.06410993e−9`). **Grep the record type for every field whose value is a difference or a ratio of two nearly equal quantities, not just the one the last claim named.**
- **`M = round(h/Δz)` makes the SCF pressure a DISCONTINUOUS function of the wall height.** The node count steps at every half-integer multiple of `Δz`, the spacing `h/M` steps with it, and `pressureAt` jumps by `4.9e−4`–`9.8e−4` — worth `7.9e−5`–`9.9e−5` of indeterminacy in a solved height, whatever tolerance the root finder is given. And **tightening `HEIGHT_TOLERANCE` from `1e-6` to `1e-9` costs only 1.02× the SCF solves** (Illinois is superlinear), so cost is *not* what argues against it; reachability is.
- **A decision precision and an emission precision that differ mean a file's flags cannot be reproduced from the file's own numbers.** `window/` decides constraint flags at six significant digits — correctly, and it says why — and then emits the numbers they were decided from at nine.
- **A result file is an INPUT, so rounding a producer down moves every consumer that reads it.** `window/DesignWindowStudy.kt` builds its 61-point grafting-density grid out of `gpd/results/T-1d-…json`, so `P-18`'s six-digit `T-1d` moved 4 864 fields of `T-2` through an emitter nobody touched. An audit that greps only for the literal filename misses the directory-joined call site.
- **When a repair moves a downstream result file, RE-EMIT it and amend the claim — never keep the stale file as "the record of what the claim was written on".** Git already holds that record exactly, and a file the code cannot reproduce destroys the byte-for-byte re-run diff half this repository's claims rest on. `T-159` moved **eleven** files and copied none back; `C-0101` re-emitted all eleven. Amend the claim only where it **quotes** a moved number — nine of the eleven needed nothing.
- **A diverged solver parameter can be written into a catalogue as a physical reason, and it reads perfectly.** `C-0050` explained two refusals by *"the arm folds"* on a note citing a near-end rotation of **3.03 × 10¹²¹** — a doubling ladder running away, recorded as a fold. Re-run on a continuation, the two rows refuse for reasons that are *different from each other and from a fold*: one purely **kinematic** (the contour is shorter than the demanded stroke, no solver needed), one a genuine branch limit at **99.7 %** of the stroke where the ladder had reported 89 %. **The verdict survived and its ground was wrong in both** — so grep an emitted note for an implausible magnitude before trusting the sentence around it.
- **An audit is only as complete as the SHAPE of the search that performed it, and a path assembled from a directory and a name is invisible to a search for either half.** `C-0073` audited `P-18`'s propagation with `grep 'File("gpd/results/'`, concluded it closed at one reader, and left `T-25` and `T-118` in the repository built from nine-digit inputs — 325 and 2 751 fields moved on a re-run (`CH-0092`). Grep for the **basename**, and cross-check against what the study's own `sources` field says it reads: a study that names its inputs in a result field has already published the answer the audit is looking for.
- **A reproduction residual is a STALENESS detector, and reading it costs nothing.** `T-118`'s `reproductions/22/relativeDeparture` sat at `8.79e−07` for an iteration and collapsed to **exactly 0.0** once its stale input was re-emitted: the residual *was* the staleness, in a field designed to carry exactly that, unread. - **An assertion tighter than a result file's EMISSION precision is not a stronger test, it is a test of the printed digits.** Five gates that recompute an identity from fields read out of `gpd/results/*.json` asserted at `1e-7`–`1e-6` and passed only because the file printed three digits past what its solver determined; at six digits a single field carries `5e-6` and an identity over three of them `2e-5`. Derive such a tolerance from the emission precision (`EMITTED_FIELD_SLACK`), never from what happens to pass.
- **A number emitted as a STRING is not rounded.** `roundedForResult` dispatches on the JSON type and passes strings through, correctly — so a `(a/b).toString()` in a parameter map carries `Double.toString()`'s full round-trip precision into a file that declares nine digits (ten, in `T-2`'s `graftingDensityGridRatio`). - **A tie is not a rounding artefact, it is a statement that the quoted precision is the wrong one.** `0.260150` is exactly a tie at four significant figures, so `C-0016`'s *"0.2601"* is the side of it one run happened to land on — a `1.5e−6` movement flipped it to `0.2602` (`CH-0085`). If a headline number's next digit is a `5`, the reader cannot recover the value and the *renderer*, not the writer, chose the precision.
- **A per-key rounding rule is the right granularity for PROVENANCE and the wrong one for AMPLIFICATION.** Provenance (which solver produced this quantity) is constant down a column; amplification is a property of the *state* and runs 27.6× at the 10 nm design point against `10⁴` at the sweep's stiff end for the same key. - **A penalty-derived constraint force has a far worse noise floor than the solve does** — a large penalty multiplying a difference of two nearly equal nodal deflections sits near `1e-11` relative, above what nine-significant-digit rounding assumes.
- **`NoClassDefFoundError` has a *third* cause** besides the build race and the OOM test worker: **a sibling package another agent has left mid-TDD**, whose failed `compileKotlin` leaves `build-<agent>/classes/kotlin/main` half-written. Check the classes directory exists before blaming the harness.
- **Rannacher start-up silently breaks SCF density normalisation.** Backward Euler for the first contour steps — the standard cure for Crank-Nicolson ringing on a delta initial condition — destroys `∫q(n)q†(N−n)dz = Q`, which holds only if every contour step applies the *same* operator. - **Kotlin's `+` binds tighter than `.format()`**: `"a %s " + "b %f".format(x, y)` formats only the *second* literal and throws at runtime. Parenthesise the whole concatenation before calling `format`. It can also fail **silently and plausibly** rather than throwing — the leading placeholders print raw and the trailing ones consume the *wrong* arguments, so a findings string came out as "No candidate joint lands below 83 or above 44", a grammatical sentence. - **A `String.format` defect is a LAST-LINE defect, and where the prose IS a field of the result the standing cure — *"build the result and write the JSON before formatting any prose"* — is unachievable.** The `+`-binds-tighter trap above fired again with the rule already written down, and threw `IllegalFormatConversionException: f != Integer` after **49 minutes** of completed computation. - **Before a study of any length, SMOKE-RUN it at toy sample counts in the snapshot.** Every prose path, every argmin and every serialisation runs in three minutes at 300 realisations instead of thirty-five at 10 000, and the only thing that does not transfer is the numbers. Two of `T-155`'s three reproduction failures at smoke scale were the Monte Carlo ones and were *expected*; the rest of the file was already correct.
- **`-PbuildDirectory` isolates `build-*/test-results` but NOT `build-*/classes`** — a concurrent agent's Gradle invocation can delete another's compiled classes mid-run, and a long `study` then dies with `ClassNotFoundException` on its own types. Run a multi-minute study from a snapshotted classes directory, or expect to lose the run.
- **`crossingOf` returns `NaN` bounds when a constraint is empty on its own** — there is no crossing to quote, one demand simply admits nothing — and `kotlinx.serialization` refuses to encode it. Detect it and report the emptying constraint instead.
- **A margin of `Infinity` is not a margin, it is the absence of a requirement.** A zero stability floor means no coupling stiffness is needed at all; record it as `null`, not as a number.
- **A tolerance THRESHOLD is not a slope at the origin, and the two can have opposite signs.** A 10 % attachment scatter *lowers* `C-0058`'s dishing (0.0767 → 0.0571) and only a ~35 % one loses the verdict, so a linearised tolerance budget gets the direction wrong. - **A symmetric matrix written symmetric is not a Maxwell-Betti test.** Verify reciprocity between two *different quadratures* — the tip translation under a unit tip moment (a double integration) against the tip rotation under a unit tip force (a single one). - **`private` does NOT scope a top-level Kotlin declaration to its file for the purposes of redeclaration** — two studies in one package each declaring `private data class LoadLine` fail `compileKotlin` with `Redeclaration` *and* a cascade of `Cannot access 'data class LoadLine': it is private in file` in the innocent one. - **An upstream result file may hold more than one record per state.** `gpd/results/T-3b-*.json` carries two solved profiles per `(concentration, gap)` — one per operating bias — so `firstOrNull { c && h }` silently takes whichever is listed first, at a bias the claim's own headline table does not use. - **`git add -A` during a parallel iteration commits tests without their implementations, by construction.** This project mandates TDD, so there is *always* a window in which a sibling agent's test exists and its main source does not — and the coordinator's `git add -A` captured exactly that window, pushing a `HEAD` that did not compile. Stage explicit paths, or run **`tools/verify.sh --committed`** before pushing: that flag exists (`P-10`) precisely to answer *"does the commit pass, independently of whatever the agents have half-written into the working tree"*, and skipping it is what let this through. Caught by an agent reporting it unprompted, not by the coordinator.
- **A subagent's chat report is not its artifact — read the filed claim, never the report.** Three times in one session a report and the claim it describes disagreed *materially*: `T-79` reported "the bracket is upheld, the two errors nearly cancel" under a claim ID that never existed, while the filed `C-0039` says the bracket **fails** and both errors run the **same** way; `T-113` reported a *soft* rim at 0.0999 where `C-0058` files a *stiff* rim at 0.0753; `T-121` gave two whole reports with the same claim and challenge IDs and different physics in each. The cause is benign — an agent reports, keeps working, and reports again — but the consequence is not: **a coordinator that writes up a report can commit a summary its own repository contradicts**, which happened once here and had to be corrected in a follow-up commit. The claim, the challenge and the result JSON are the artifacts; the report is a courtesy.
- **`pkill -f <pattern>` matches sibling agents' snapshots, not just your own.** `tools/verify.sh` and `tools/study.sh` name their copies `plenty-of-room-{verify,study}.$$`, so `pkill -f "plenty-of-room-study"` killed another agent's nine-minute run alongside the killer's. Match on the **PID**, or on the `$$`-suffixed directory name — never on the shared prefix. (This is the second form of the trap `CLAUDE.md` already records for killing your own shell.)
- **The agent scratchpad directory is shared between concurrently running agents**, so generic filenames (`first.json`, `run1.json`) collide silently — one such collision produced a false "not deterministic" verdict during a result-file check. Prefix scratch filenames with the task ID.
- **Re-listing `gpd/claims/` immediately before writing does NOT prevent a claim-number collision** — three agents took `C-0032` within 91 seconds of each other, each having listed an empty slot. The only rule that resolves without coordination is *the agent that notices moves*, and with three colliding it has to move **past** the next free number or it collides again. Report the renumbering; the coordinator can compact.
- **A claim number can be taken twice between one `ls gpd/claims/` and the next, and a sibling can RE-number onto you afterwards.** Three agents landed on `C-0032` in one session and one of them then moved to `C-0034`, colliding a second time. **Claim the number in `TASKS.md` first — that is the register the other agents read** — and treat a gap in the numbering as cheaper than a collision.
- A KDoc comment containing `h*/L₀` **terminates at the `*/`**. Write "`h* over L₀`" instead. It fired a second time on `x*/L`, one iteration later: **any starred symbol followed by a slash does it**, and the compiler reports twenty-odd "Expecting a top level declaration" errors pointing at a line *below* the comment, never at the comment.
- **`cosh(u)/sinh(u)` returns `NaN` above `u ≈ 20`** — both overflow, and the quotient does not fall back to the 1.0 it tends to. The **third** occurrence of this family of trap here (after the Brinkman drag and `1 − tanh(x)/x`); in a Langevin inversion it does not throw, it collapses the bisection silently onto its bracket floor.
- **A dense `n × n` matrix per element type is what turns a comfortable lattice solve into an OOM.** Gradle test workers default to ~512 MB, and at 1665 DOF five retained contribution matrices are 110 MB before the Cholesky factor. Assemble straight into one stiffness matrix and expose the contributions as *energies*. The JVM reports the failure as `NoClassDefFoundError` on classes that plainly exist — which reads exactly like the multi-agent build race and is **not** one.
- **A placement sweep is a BANK problem, not a solve problem.** Discrete anchors enter an `OrigamiGrillage` as *loads*, so the host factorises once: build `C-0058`'s `InfluenceSurrogate` over **every candidate station** of a lattice and **slice** it per candidate placement, and 1.1 million placements cost a 34 × 34 Cholesky each instead of an 855-DOF one (354 s for the lot). Superposition is exact, so the sliced surrogate must reproduce the assembled solve at departure **0.0** — assert that, or the whole sweep is on the wrong object.
- **An unused degree of freedom is a zero pivot, and Sylvester's criterion reports it as a MECHANISM.** Allocating a per-leg head-rotation DOF that is then merged away when its junction spring is infinite leaves an all-zero row, so an `LDLᵀ` buckling search returns `P_c = 0` for the *stiffest* case in the sweep — which reads exactly like the pinned-base mechanism it is not. Size the matrix from the DOFs the assembly actually uses.
- **A bisection on an `LDLᵀ` pivot sign has its own noise floor and it is above the discretisation error.** Exiting on a `1e-14` relative bracket leaves ~`1e-10` of scatter in the reported load, so a mesh refinement 32 → 64 can move the answer *upwards*. Assert monotonicity only while the discretisation error is above that floor, and assert settling below it.
- **Finite-element mesh monotonicity holds only on *nested* refinements.** Subdividing a beam 1 → 2 → 4 softens monotonically as the theorem says; a subdivision of 3 moves a point load from a node to mid-element and the deflection drops. Sweep 1/2/4, never 1/2/3/4.
- **A uniform load on a uniform Winkler foundation produces no dishing at all** — a free plate translates exactly, whatever its flexural rigidity, because `w = q/k_f` has zero fourth derivative and satisfies the free-edge conditions identically. If a plate-on-foundation solve reports dishing under a uniform load, the solver is wrong, not the physics. It makes an excellent falsifier; wire it in as a test.
- **The plate-on-foundation ripple transfer function `1/(1+(2πℓ/λ)⁴)` does not apply at a free edge** — it attenuated a rim perturbation 50× more than the finite-plate solve, because a free edge has no material beyond it to bend against. Interior non-uniformity only.
- **`Double` results are not reproducible across runs of the same JVM.** The JIT compiles a hot reduction part-way through a run, changing summation order and moving the last one or two ulp, so `gpd/results/*.json` differs on a re-run that changed nothing. Round the whole tree at the *serialisation boundary*, not at each construction site, so nothing can be emitted unrounded by omission.
- Comparing two quantities that are both meant to be zero **relatively** compares their noise — compare absolutely. (An odd Gauss-Legendre rule has a node at the origin, which is where this bites.)
- `1 − tanh(x)/x` loses every significant digit to cancellation below `x ≈ 1e-2`, which is exactly the free-film limit of the Brinkman transmissivity — use the series `x²/3 − 2x⁴/15 + 17x⁶/315` there.
- After upgrading the Gradle wrapper, `test` may fail with `NoSuchFileException: build/test-results/test/binary/in-progress-results-generic.bin`, because the results of the previous Gradle version are stale — delete `build/test-results` (or run `clean`) and retry.

## Research practice

- **A defect's LOCATION is a number like any other, and two records can agree with each other and both be wrong about it.** `C-0101` §4 and the `TASKS.md` row it raised both said the withdrawn-ceiling note was in `synthesis/DesiredStrokeReachStudy.kt` / `T-108`; it was in `anchoring/TwoSpringElasticaStudy.kt` / `T-79`, and `T-108` is where the correction had been *derived*. Grep the string out of the tree before accepting a filename from a claim — the same rule the project already applies to figures.
- **A delegated literature search is a summary, and a citation attached to it is not evidence that anything was read.** A subagent sent for Hamaker constants fabricated two of four sub-reports — inventing a textbook section number, a quoted sentence, a journal equation number and a screening formula with numbers — and retracted them only on a second pass. Ask for a *"read directly / abstract only / not found"* flag per number, and treat anything else as unsourced.
- **The finding can be a SCOPE clause rather than a number, and it is the one thing a keyword search will not surface.** The only published measurement of an origami crossover's interduplex roll (Snodin et al., *NAR* **47**:1585 — oxDNA, ≈ 8–9° average and ≈ ±16° per junction, digitised from its Figure 5) says in one clause of one sentence that it *"exclude[s] the outermost junctions on the tile, and the junctions next to the scaffold seam as well as the seam itself"* — i.e. exactly the sites the question was about. **Read the scope statement of the paper that has the right coordinate before concluding the quantity is unmeasured, and quote it: an explicit exclusion is far stronger evidence than a null search.** And check what the measured structure was *designed* to be — that tile is **twist-corrected** (a 31/32 bp domain mix), so its number is what remains when the driver has been designed out.
- **Checking `gpd/data/` first has now paid three times.** `T-182`'s single most load-bearing source was already in the repository, fetched by `T-151` for a different question two iterations earlier, and its decisive sentence was three paragraphs from a passage another claim had already quoted. `grep` the corpus before writing a fetcher.
- **A negative existence result over a SEARCH needs its expected yield, not just its budget.** Recording *"0 of 24 lattices"* is necessary and not sufficient: where the target is a conjunction of independent events, measure each event's marginal rate first and multiply. `C-0059`'s 24-lattice trio budget was worth **0.28** hits under its own marginals, so its null result carried no information at all — and the deepened search found the trio at every one of 21 configurations. **The marginal rate is cheap, it runs before the sweep, and a zero in it is a genuine proof of exclusion.**
- **A search over a continuum returns a DENSITY, and a density is not a converged number.** Refining the helical phase 45 → 90 → 180 steps takes the closing-lattice count 2 → 6 → 18. Existence is monotone under refinement and therefore safe; the count is a sampling statistic and every optimum found is an **upper bound**. Quote a search grid beside any "how many" — and expect a coarse enough grid to report an empty set.
- **A locally refined grid search does NOT dominate a coarser one, in either direction.** The natural argument — a finer grid can only improve a lexicographic optimum — is false once the grid *zooms* around its current best: 5 of 44 junction closures found at 60 steps / 4 refinements are missed at 180 / 6, and some go the other way. A test asserting that monotonicity passed on a three-point sample and had to be replaced by one that **measures** the disagreement.
- **Measure the cost of the inner loop before choosing the budget of the outer one.** One junction closure solve is 0.77 s; 32 assignments of donor end, polarity and pucker are solved and **only 12 % survive the reach bound that is already computed**, so pruning per assignment is a 17.2× speedup that is *exact* rather than heuristic. That arithmetic — done before any code was written — is the whole difference between a 24-minute study and a 30-hour one nobody would run.
- **A negative existence claim needs its query strings recorded, not just its conclusion.** ~110 queries across EuropePMC, arXiv and Crossref in nine named families is what makes "not established" falsifiable by one paper. And **the closest published precedent to a novel motif is often a *failure* buried in a supplement** — Rothemund's 2006 SI reports a protruding duplex marker that did not work and names the cause in one clause, *"attached to the origami by only one covalent bond"*.
- **A range quoted in an ABSTRACT is not a field, and the map is usually in the SI.** *"48 % on the edges to 95 % in the center"* reads as an edge value; on the 168-cell map of the same paper's Supplementary Fig. 14 the 48 % is the **single worst cell** and it is a **corner**, the **perimeter mean** is 77.5 %, and one corner (87.1 %) is *above* the interior mean. Two claims and a `CLAUDE.md` entry carried the abstract's phrasing for two iterations. **A transcription from a figure is checkable three ways and all three are cheap**: against the paper's own printed min/max/mean; against an internal identity the paper states (here panel *b* = panel *a* + 7 pp, on all 168 cells); and against the **quantisation the sample size imposes** — every cell is `k/186` because `n = 186` structures were imaged, and the mean distance to the nearest whole count is 0.067 at 186 against 0.23 at 185 or 187, a 3.5× discrimination that identifies the denominator and certifies that no digit was mis-read.
- **`pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC<id>/` is now reCAPTCHA-gated too** — it served 21 KB of Google challenge for `PMC5913233`, so the note below that the *new* PMC host serves the real text no longer holds. **For a Nature-family open-access paper go to the publisher**: `https://www.nature.com/articles/<doi-suffix>` served the full article and `https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A<encoded-doi>/MediaObjects/<id>_MOESM1_ESM.pdf` served the complete 23-page SI, both `200` to a bare `curl -sL`. Caltech's `dna.caltech.edu/Papers/` serves Rothemund 2006's main text but 404s its SI; the SI is on nature.com.
- **A subagent's ARITHMETIC about your own corpus needs the same check as its citations.** A literature agent that recovered a 168-value map correctly also reported that `CLAUDE.md`'s `σ_rel = √(f/(1−f))` is inverted. It is not — `√(f/(1−f))` gives the 43.6 % and 104 % that are published, and the inverted form gives 229 %. The check is one line of algebra and it is the same discipline as *"treat anything else as unsourced"*.
- **`fullTextXML` is no longer the reliable EuropePMC route — `https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC<id>?pdf=render` is.** Five consecutive `fullTextXML` requests returned 404 for articles whose full text exists; the `?pdf=render` URL served three of them with no cookie. PMC's own PDF endpoint now sits behind a SHA-256 proof-of-work challenge (`cloudpmc-viewer-pow`) with a reCAPTCHA fallback, so it is not a route at all.
- **A cited fit range is a number like any other, and it can be recovered when the paper does not state one.** The adopted PEG equation of state states no concentration range; its Fig. 1 caption names the source dataset, which the Wayback Machine still serves, and the real range is 1.5–67.5 wt % against the "0–50 wt %" in circulation. Cross-check it by measuring the paper's own figure against its axis. **Check the DOI too** — `C-0002` carried one that 404s at Crossref.
- **A scanned textbook has no text layer and `pdftotext` returns form feeds.** `pdftoppm -r 120` plus reading the page image is the only route, and the PDF page is the book page plus the front matter offset. Two printed equations were recovered that way and both are now executable tests.
- **EuropePMC's REST search returns HTTP 503 under rapid sequential querying**, and the unretried failure is an HTML error page that parses as a JSON error — which reads exactly like a zero-hit result. Sleep ~8 s between queries and retry.
- **When EuropePMC's `fullTextXML` returns zero bytes, try `https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC<id>/` — it serves the complete article HTML.** The *old* host `www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/...` answers with a reCAPTCHA challenge page that is 21 KB of valid HTML and parses as an article; the new host answers 200 with the real text. Three load-bearing quotations for `T-119` came that way from a paper EuropePMC reports as `isOpenAccess = N`.
- **A design RULE is a citable primary number and should be read, not recalled.** The square lattice's crossover geometry — four neighbours, planes at 8 bp, the same pair every 32 bp, 33.75°/bp against a preferred 34.3 — is stated verbatim in one paragraph of Ke et al. (2009); the honeycomb analogue (7 bp, two-thirds of a turn, 21 bp) is one paragraph of the caDNAno paper. A whole task's verdict rested on the difference between the two families, and both were free.
- **A duplex standing normal to a single-layer origami sheet is NOT an established motif.** Out-of-plane duplexes there are hairpin/staple-extension overhangs; perpendicular helices in origami are perpendicular *within* the plane (the crossover motif requires parallel helices, and the gridiron paper says so); **every published body standing on an origami plate is held by a PIN** (1–5 unpaired nt, or one base pair with flanking ssDNA). Where an out-of-plane element is held rigidly it is **triangulated** — "a set of double-helical spacers" — never clamped at a single junction. Check whether a proposed motif exists before pricing its stiffness.
- **A short duplex in axial compression HAS been measured, at exactly the Gen-1 length scale.** Fields et al. (*NAR* 41:9881) find buckling at 40–41 bp under 9 pN; inverting Euler gives `L_p = 41.7 nm`, inside the measured band and **25 % below CanDo's `EI = 230 pN·nm²`**. Any origami buckling load computed on CanDo's rigidity is the **optimistic** end by that factor.
- **A quantity absent from a main text is not a quantity that was not measured — look in the SI.** Three of the four load-bearing numbers in `T-134` were in supplementary tables their own papers never discuss, and one (`w_a`) is never defined in words anywhere; flag it *"read directly, meaning inferred"* and carry a rigorously defined companion beside it. **And a fit uncertainty on a peak POSITION is the standard error on the mean, ~20× smaller than the peak's WIDTH** — reading the one as the other is how a nine-per-cent lattice tolerance reads as a two-tenths-of-a-per-cent one.
- **PMC now gates file downloads behind a SHA-256 proof-of-work cookie** (`POW_CHALLENGE` + nonce, difficulty 4, cookie `cloudpmc-viewer-pow="<challenge>,<nonce>"`); solving it is ~10 ms in Python and it is the **only** route to several SIs. EuropePMC's `fullTextXML` returned **zero bytes** for five articles in one survey that are all fully readable on the PMC article page, and Edinburgh Research Explorer and Oxford ORA both `403` their PDFs to `curl` *and* to WebFetch despite being listed green OA.
- **Do not take a numeric result from a search-engine summary or from memory.** Download the paper and read the passage. A summary of Hansen et al. (2003) reported the des Cloizeaux onset as `φ# ≈ 0.04 / 0.025`; the paper says `0.15` and `0.07–0.09` — the summary had picked up the *overlap* concentrations instead. Acting on the summary would have inverted the conclusion of a whole iteration. `pdftotext -layout` on the arXiv PDF is the cheap, reliable route.
- **When a constant can be MEASURED from a public data bank, measure it rather than cite it.** The RCSB search and download APIs are open and unauthenticated, and one pass over 876 X-ray DNA-only entries reproduced the whole phosphodiester restraint library to ≤ 1.6° and ≤ 0.3 %, the canonical BI torsions to ≤ 7.1°, and supplied the primary measurement for a window this project had been citing from a source whose own references are textbooks. Generate the constants into source with a retained script (`tools/T-71-emit-kotlin-constants.py`) so nothing is transcribed by hand.
- **Check `gpd/data/` BEFORE fetching anything.** `T-161`'s entire literature answer — the design rule, the built precedent, that precedent's yield, and the software's own statement of what it permits — was four `grep`s on Rothemund 2006, its Supplementary Notes and the caDNAno paper, all fetched and manifested by `T-151` **two iterations earlier**. The twelve EuropePMC searches that followed added a recorded negative existence result and nothing else. A source manifest is an asset; the second task on a topic should cost minutes, and the cheap-bound discipline applies to reading as much as to arithmetic.
- **arXiv identifiers recalled from memory are unreliable — and so are PMCIDs; search for them, never guess.** A recalled `PMC2783486` for Ke et al. 2009 resolved to a paper on head-and-neck cancer screening; the correct `PMC2821935` came from a `TITLE:"..."` EuropePMC search (and then served no full text either, so the source is flagged **not found**). All four arXiv IDs recalled for the strong-coupling electrostatics literature resolved to unrelated articles; one was a paper on electricity-price risk management. Query `https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?...` with `curl -sL` (the `http` endpoint 301-redirects and returns an empty body without `-L`), then read the PDF with `pdftotext -layout`.
- **"Not open access" from Unpaywall or OpenAlex is not evidence that a paper is unobtainable.** Both reported a load-bearing 2012 paper as `closed` with no repository copy; the full published PDF is free from **NIST's public repository** because two coauthors are federal staff. Check the *authors'* institutions before declaring a body unreachable. Likewise an **empty** EuropePMC `fullTextXML` is not an absence of full text — one article returns zero bytes there and the complete text from the PMC article page.
- **Publisher sites are mostly closed; Crossref and EuropePMC are not.** ACS and Elsevier answer `403`, Springer redirects to an identity provider. `https://api.crossref.org/works/<doi>` returns a **verbatim abstract** for many older papers, and EuropePMC's REST search does the same for anything PubMed-indexed. An abstract read verbatim from Crossref is a citation and may be quoted as one; a search-engine summary of the same paper is not, and must be labelled differently.
- **When a coefficient cannot be sourced, deliver a ceiling and a threshold instead of a value.** The largest effect any member of the family reaches, plus the value the unknown would need for the answer to change, together decide the question without the missing number — and either half is falsified by a single published measurement. `P-6` closed this way after finding that the quantity it was sent for does not exist and is not even well posed.
- **A paywalled primary source is a reason to demote the number, not to quote the secondary.** Flag it unverified *in the code*, use it only as a cross-check against an independently derived bound, and make sure nothing changes if it is wrong. Where a publisher blocks the fetch with a reCAPTCHA, EuropePMC's REST full text often serves the same article: `https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC<id>/fullTextXML`.
- **Count a paper's strand table before inheriting a qualitative word from its prose.** Pumm et al.'s plates *"held rigidly … with a set of double-helical spacers"* — the programme's one citation for a rigid out-of-plane mounting — turns out to be **exactly two** spacers, 39 bp each, whose universal complements are **exactly 39 nt** and therefore carry no flank: **one covalent bond per end**, i.e. the ball joint Rothemund reports as a *failure*. The rigidity belongs to the **pair**, not to either joint. The count and the sequence lengths are in the SI; the arrangement is nowhere in the paper.
- Prefer **published measurement on the actual material** over simulating it. For `P-3` the "serious" method (MD/SCF for PEG's excluded volume) would have been *less* trustworthy than existing osmometry over the same concentration range, not merely more expensive. Say so explicitly in the Plan section — that is the cost justification NDI asks for.

### Keeping the synthesis honest

- **A synthesis drifts by keeping its ANSWERS, not by mis-copying its numbers.** The first end-to-end reconciliation of `ANSWERS.md` found **414 of 415** numeric tokens present in the claim corpus and **three** entries of its *"What we cannot answer, and why"* list that the programme had **answered** — one of them seven iterations earlier. **A deliverable that under-claims is as wrong as one that over-claims and is far harder to catch**, because a reviewer's instinct is to check the assertions and not the disclaimers. Re-read the *"still open"* list against the task queue every time a task closes.
- **A retained checker mechanises the half of the finding that was easy, and the half named as WORST gets left to memory.** `C-0067` concluded that the drift is in the **status** of answers rather than the value of numbers — and the tool it retained in the same hour checks **numbers**. Two iterations later the status class was back: of three places `ANSWERS.md` asserted a task was open, one had been closed by a claim in the previous iteration, with a substantive answer the deliverable was therefore not carrying. **A stale *"`T-129`, open"* contains no number, so no numeric check can ever reach it** — the register that knows is `TASKS.md`. Check it mechanically and run the check **unconditionally**: a check nobody remembers to ask for is not a check, which is the same sentence as the finding it was built from.
- **A hand-audit finds the contradictions that exist WHEN IT RUNS; the very next edit can make a new one, and a STATUS change is the class likeliest to do it.** `C-0080` found `ANSWERS.md` calling `T-45` answered in §1 and unmeasured in §3 — invisible to both existing checks, because one sentence has an owner and the other carries no number. Mechanised as a third check (`C-0088`), it immediately found a **second** task, `T-95`, created **ninety minutes earlier in the same iteration** by a discharge fix that updated one of two mentions. **A one-off reconciliation cannot converge on this class and a standing check can.**
- **Third consecutive iteration in which a check caught the mistake of the person who wrote it** — a headline list (`C-0078`), two `TASKS.md` rows added minutes before (`C-0083`), a half-finished discharge (`C-0088`). That is the argument for writing the check at all, and for running it **unconditionally**: the author is inside the population it is checking.
- **When a drift class cannot be mechanised, say what it would take and price the nearest approximation — do not defer it.** A *superseded* number reads `CITED` **because** it has an owner, so no corpus comparison can see it; the exact check needs a `superseded-by` edge at **statement** granularity that no claim carries, i.e. a corpus convention change. The cheap approximation (flag a number whose newest containing claim is not the cited one) would catch 3 of 5 known instances and is deliberately **not** shipped, because its false-positive rate is unmeasured — and an unmeasured false-positive rate is what makes a checker stop being believed.
- **A drift checker's FALSE positives cost more than its true ones, because the tool exists in order to be believed.** Three live traps in this repository's own files: *"Left undone"* contains `DONE` and *"cannot be answered"* upper-cases to `ANSWERED`, so a closing word must be matched **case-sensitively on whole words** (the queue writes verdicts in bold upper case and prose in lower); and *"`T-45`, open since iteration 3, is answered"* is **history**, not an assertion — an `open since|for|from`, or any answering word in the window, cancels it.
- **A number the synthesis got from a RUN rather than from the claim is undetectable by plausibility.** `ANSWERS.md` printed `C-0064`'s four device-range minimaxes as `0.0372 / 0.0436 / 0.0619 / 0.0500` against a claim that says `0.0373 / 0.0435 / 0.0620 / 0.0504` — and the first is a real number this repository produced, `C-0064` recording its optimum as a **manifold** with 0.0372 as the other equally-good member. All four are inside the acceptance convention either way, which is exactly why nothing caught it. **Round a claim's number in the synthesis only where the precision is not the content**: `C-0071`'s three unmargined quantities must be quoted at full width, because `C-0072`'s whole finding is that they are one arithmetic asserted equal to `1e−12`.
- **A range that is a min/max over one claim's own table is stated by no claim, and no `grep` can confirm it.** `"the peak per-load-path force is 3.9–8.9 pN"` is true and is assembled from four cells of `C-0016`; it is exactly the shape of number a synthesis manufactures without noticing it has become a source. Quote the construction beside it, or quote the cells.
- **A number's owner is not the claim cited nearest to it.** `ANSWERS.md`'s working-point layer stiffness sits under a table row citing three claims and belongs to a fourth (`C-0010`'s tangent at 100 pN). Proximity in a synthesis is not provenance.
- **Two claims can be one number, and only the RESULT FILES can see it.** `C-0091` audited six claims said to independently recommend a buffer and found **three**: two were the other three re-read. Each of the six states its route truthfully, so a synthesis reading *claims* is blind to it, and `tools/result-reader-census.py` is too — that finds dependencies flowing through **code**, and a transfer flows through a **person**. `tools/result-transfers.py` finds them; measured recall **2 of 2** against the instances found by hand.
- **An `==` between two emitted numbers is the wrong test for whether they are the same number.** The live instance had one file printing eight significant digits where the other printed nine — equality says *not a transfer*. Compare at the **emission** tolerance and **report the departure** rather than swallowing it.
- **The commoner shape of a transfer is a SUBSET, because a synthesis SELECTS the states it needs.** `T-2` quotes 12 of `T-3`'s 72 solved biases, under the same key name, at departure 0 — invisible to any equal-length comparison. Detecting it costs 260× the exact match (271 s against 1.0 s over 84 files) and returns 7× the output, which is why it is an audit instrument and not a gate.
- **A transfer is not a defect, so it must not be a gate.** It becomes one only where two files are **counted** as independent evidence, and no tool can see that. A gate that fires on a legitimate shared input — a foundation sweep two studies both consume — is turned off within an iteration.
- **A Markdown table row must be ONE physical line, which is the single place [semantic line breaks](https://sembr.org/) do not apply.** `ANSWERS.md`'s §3 row (f) — the longest answer in the primary deliverable — was written across six lines and had not been rendering as a table cell at all. Never break a row at a clause boundary, and check `|`-counts after editing a table.
- **And a bare `|` inside INLINE CODE splits a cell too — GFM splits the row before it parses backticks.** The only correct literal pipe is `\|`, which works inside a code span. A scan of 245 tracked files found **38** defects in **22**, in three kinds: a bare pipe in a body cell, a bare pipe in a **header** (which widens the whole table, so every *correct* body row becomes the odd one out and the writer scanning the rows finds nothing wrong with any of them — this was live in `C-0017`, the claim that owns the coupling mandate), and a genuinely missing cell. `tools/check-markdown-tables.py` checks it, 26 self-tests, exit 1 on any defect.
- **An invariant that forbids fixing something must be taught to the checker, or the checker decays into a warning.** `third-party/` is the problem definition as received and its table must keep its defect, so the sweep excludes it by default and an explicit path still overrides. A check that can never come back clean cannot be a gate — the same design decision as running a check *unconditionally* (`C-0078`): a check's **output discipline** is as much a choice as its logic.
- **`tools/verify.sh` cannot supply a suite test COUNT** — its `trap cleanup EXIT` deletes the snapshot, and `build/test-results` goes with it, so the log carries `BUILD SUCCESSFUL` and nothing to count. Run `./gradlew test -PbuildDirectory=build-<agent>` in the checkout if a claim needs the number; do not guess it from the previous claim's.
- **Before counting corroborating routes, DIFF THEIR RESULT FILES.** Two of the *"six independent routes"* recommending 0.5 mM were the other four read again: `T-2`'s bias clause carries `T-3`'s own blocking bias at **15 of 15** states at a departure of exactly **`0.0`**, and `T-25`'s buffer comparison carries `T-16`'s and `T-4`'s extrema at **20 of 20** at `2.7e−8` — which is one file printing eight significant digits where the other prints nine, so a `==` calls it *not* a transfer. **A synthesis that reads CLAIMS cannot see a transfer, because each claim is telling the truth about itself**; only the JSON shows that two of them are one number. Two independent derivations of the same physics would not agree to nine digits.
- **A buffer advantage — or any "X is better than Y" ratio — is a quantity that needs the state it is read at.** `C-0012`'s *"a factor of five better at 0.5 mM"* is the bias for 100 pN of **blocking** force, i.e. at **zero stroke**; at the held operating point the device occupies it is **1.48–1.57×**, an overstatement of 3.16–3.35×. Same discipline as quoting a stiffness with its compression, applied to a comparison rather than to a value.
- **A verdict that survives can survive on a different reason.** *"The adverse mounting fails, past the 40 pN/nm ceiling"* stayed true after `C-0049` withdrew that ceiling — because `C-0035` had independently shown the same mounting cannot place §3's effort point. Re-check the **ground** of every verdict whose premise is withdrawn, not just the verdict. **And the ground can move in the *favourable* direction and still have to be restated**: three of the four floors under `C-0071`'s knife edge stop firing at `CH-0089`'s measured girth, so *"the best element the catalogue contains and not a buildable design"* survives on a **weaker** ground than the one it was published on — which a synthesis reading only the verdict records as unchanged.
- **A SUPERSEDED STANDING VALUE reads `CITED`, and that is the third drift class.** `C-0067` found numbers in no claim, `C-0078` found tasks asserted open that the queue had closed, and both are now machine-checked at zero — but a number whose *owner still states it* and whose *later supersession lives in a different claim* passes both. Five in one pass (`C-0080`), all headline: `53×` for `68.9×`, a plan **rule**'s 0.2603 for a **search**'s 0.1670, a knife edge quoted against a convention nobody has measured, an *"is still unmeasured"* on a task the file's own §1 calls answered, and a *"requirement"* earned on a load line the recommendation does not use. **The question a tracer cannot ask is *"has a LATER claim superseded this?"*** — ask it per passage, in the range since the last synthesis.
- **A tool that checks a document against a corpus cannot see a document that disagrees with ITSELF.** `ANSWERS.md` §1 read *"`T-45` … is answered from published measurement — and the answer is a failure"* while §3 read *"(`T-45` is still unmeasured)"*, two hundred lines apart, **both halves tracing clean**: §1's sentence has an owner and §3's parenthesis carries no number at all, so the numeric half is silent, and *"unmeasured"* is not one of the phrasings the status half looks for. Self-consistency is a separate check, it is cheap because the deliverable is one file, and neither retained tool performs it.

- Do not add content to this file that is already discoverable by reading the source or build scripts — that inflates context without adding signal, reducing AI agent task success rates (see [arxiv 2602.11988](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988)).
