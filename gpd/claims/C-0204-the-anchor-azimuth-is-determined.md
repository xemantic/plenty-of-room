## The claim, in four lines

`C-0201` prices route B's raster turn at a **span**, and carries that span as an azimuth bracket
because `C-0147` and `C-0193` were bounding **reach** — where a bracket is the right instrument.

For a mechanical **element** it is not, and the cost was the whole verdict: the bracket straddles
`T-5b` on the free tile, `24 of 36` corners flat.

**The span is a lattice arithmetic.** A chain leaves a helix at the phosphate of its last paired
base, and a phosphate's azimuth is fixed by its base-pair index and the lattice's own phase.

Derived, it is one number, and at it the free tile is flat everywhere.

## 1. The cheap bound ran first and it is the whole answer

No solver appears in the primary deliverable. The residues are
`HoneycombRasterResidues.reducedResidues`, already in the tree; `b₀` is
`HoneycombRasterTurnSigns.classZeroResidue`, which **refuses** rather than guessing on a raster
that does not close; the azimuth is one subtraction and one multiplication; and the span is
`forcedCrossoverSpan`, which `T-246` already tests. **Fifty-nine turns, eight datum readings,
microseconds.**

The closed form, and its reference is `5.25` rather than `5`:

> `θ(ρ, b₀) = fold((ρ − b₀ − 21/4) · 240/7°)`, &nbsp;&nbsp;
> `span(θ) = √(d² − 4 d r_P cos θ + 4 r_P²)`

caDNAno's rule is *"five base pairs, **or half a turn**"* and the half turn at 10.5 bp/turn is
**5.25** bp (`C-0152` §5, `CH-0197`). Because `10.5 bp` is exactly `360°`, `b₀ + 5.25` and
`b₀ − 5.25` are **one** azimuth, so the reference is single-valued — and the form reproduces
`C-0187`'s `scaffoldDisplacementDepartureDegrees` at **both** allowed residues at **all 21**
lattice constants, exactly. That agreement is a named test, and a mutation replacing `21/4` by
caDNAno's integer `5` fails **ten** of them.

## 5. The anchor offset is a DESIGN, and one rung of it lands exactly on `C-0201`'s worst corner

The recommended raster's `102 / 109` are **paired** row lengths and route B's unpaired loop sits
**outboard** of the duplex. That is what `C-0200` reads off the built block — the duplex over the

the row is that many base pairs of duplex"*). So the last paired base sits **at** the raster level.
