## Formulate

### The question, stated exactly

`C-0084` finds the recommended element — `C-0069`'s `Q5`, 34 hinge-rooted elastica arms of 8.16439083 nm —
has **no pull-in fold at 2 mM at 0 of 6** layer models, and bounds its own negative:

> the recommended arm is inextensible and `C-0039`'s shooting solve enumerates only the small-rotation branch,
> so the path is truncated at **7.9097 nm** of stroke, and *"no fold"* means *"no fold below 7.9097 nm"*.

**Does a fold exist above it?** Three sub-questions, and they have different costs:

- **`P1`** — is there a stroke above 7.9097 nm at all? The element must supply a reaction there for an
  equilibrium to exist, let alone a fold.
- **`P2`** — is `C-0084`'s 7.9197 nm *refusal* a property of the elastica or of the solver that reads it?
- **`P3`** — over whatever domain the element does have, does `V_eq(s)` turn over at the recommended device?

An honest **"the branch cannot be enumerated"** is an acceptable answer to `P3` provided `P1` is settled,
because a stroke that does not exist cannot carry a fold.

### Acceptance predicates

- **`A1`** — a bound on the stroke that holds on **every** branch of the elastica, at every tip force, with
  no solver and no convergence parameter. Stated as a proof, not a scan.
- **`A2`** — an enumeration of the shooting residual's roots at a declared set of forces, reporting per root
  the stroke `δ`, `max_s|φ|` and the first-integral spread, so a reader can see which branch a state is on.
- **`A3`** — the largest stroke at which the branch continuously connected to the unloaded state still closes,
  with its own **measured** validity limit rather than an assumed one.
- **`A4`** — either a fold in `(7.9097 nm, A3]` at the recommended device, or the statement that there is none,
  with the residual window `A1` leaves unexplored quoted as a length.
- **`A5`** — a verdict on `C-0084`'s 7.9197 nm and `CH-0099`'s *"element model branch end"* ceiling: is the
  **value** right, the **candidate** right, both, or neither?

### Units, conditions and conventions — restated, not inherited

- Lengths **nm**, forces **pN**, stiffness **pN/nm**, rotational stiffness **pN·nm/rad**, rotation **rad**,
  potential **V**, concentration **mM**; `k_BT = 4.141947 pN·nm` at **300 K** in aqueous MgCl₂.
- **`C-0039`'s geometry, unchanged.** Arc length `s ∈ [0, L]` from the hinge; `φ(s)` the tangent angle from
  the undeformed axis toward the stroke direction; `x(s) = ∫cos φ`, `z(s) = ∫sin φ`;
  `EI φ″ = −F cos φ`, `EI φ′(0) = k_n φ(0)`, `EI φ′(L) = −k_f φ(L)`.
- **The stroke of one arm is `δ = z(L)`**, and `δ = r sin θ` rather than `r θ` (`CH-0040`).
- **The stroke `s = L₀ − h`** is positive downward; a **load line** `R(s)` is positive upward, in pN over the
  whole 34-arm array; `C-0017`'s 33.3333 pN/nm is a **sum**.
- The **recommended device** is the 10 nm layer at `σ` = 0.024 nm⁻², placed at 100 pN over §3's acceptable
  3 nm stroke, in 0.5 and 2.0 mM (`C-0071`, `C-0068`, `C-0084`).
- **A fold is `max_s V_eq(s)`**, and at it `k_c(s) + k_eff(s) = 0` exactly.

## Plan

### The cheap bound, and it settles `P1` outright

`δ = z(L) = ∫₀^L sin φ(s) ds ≤ L`, with equality only if `φ ≡ π/2` almost everywhere. But `φ ≡ π/2` forces
`φ′ ≡ 0`, and the near-end condition `EI φ′(0) = k_n φ(0)` then gives `φ(0) = 0 ≠ π/2` for any finite
`k_n > 0`. **So `δ < L` strictly, on every branch, at every force, at every rotation.**

`L = 8.16439083 nm`, so **no equilibrium — and therefore no fold — exists at any stroke at or above the
contour.** The open window is `(7.9097, 8.16439083)`, of width **0.2547 nm**: 8.5 % of §3's acceptable
stroke and 3.1 % of the arm's own length.

**This runs before any code and it converts an unbounded question into a bounded one.** It also fixes what
the expensive half has to do: not *"search for a fold at large stroke"* but *"close a quarter-nanometre"*.

### `P2` — the refusal, and why it is worth checking before enumerating anything

`C-0084` reads its ceiling off `TwoSpringElastica.forceForDisplacement`, which **doubles** a tip force from
`0.5 δ k_small` until the stroke reaches its target, and off `stateAtForce`, which brackets the shooting
parameter by **doubling from a seed** and then runs Illinois inside whatever bracket that produces. Both are
correct while the residual has one root. `C-0039`'s own KDoc says the residual stops being monotone past a
right angle, and reconnaissance shows a second root appearing **well before** that. A doubling ladder that
steps over the last single-root force does not report a branch end; it reports having lost the branch.

So the first thing to measure is whether the refusal is a property of the elastica. It costs one scan.

### The method

1. **Enumerate**, at a declared ladder of tip forces, **every** sign change of the far-end moment residual on
   a declared shooting range, and integrate each root: `δ`, `max_s|φ|`, the first-integral spread and the
   moment-balance residual. That is the multi-branch elastica `C-0084` says a shooting solve cannot supply.
2. **Continue** the branch connected to the unloaded state by marching the **near-end rotation** `φ0` upward
   in small steps and taking, at each, the **first** sign change of the residual in the tip force above the
   previous one — `CLAUDE.md`'s *"scan for the first sign change and bisect on that bracket"*, applied along
   a branch rather than across one. Stop where the **first integral** stops being conserved to a declared
   tolerance: a measured limit, not a ladder artefact.
3. **Re-run `C-0018`'s equilibrium path** with a load line that answers over the extended domain, at the
   recommended device — 10 nm, 0.5 and 2.0 mM, six `C-0003` models — and look for a fold.
4. **Report the residual window** `A1` leaves: `L` minus the deepest stroke step 2 reaches.

**Cost justification.** Step 1 is `O(scan × forces)` RK4 sweeps, microseconds each; step 2 is a few thousand
sweeps; step 3 is 12 states × ~50 path points × ~42 Poisson-Boltzmann solves, which `C-0084` measured at
7.6–8.1 ms each — about 4 minutes. The alternative, an oxDNA or all-atom treatment of a 24 bp arm, answers a
different question and costs days. **And the cheap bound has already removed the unbounded half**, so the
expensive half is spent on 0.25 nm rather than on an open interval.

### What would falsify this approach

- **`F1` — the refusal being real.** If the branch genuinely ends near 7.92 nm — the residual losing its root
  rather than the ladder losing the branch — then `C-0084` and `CH-0099` stand as written and the answer to
  `P3` is *"the branch cannot be enumerated past there"*, which is the acceptable negative.
- **`F2` — a fold in the extension.** If `V_eq(s)` turns over anywhere in `(7.9097, A3]`, then `C-0084`'s
  *"no fold"* is not merely bounded but **wrong at some model**, and §6 task 4's discharge weakens.
- **`F3` — a branch reaching past the contour.** Impossible by `A1`; if the enumeration reports one, the
  integrator is wrong and every number in `C-0039`, `C-0069`, `C-0071` and `C-0084` is in question. It is the
  strongest falsifier available here precisely because it cannot fire.
