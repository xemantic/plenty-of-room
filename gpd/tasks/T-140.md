## The problem, restated

`C-0076` established a congruence: `C-0055`'s upward roots sit on planes `k ≡ 2b+3 (mod 4)`, odd
for every duplex, while the weave's extrema are the crossover planes, which are even — so on a
triangular wave **all 34 of `C-0063`'s stations sit on a node**, at all 32 phases, worst departure
`4.4e−16 nm`, and **independently of the amplitude**. That annihilates the 1.2–1.75 nm amplitude
bracket three sources disagree over.

The congruence is exact for a **periodic** weave whose extrema are the crossover planes, and
`C-0076` says so and names its own counter-case: Snodin reports that a Rothemund rectangle's
scaffold seam gives one group of helix pairs *"a particularly large section without any junctions"*
which *"opens up to the largest extent here"*. **That is precisely the asymmetric case in which a
triangular wave's node stops sitting midway, and this programme's sheet model has no seam at all.**

## Numeric target and acceptance predicate

**Acceptance** (as registered in `TASKS.md`): *a weave phase model carrying the scaffold seam,
checked against `C-0063`'s 34 stations — or the statement that no station lies within one weave
period of a seam.*

**Falsifiable predicates.**

1. **P1 — the seam model reduces to `C-0076`'s exactly.** With no seam the model must reproduce
   `WeaveProfile.distanceAtPlane` and `axisOffsetAtPlane` to `1e−12` at every interface, including
   the halved-amplitude edge reading. A model that does not is a different weave, and nothing it
   says about the seam is inherited.
2. **P2 — the departure at a station is measured, not asserted.** For every seam position the tile
   admits, the interhelical distance at each of a station's two bounding interfaces and the host
   duplex's own axis offset are reported, against the zero of `C-0076`'s congruence.
3. **P3 — the amplitude dependence is reported.** `C-0076`'s headline is that the amplitude
   bracket has coefficient zero. If a seam gives it a non-zero coefficient, the bracket is
   **restored** at the affected stations and the three literature amplitudes must be quoted there.
4. **P4 — the plan-margin coefficient is re-derived, not inherited.** `C-0076`'s categorical
   argument (`M = p − d − L` charges an *along*-helix gap between *unbonded* bodies; the weave is
   an *across*-helix separation) makes no reference to the weave's phase, so a seam cannot move it.
   That must be asserted as a test rather than repeated as prose.
5. **P5 — the count of affected stations is a function of the seam's position, and the position is
   a design variable this programme has never fixed.** The sweep must be exhaustive over the
   integer coordinate, and it must say what the Rothemund convention (a seam in the middle) gives.

## Conventions, fixed before deriving

- Lengths **nm**; `x` **along** the helices, `y` **across** them, `z` normal, positive upward.
- A **plane index `k`** is `C-0055`'s 8 bp crossover plane, `x = phase·rise + k·8·rise`.
  **Interface `b`** lies between duplex `b` and duplex `b+1`.
- Duplex `b` crosses to `b+1` at planes `k ≡ 2b (mod 4)` and to `b−1` at `k ≡ 2b+2 (mod 4)`; both
  are even. Its **upward** sites are `k ≡ 2b+3 (mod 4)`, odd for every `b` (`C-0055`).
- **A seam is a plane index.** Snodin: *"the origami's seam (a series of junctions where the
  scaffold strand is exchanged), which runs along the middle of the tile"* — a column
  perpendicular to the helices. On this lattice, "the junctions at plane `s` are absent" is one
  integer, and it removes a crossover from **every** duplex, because at any even plane every
  duplex participates in exactly one interface crossover.
- **The weave is a chain of pull events.** Duplex `b` is displaced `+Δ/4` toward `b+1` at each
  plane `k ≡ 2b (mod 4)` and `−Δ/4` toward `b−1` at each `k ≡ 2b+2 (mod 4)`, straight in between —
  which is Snodin's own *"the bending … is mostly localized at the junctions with the intervening
  sections basically straight"*. This reproduces `C-0076`'s closed-form triangular wave exactly,
  and it is the representation a seam can be expressed in at all.
- **Sign**: a positive axis offset is toward `+y`; a positive interface departure is an *opening*.

## Plan — the cheap bound first, and what would falsify the approach

**The cheap bound is integer arithmetic and it does most of the work.** Removing all crossovers at
plane `s` removes exactly one pull event from every duplex, so between `s−2` and `s+2` every duplex
is *straight* at `±Δ/4` — its sign set by whether the event it lost was the `+` or the `−` one, and
that is a parity of `2b − s (mod 4)`. Therefore every interface is pinned at an extremum over that
window: alternate interfaces at `mean + Δ/2` and at `mean − Δ/2`. **The stations at planes `s±1`
are exactly the ones inside the window**, so the answer needs no solve at all — only a count, and
the sweep of `s`.

That the two interface groups come out **opened** and **closed** is not put in by hand, and it is
the model's cross-check against the source: Snodin reports one group *"opens up to the largest
extent"* and *"the other group … opens up much less"*. Nothing in the construction forces that.

**Why not oxDNA.** Simulating a seamed tile would cost days and would answer a question the lattice
already answers exactly, because the *positions of the extrema* — the only thing the congruence
depends on — are set by where the junctions are, which is a design input and not a result.

**What would falsify the approach.**

- If the event model does not reproduce `WeaveProfile` at zero seams, the two are different weaves
  and P1 fails.
- If the departure at a station turns out to depend on the seam's *width* or on a relaxation length
  rather than only on its position, the lattice reading is insufficient and an elastic model is
  needed. The measurable signature is that Snodin's Figure 3 shows *modulation* inside the opened
  region where this model predicts a plateau; the plateau **level** is what the station check uses,
  and modulation can only reduce the departure, so the model is the conservative reading — but if
  the answer turned on the difference, it would have to be solved rather than bounded.
- If the seam turns out not to be expressible as a missing junction column — if it is a different
  motif with its own crossover pattern — then the phase model is wrong in kind. Snodin's own
  parenthesis (*"a series of junctions where the scaffold strand is exchanged"*) is the check.
