## Formulate

Six claims and five challenges have landed since `C-0121`, and that pass closed with a specific instruction:

> §3 row (g) now carries the entire history of the flatness question — its reversals, its withdrawn readings
> and its four re-openings — in a single table cell. The history being visible in one place is deliberate and
> valuable; the row is nonetheless the passage **most in need of a rewrite rather than another append**.

### Acceptance predicate

1. Every item in `C-0122`–`C-0128` / `CH-0147`–`CH-0151` is partitioned, with a reason per row.
2. The cheap bound is derived from git, not typed.
3. **Row (g) is rewritten**: it states the current answer first, and its derivation history is **preserved
   verbatim** rather than deleted.
4. All five retained checkers run before and after, and both readings are quoted.

**Falsifier.** If the rewrite loses anything — a withdrawn reading, a reversal, a number a later claim
depends on — it is a deletion wearing a rewrite's clothes, and the append was the better option.

## Plan

**Cheap bound first**, from `git show <base>:ANSWERS.md`: how many of the eleven the deliverable cited before
the pass, and **how long row (g) actually is**, since that is the quantity the rewrite is judged on.

**The rewrite's method is the whole of it.** A deliverable whose answer has reversed twice owes a reader
**two different things in two different places**: the current answer, stated plainly at the top; and the
reversals, which are the most instructive material in the document and must not be lost. So the row states
the answer in five numbered steps and the cell as it stood is quoted **verbatim, unedited**, below the table.

**Justification against cost.** It is a read and an edit. Against that: a reader wanting to know whether the
tile is flat currently has to reconstruct it from a chronological accretion in which the standing verdict
reversed twice — which is the failure mode a synthesis exists to prevent.

**What would falsify the approach.** That the history cannot be separated from the answer — that the current
verdict is only intelligible in sequence. It is not: the answer is five statements about a four-layer tile,
and the sequence is why anybody believes them.
