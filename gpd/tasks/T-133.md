## Conventions, restated rather than inherited

- Lengths **nm**, forces **pN**, stiffness **pN/nm**, rotational stiffness **pN·nm/rad**, angles
  **degrees** in every reported number and radians in code;
  `k_BT = 4.141947 pN·nm` at **300 K** in aqueous **2 mM MgCl₂**.
- **Plan.** `x` runs **along** the host sheet's helices, `y` **across** them, origin at the tile
  centre. `z` is normal and positive **upward**, away from the grafted layer; §1's bias pulls the
  tile **down**.
- **A duplex in plan is a rectangle of width `d = 2.69 nm`** — the SAXS single-layer interhelical
  distance — so two parallel duplexes at exactly `d` are **tangent and admissible**. `C-0041`'s
  and `C-0053`'s convention verbatim; the 2.0 nm steric and the 2.73 nm square-lattice readings
  are swept.
- **A rooted element occupies `[root, root ± L]` and the next one along the same row may start at
  `high + d`** — `C-0053`'s footprint convention, carried unchanged in `armDirections`. It is the
  convention that turns the 10.88 nm root pitch into an **8.19 nm** length ceiling, and the
  zero-gap reading is swept.
- **The stations are `C-0063`'s**, read from `gpd/results/T-125-upward-root-placement.json`: 34
  upward (`EAST`) roots at crossover phase **24** on a 15-duplex, 40.0 × 40.35 nm sheet, four rows
  of three and eleven of two, root pitch **10.88 nm** within a row and **5.44 nm** between
  adjacent rows.
- **One load path is one element**, and the coupling is `C-0017`'s **33.3333 pN/nm** as a **sum**,
  so a 34-path element is placed on a **per-path secant of `33.3333/34 = 0.980392 pN/nm` at §3's
  acceptable 3 nm stroke**. Placement is on the **secant** and stability on the **tangent**
  (`C-0017`), and each is read over the strokes the device traverses, `[0, 3 nm]` (`C-0049`).
- **"Lies in the plan"** means the element's compliant member is a body whose long axis is
  parallel to the sheet, so that its length is charged against the sheet's own plan. An element
  *out of* the plan is one whose compliant member runs along `z`, or is folded, or is nested at a
  second level.

## The question, made precise

`C-0065` composed `C-0062`'s truss trios with `C-0063`'s placement and found **all 44 of 44 place
34 times, 0 overlaps, one level** — and then reported, apart, that the **flexure those trusses cap
does not follow**: at `C-0030`'s 34-path span of **27.41 nm** the same array covers **1.84×** the
footprint, needs **7 levels** and places **12 of 34**. That is `C-0041`'s standing negative — 45
flexures do not pack, the tile carries 15 — re-measured at 34 paths on a real placement, and it is
independent of every trio.

Every coupling element this programme has priced lies in the plan: `C-0023`'s transverse duplex
flexure (`E3`), its crossover-hinge flexure (`E5`), `C-0030`'s coupled-standoff flexure,
`C-0039`'s two-spring elastica arm. The question is whether the element space contains anything
else — and, if it does not, whether the plan is really what refuses the flexure.

## Plan — the cheap bounds first, and what would falsify the approach

**The whole search is a length-for-stiffness question, and it is closed form in every mechanism.**
DNA offers exactly the compliances `C-0023` names — entropic (axial, one-sided) and bending
(signed) — plus the two ways a bending member can be *used*: rotated at a hinge on a lever, or
bent along its own span. Each has a closed-form length at a given stiffness, so the element space
can be swept before any packer runs.

| | bound | why it is cheap | what it settles |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **the row-of-three ceiling.** `34 = 3a + 2(15 − a)` forces `a = 4` rows of three (`C-0063`'s bound 1), and three roots on a 10.88 nm lattice cap a rooted element at `pitch − d` | two subtractions | **the plan budget of any rooted element, over every placement on the upward lattice and not only `C-0063`'s** — 8.19 nm |
| **B** | **the length each mechanism demands** at `k₁ = 0.980392 pN/nm`: `S/k` axial, `3k_BT/(k b)` entropic, `√(k_θ/k)` hinge, `(c EI/k)^(1/3)` bending, `GJ/(k r²)` torsional | one closed form each | which mechanisms are inside bound A at all |
| **C** | **the two-support flexure's own floor.** Its `c` is `48 … 192` (`C-0025`), so its shortest possible span is `(48 EI/k₁)^(1/3)` | one cube root | whether *any* end condition or mounting rescues `C-0023`'s `E3` / `C-0030` family. If the floor is past bound A the family is refused **at every span and on every placement**, which is a strictly stronger statement than `C-0065`'s 12 of 34 |
| **D** | **the normal direction.** A member standing along `z` and loaded along `z` is loaded **axially**, so its length is `S/k₁` | one division | whether "out of the plan, along the surface normal" is available at all, against `C-0017`'s 10 nm envelope |
| **E** | **the fold.** A fold trades length along a row for width across the rows; the along-row slack is `pitch − L` and the **across-row pitch is exactly one duplex** (`C-0041`'s Fact A) | one comparison | whether folding or serpentining can buy anything, before any junction is priced |

**The expensive part is warranted only where a mechanism lands inside bound A**, and it is then
two things and no more: the **placement** of that element's 34 instances through `C-0053`'s own
packer on `C-0063`'s stations, and the **clauses** — `C-0017`'s placement equality on the secant,
its stability floor on the tangent read over `[0, 3]` (`C-0049`), the 10 pN per-path unzip
allowable and `C-0049`'s `n·a/s` secant ceiling, `C-0023`'s two-sidedness evaluated at negative
argument, and `CH-0037`'s buckling duty taken from the element's own law wherever the element owns
a compression member.

**The free limiting case, declared before the run:** an in-plane beam candidate must reproduce
`C-0041`'s and `C-0065`'s negative — `C-0030`'s coupled flexure at 34 paths must come out at
**27.41 nm**, **7 levels** and **12 of 34** through `C-0065`'s own `placeTrussArray`.

**What would falsify this approach:**

1. **Bound A coming out above the two-support flexure's floor**, i.e. the plan admitting a
   22.6 nm element. Then the flexure is refused by `C-0063`'s particular placement rather than by
   the lattice, and the task becomes a placement search rather than an element search.
2. **A mechanism outside `C-0023`'s two** — a compliance in DNA that is neither entropic nor
   bending. Then the closed-form census is incomplete and the cheap bound cannot decide anything.
3. **The elastica refusing to place any admissible arm**, i.e. every `(k_near, k_far)` pair whose
   small-rotation length is inside bound A having an exact length outside it. `C-0039` shows the
   exact solve always *stiffens*, so the exact length is always **longer** than the
   small-rotation one — the correction runs the wrong way and it has to be measured, not assumed.
4. **The 34-instance placement of an admissible element failing at one level.** Then the element
   space is empty at 34 paths and the answer is the negative half of the predicate.

**What this task does NOT do.** It does not re-open `C-0063`'s placement (a different placement
re-solves `C-0055`'s self-consistent 34 and everything downstream), it does not price staple
routing or yield, and it does not adjudicate `C-0055`'s open item 2 — the arm's rotation axis is
taken **across** its own length at the root, exactly as `C-0066` states it, because under the
other reading the element delivers no stroke and there is nothing to place.
