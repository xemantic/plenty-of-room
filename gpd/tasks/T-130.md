# T-130 — Do `C-0062`'s closing trio lattices PLACE 34 times?

## Formulate

### The question

`C-0062` searched **one crossbar at a time**.
It enumerated 113 040 lattices, solved 49 857 of them and found **609** fully closing trios —
two leg heads and a flexure end, six covalent links, on one lone crossbar duplex —
at **every one of the 21 admissible `(crossbar length, row pitch)` configurations**.

A Gen-1 device needs **34** of them (`C-0055`'s self-consistent count on the upward azimuth),
at the placement `C-0063` found flat (phase 24, dishing **0.0706** of the free-tile stroke, equal springs).

> **Does a trio that closes in isolation survive being repeated 34 times on `C-0063`'s placement?**

Three things a single-crossbar search cannot see, and each of them is a *composition*:

1. **Register.** A trio is a property of the crossbar and its two leg *heads*.
   A leg also has a **base**, and a base can only sit where the host duplex's own backbone offers one.
   `C-0059`'s and `C-0062`'s base misalignment floors are **minima over the base's axial position on the host duplex** —
   and an array **pins** that position, at 34 stations at once.
2. **Plan.** Each instance occupies plan area — two legs, a crossbar above them, and the flexure the third junction caps —
   and must clear its neighbours' at the pitch `C-0063`'s placement actually has.
3. **Sharing.** 34 instances draw on one sheet's phosphates, one scaffold and one set of stations.

### Acceptance predicate

**`Q1` — a number.** How many of `C-0062`'s closing trios place **34** times on `C-0063`'s placement, at which phase;
and if none does, **what count is reachable**, with the binding constraint named.

**`Q2` — the composition is real.** The verdict is computed on `C-0053`'s own plan-view packer and on
`C-0059`'s own base-junction closure, both re-run as libraries, with `C-0062`'s and `C-0063`'s result files
**consumed as data** and their published numbers re-checked and any departure quoted.

**`Q3` — a torsion closure is a NECESSARY condition only.** Every *"places"* verdict here is an **upper bound**
on buildability, exactly as `C-0057`, `C-0059` and `C-0062` all say; nothing is measured.

**`Q4` — the free limiting cases.** A **single** instance must reproduce `C-0062`'s closure exactly,
and **collapsing the trio to a bare root** (no crossbar, no separation) must reproduce `C-0063`'s placement exactly.

**`Q5` — what the array costs the flatness.** If the register moves the stations,
`C-0063`'s flatness pipeline is re-run **on the moved stations** and the dishing quoted against `T-5b`'s 0.10.

### Units, geometry and sign conventions — restated, not inherited

- Lengths **nm**, forces **pN**, stiffness **pN/nm**, angles **degrees** in every reported number, radians in code.
  T = 300 K, `k_BT = 4.141947 pN·nm`, aqueous **2 mM MgCl₂**.
- `x` runs **along** the host sheet's helices, `y` **across** them, `z` **normal and positive upward** —
  away from the grafted layer, which lies below the tile. Origin at the tile centre.
- **A duplex in plan is a rectangle of width `d = 2.69 nm`** (SAXS, single-layer) — `C-0041`'s and `C-0053`'s convention verbatim,
  so two parallel duplexes at exactly `d` are **tangent and admissible**; the 2.0 nm steric reading is swept.
- **One truss instance** is `C-0048`'s cap assembly: two legs standing along `+z` from **one** host duplex
  at `x_root ∓ w/2`, `w = ` row pitch × 0.34 nm; one crossbar duplex of `crossbar × 0.34 nm` along `x̂`,
  its axis one duplex radius above the leg heads, offset by the trio's own `axialPhase` in `x` and `lateralSeat` in `y`.
  In plan it is **one rectangle with two vertical members** — `C-0053`'s `PlanElement` exactly,
  and the crossbar always covers both legs because `C-0048`'s minimum crossbar is `row + 6` bp.
- **The flexure is carried as a SEPARATE reading**, not folded into the headline:
  it is a body of `C-0030`'s span running along `−ŷ` from the crossbar's midpoint, and
  `C-0041` has already refused an array of those at 45 paths. Both readings are reported.
- **A station is where the coupling enters the sheet.** For `C-0063` that is one upward crossover;
  for a truss it is **two leg bases**, one per leg, each carrying half the path's stiffness.
- **The upward (`EAST`) azimuth is `C-0055`'s**: `24 bp` from a duplex's `NORTH` plane, pointing `+z`,
  with duplex `b`'s azimuth class at plane `k` equal to `(k − 2b) mod 4` — so **every** station of **every** row
  sits at the identical helical phase of its **own** host duplex.

## Plan

### The cheap bounds, which run before the composition

| | bound | what it settles | falsifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **the plan area of 34 truss blocks** against the 1614 nm² footprint | whether the question is an area question at all (`C-0053`'s bound 1 in a new place) | the ratio exceeding 1, which would close the task in a line |
| **2** | **the block's plan span `crossbar + d`** against the station pitch **10.88 nm** | the along-row packing, before any packer runs | a span above the pitch |
| **3** | **the helical phase class census of the 34 stations** | **the shape of the whole answer.** Every station is an `EAST` site of its own duplex, and adjacent rows' duplexes are phase-shifted by exactly the 16 bp their sites are offset by — so there is **one** class, the register test is **one** test, and the placed count is quantised at **0 or 34** | two or more classes, which would cap the count at the largest class |
| **4** | **the flexure reading's plan area** at `C-0030`'s span and 34 paths | whether the full element can place **at one level** at all, independently of any trio | a ratio below 1, which would re-open `C-0041` |
| **5** | **the base-junction closure density** on one 32 bp period of the host | how far a truss has to move off its station to root at all — the size of `δ` before any flatness is solved | a period with no closing position, which refuses the array outright at that row pitch |

**Bound 3 is the method.** It is pure lattice arithmetic on `C-0055`'s own azimuth rule and it is what makes the
expensive part affordable: because all 34 stations are one phase class, the register question is asked **once**
per row pitch rather than 34 times, and the answer is a **rigid translation `δ`** of the whole array — which is
then priced on `C-0063`'s own flatness pipeline.

### The method

1. **Consume and re-check.** `C-0062`'s trios and `C-0063`'s 34 roots read from their result files;
   their published counts, chords and dishings re-derived from their own classes and every departure quoted.
2. **The five cheap bounds**, as above, each with its falsifier declared.
3. **The register.** `C-0059`'s single-junction feasible set and `C-0062`'s pruned closure verdict, re-run as libraries,
   over one 32 bp period of a host duplex at 0.17 nm steps: which axial positions carry a torsion-closing 90° base,
   and hence which **pair centres** at each row pitch 6–12 bp do. The offset `δ` from the `EAST` station follows,
   under **both** strand readings of "the upward site", and the whole periodic map is reported so that the
   verdict does not depend on the convention.
4. **The array.** 34 instances of every recorded closing trio placed at `C-0063`'s phase-24 stations translated by `δ`,
   through `C-0053`'s `elementPackingVerdict` — overlaps, mutual blocks, level-independent member clashes, levels required.
5. **The flatness of what places**, on `C-0063`'s own grillage under `C-0022`'s solved load, at the **68** realised
   entry points rather than 34 nominal ones, against `T-5b`'s 0.10.
6. **The flexure reading**, reported beside the headline and never folded into it.
7. **The five gates**, as executable tests, including both free limiting cases.

### What would falsify this approach

1. **The phase-class census finding more than one class**, which would make the register a per-station question
   and the count something other than 0 or 34 — the bound would then be an ordinary upper bound and not a shape.
2. **The pruned closure verdict disagreeing with `C-0057`'s own `bestLinkClosure`** on the base junction —
   `C-0062`'s gate, re-run here because this task uses the pruning on a junction `C-0062` did not prune.
3. **The single-instance limit failing to reproduce `C-0062`'s trio**, which would mean the array model is not
   placing the object `C-0062` found.
4. **The collapsed limit failing to reproduce `C-0063`'s placement**, which would mean the stations are not `C-0063`'s.
5. **A register offset that no phase can absorb** — expected, and if it appears it is a *finding*, because the
   sheet's own phase moves the station and the base lattice **together** and therefore cannot absorb anything.

### Cost

Kotlin/JVM, openrndr-math for the geometry, `C-0053`'s packer, `C-0059`'s junction set, `C-0062`'s pruned verdict
and `C-0063`'s grillage all re-run as libraries. The only expensive term is the register field —
one 32 bp period at 0.17 nm steps is ~64 positions × ≤ 4 candidates, and `C-0062`'s per-assignment pruning makes
each solve ~7 ms rather than 0.77 s. No new physics and no new literature is needed: every ingredient exists,
and what has never been done is the composition.

## What changed during execution, recorded rather than silently absorbed

1. **The rigid translation became a per-instance choice.** The Plan expected one offset `δ` for the whole array,
   because every station is one phase class. That is right about the *register* and wrong about the *design*:
   the registered centres repeat every 32 bp on every row, so each instance may take **any** of them independently.
   The placement therefore offers each instance the admissible offsets in ascending magnitude and takes the first
   its containment rule admits — which is what turns *"26 of 34 at five of the seven row pitches"* into 34 and
   makes the negative, where one appears, a searched one rather than an artefact of a common offset.
2. **Two containment rules are reported rather than one.** `C-0053`'s rule (the whole block inside the tile) is
   right for an arm cut out of the host and wrong for a crossbar that is a free duplex above it. Both are emitted;
   they differ by exactly the outer column, and the difference is the register offset's doing.
3. **The strand convention is applied to the OFFSETS, not to the grid.** The first attempt re-gridded the register
   field around the shifted datum and reported *"no registered centre at all"* — a statement about the grid, since
   the closing set lives on a continuum. Moving the datum in the results leaves all three centres.
