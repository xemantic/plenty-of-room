## Formulate

### The question

`C-0059` re-derived all three junctions of the truss branch on `C-0057`'s torsion-feasible set and returned a **split verdict**:
the single junction closes at a chord of exactly 90.0° (`cos²ψ = 1.0000`),
the pair closes at every separation from 6 to 12 bp (at 6.0° at the 9 and 10 bp rows),
and the **trio does not close** — 0 of the 24 best-aligned of 750 reach-feasible lattices, 134 junction solves.

`C-0059` labelled that third result honestly as a **"not found within the budget"** and named the budget:
24 of 750 lattices, **two** candidate azimuths per junction, **two** crossbar lengths (13 and 15 bp), **one** row pitch (7 bp).
So the branch now hangs on a question about the *search*, not about the *geometry*:

> **Is `C-0059`'s trio negative a property of the crossbar, or a property of a thin search?**

This project's own discipline answers what such a question needs:
*"a negative existence claim needs its query strings recorded, not just its conclusion"* — here, its **search budget**.

### The design space, and what "the trio" means

Three 90° junctions on one **lone** crossbar duplex (`C-0052`):
two leg heads arriving from below at `±w/2` and the flexure's own end arriving from the side at the midpoint.
A trio **exists** at a lattice when all three junctions simultaneously

1. are **covalent** — both links of every junction inside the measured `[0.60, 0.70]` nm phosphodiester step, no van der Waals contact (`C-0029`'s test);
2. are **reach-feasible** — every link inside `[0.2228, 0.4095]` nm of `O3′···C5′` at three measured σ (`C-0057`'s closed-form bound, a proof of exclusion);
3. **close at torsion level** — every link's covalent geometry within 3 σ *and* every torsion in a ≥ 0.1 %-occupied ten-degree bin (`C-0057`'s solve);
4. keep **six distinct** crossbar phosphates and no two termini of different junctions in van der Waals contact;
5. keep every junction's truncated seat contact above `C-0042`'s 1.60 nm floor.

**Alignment is not a condition of existence** and is not imposed as one.
`C-0059` *ranked* by alignment and reported the closure; this task does the same and reports the alignment of what it finds.

### Acceptance predicate

**`Q1` — the search is genuinely deepened along every axis `C-0059` names as unexplored**, and the depth is stated as a number:
more lattices (against 24), all reach-feasible candidate azimuths per junction (against 2),
more crossbar lengths (against 2), more row pitches (against 1),
and a **joint** sweep over the sheet lattice, the crossbar's phase and the leg length together rather than `C-0059`'s deliberately conservative independent composition.

**`Q2` — the budget is a first-class number.**
How many configurations, lattices, junction slots, candidate azimuths, junction solves and link closures were examined,
and what fraction of the enumerated space that is.
A negative that names its budget is falsifiable; one that does not is an opinion.

**`Q3` — the feasible-set method is consumed as a library and re-checked, not re-derived.**
`C-0057`'s and `C-0059`'s published numbers are reproduced from `gpd/results/T-71-*.json` and `gpd/results/T-124-*.json`
and any departure is quoted.
**The free limiting case: restricting this task's search to `C-0059`'s own budget must reproduce its 0-of-24 result.**

**`Q4` — if a trio is found**, its geometry is carried into `C-0048`'s cap
(`12EI/w` bending, `4C/w` torsion, the frame couple `k_a Σd_i²` as a rank-one tensor with `Σx² + Σy² = w²/2` conserved)
and `C-0037`'s nine predicates and buckling margin are re-quoted on **both** rigidities —
CanDo's `EI = 230 pN·nm²` and Fields et al.'s implied **172.906 pN·nm²**, 25 % below it.

**`Q5` — if it is not found at a serious budget**, the claim says so plainly and states what that does to the truss branch:
no trio means no cap means the branch closes at `C-0037`'s design, which is a result §7 wants early rather than late.

### Units, geometry and sign conventions — restated

- Lengths **nm**, angles **radians** in code and **degrees** in every reported number, torsions IUPAC folded to `(−180, 180]`.
- T = 300 K, `k_BT = 4.141947 pN·nm`, aqueous **2 mM MgCl₂**.
- The crossbar is a lone duplex along `x̂` through the origin; a leg stands along `+ẑ` below it and meets its underside; the flexure arrives along `−ŷ` and meets its flank.
- Rise **0.34 nm/bp**, **10.67 bp/turn** (square lattice), phosphate radius **1.00 nm**, minor groove **120°** — `C-0029`'s geometry, which `C-0052`, `C-0057` and `C-0059` all run on.
- **The phosphodiester step is a WINDOW, not a reach**, and `C-0057` found that the `[0.60, 0.70]` nm window in circulation **has no primary source**:
  measured on 13 084 crystallographic linkages the pair is **0.607 / 0.664 nm**, so the window is **5 % wider at the top than the backbone is**.
  **The window used here is the inherited `[0.60, 0.70]` nm**, unchanged, because the feasible set must be `C-0057`'s and `C-0059`'s;
  the measured pair is carried as a **sensitivity**, and it can only shrink the space.
- A duplex **end** has exactly two strand termini and a lever arm ≤ **1.0 nm** (`C-0029`'s counting theorem) — no search here can add a third link.
- **A leg is one body with two junctions**, so its base and cap chords differ by `m × 33.74°` folded mod π and only the *difference* is quantised (`C-0052`).
- **The azimuthal quantum belongs to the sheet**, not to a free duplex's own chord (`CH-0056`): a standoff's `ψ₀` is continuous and is swept as a continuum at a stated resolution.

## Plan

### The cheap bound, and it is the whole method — the MARGINAL closure census

The trio is a conjunction of three junction closures on one lattice.
Each junction's placement problem is **conditionally independent of the other two given the lattice** —
they share the crossbar's phase, axial phase and lateral seat and nothing else,
and the only couplings are the six-distinct-targets and termini-clearance conditions, both of which are arithmetic and free.

So the *marginal* rate is measurable before any joint search:
sweep a sample of lattices, and for each junction count what fraction of lattices carries **at least one** torsion-closing placement.
Call those `q₋`, `q₊`, `q_f`. Under independence the expected number of closing lattices in `N` is `N q₋ q₊ q_f`.

**This is a predictor with two teeth.**

- If some `q = 0` over a large sample, the trio is **dead by a necessary condition**, cheaply and decisively — no joint search is needed at all.
- If the `q`s are all healthy, then `N q₋ q₊ q_f` says what `C-0059`'s budget could have been expected to return,
  and therefore whether its negative carried any information.

**The cost justification.** One junction solve is two inverse-kinematic link closures over 32 assignments of donor end, strand polarity and sugar pucker
— 0.77 s at `C-0059`'s own 60-step / 4-refinement grid.
`C-0057`'s reach bound is a **per-assignment** proof of exclusion, so an assignment that fails it cannot close and need not be solved:
**12 % of assignments survive it, which is a 17.2× speedup, exactly**, and the pruned verdict is asserted equal to `bestLinkClosure(...).closes` as a gate test.
With that and eight cores a junction solve costs ~7 ms of wall clock, which is what makes a 30 000-lattice sweep a 30-minute study instead of a 30-hour one.
**This is the cheap-bound-before-the-expensive-search rule applied to the solver itself**, and it is why the deepening is affordable at all.

### What would falsify this approach

1. **The marginal census showing a junction that never closes.** Then the cheap bound *is* the answer and the expensive sweep is not run — the good outcome.
2. **The pruned verdict disagreeing with `C-0057`'s own `bestLinkClosure`** on any sampled link. Then the speedup is unsound and the whole budget has to be spent at 17× the cost.
3. **The deepened sweep finding 0 closing trios where the marginal product predicts many.**
   That would not be a null result: it would mean the three junctions are **not** independent given the lattice — a correlation nobody has looked for —
   and *that* would be the finding, with the negative then resting on a real mechanism rather than on a thin budget.
4. **A found trio failing to survive the fine verdict grid** (180 steps / 6 refinements, `C-0057`'s own), which every candidate is re-solved on.

### The method

1. **Consume and re-check.** `C-0057`'s and `C-0059`'s published numbers read from their result files and reproduced from their libraries; departures quoted.
2. **The cheap bound.** The marginal closure census over a lattice sample, per junction, and the independence yield prediction — including what `C-0059`'s own 24-lattice budget was expected to return.
3. **The deepened sweep.** Every lattice of a `(phase × axial phase × lateral seat)` grid, at every `(crossbar length, row pitch)` configuration in the admissible band,
   with **all** reach-feasible candidate azimuths solved at every junction rather than the best-aligned two.
4. **The depth run.** The design point re-swept at a finer lattice grid, so that the resolution is a convergence axis and not an assumption.
5. **The mechanics**, if a trio exists: `C-0048`'s cap and `C-0037`'s nine predicates re-quoted at the misalignment the *found* trio delivers,
   on both rigidities, over the whole quantised leg-length envelope — and at a row pitch that is **the same number** at the base and at the cap.
6. **The five gates**, as executable tests.

### Cost

Kotlin/JVM, openrndr-math for the geometry, `C-0052`'s, `C-0057`'s and `C-0059`'s pipelines re-run as libraries.
No new physics and no new literature is needed: the question is one of search budget, and the instrument already exists.
