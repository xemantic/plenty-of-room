## Formulate

`C-0055` moved the `E5a` hinge array off the sheet's own crossovers and onto the unoccupied
upward azimuth.
The host therefore loses **no** crossover and **no** duplex length,
and every table in `C-0054` — all computed on *consumed* interfaces — stops describing it.
Nothing replaces them.

But 34 arms of 8.164 nm are **278 nm of duplex added out of plane at one interhelical distance**,
which is mass, drag and rigidity in a direction no model in this programme contains:
`C-0009`'s grillage is a single sheet,
`C-0006`'s plate is two-dimensional,
and `C-0010`'s variance budget has no out-of-plane member.

### The acceptance predicate

`C-0009`'s grillage, `C-0006`/`C-0047`'s flatness and `C-0010`'s variance,
**re-run on a sheet carrying the array**,
each reported with the change the array makes to it,
plus the drainage consequence `C-0004` owns,
and a statement of whether any standing verdict moves.
Where one does, a **challenge** is filed rather than the number being absorbed.

### Locked units and conventions

- Lengths **nm**, forces **pN**, stiffness **pN/nm**, rigidities **pN·nm²**,
  foundation stiffness **pN/nm³**, pressure **pN/nm²** (= 1 MPa exactly),
  drag **pN·s/nm**, mass **pN·s²/nm**, frequency **Hz**;
  `k_BT = 4.141947 pN·nm` at **300 K** in aqueous **2 mM MgCl₂**.
- `x` **along** the helices, `y` **across** them in the sheet plane,
  `z` **normal** and positive **upward** — away from the grafted layer, which is below the tile
  (`C-0055`'s convention);
  `w` positive **downward**, compressing the layer (`C-0006`, `C-0009`).
- An **arm** is a duplex lying **parallel** to its host duplex, one interhelical distance above it,
  held by **one** antiparallel crossover at its root.
  It is *not* a duplex standing normal to the sheet:
  the crossover motif requires parallel helices,
  and `C-0029`'s literature finding on that is unchanged and upstream of everything here.
- An **upward root** is a site of the `+z` column lattice,
  whose pitch is the full per-interface crossover spacing `p = 32 bp = 10.88 nm`,
  because an upward site belongs to **one** duplex and is shared with nobody (`C-0055`).

## Plan

**The cheap bound first, and it decides which of the three channels is worth computing.**

1. **Rigidity.**
   A body attached to a structure at a **single point** and otherwise free
   contributes **exactly zero** static stiffness there:
   its rigid-body motions span everything the attachment can impose,
   so the minimised energy is identically zero and the Schur complement vanishes term by term.
   This is a condensation identity, not a solve, and it costs four lines.
   The *other* end of the bracket — a genuinely tied second layer — is the parallel-axis
   `2EI + Sδ²/2`, also four lines.
   **Which end applies is decided by one inequality**: whether the arm is long enough to reach a
   second upward root.
2. **Mass.** Contour ratio → quality factor. `C-0004` already puts `Q` three orders inside the
   overdamped boundary, so this can only matter if the ratio were enormous, and it is 0.46.
3. **Drag.** Slender-body transverse drag of 34 short cylinders, against `C-0004`'s squeeze drag.
   **The arms stand on the face AWAY from the layer**, so the squeeze film is untouched by
   construction and what is added is bulk dissipation in parallel with the tile's own Stokes term.

Only after the bound does the expensive part run:
the grillage **augmented** with the arms as extra degrees of freedom in **one** stiffness matrix
(`CLAUDE.md`: a dense matrix per element type is what turns a comfortable lattice solve into an
out-of-memory failure the JVM misreports as `NoClassDefFoundError`),
solved under `C-0022`'s **solved** edge profile —
because a uniform load makes a free plate dish exactly zero whatever its rigidity,
so a uniform-load flatness answer is vacuous.

### What would falsify this approach

| # | falsifier |
|---|---|
| **1** | the condensed added stiffness at one tie failing to vanish, or failing to vanish **linearly** in the regularisation — that would mean a free body does add stiffness at a point, and the whole cheap bound is wrong |
| **2** | zero attached arms failing to reproduce `C-0009`'s grillage exactly |
| **3** | a uniform load on a uniform Winkler foundation dishing anything but zero, with or without the arms |
| **4** | the arm reaching a second upward root somewhere in the design range — the single tie would then be a modelling choice rather than a lattice fact, and the 19.3× composite would be in play |
| **5** | the arms' drag exceeding `C-0004`'s squeeze drag, which would put §4(d) back in question |
| **6** | the flatness at the arm roots coming out **equal** to the flatness on `C-0015`'s 3 × 15 grid, which would mean the placement carries no information and the task closes in a paragraph |

### Method justification against cost

The condensation identity settles the headline question for the price of a 6 × 6 matrix.
Everything expensive here exists to make it **falsifiable at the sheet level** rather than
asserted: the augmented 1059-degree-of-freedom assembly is run so that a departure, if there
were one, would appear in `C-0009`'s own published quantities through `C-0009`'s own code path.
The flatness and variance re-runs are not about the arms' rigidity at all —
they are about **where the coupling now enters**, which is the one thing the array really changes.
