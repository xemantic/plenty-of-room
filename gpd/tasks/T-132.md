## Formulate

### The question

`C-0052` established that **a leg is one body with two junctions**: its base chord and its cap chord
differ by `m × 33.74°` folded modulo `π`, so only the **difference** is quantised and rotating the
leg about its own axis trades the two misalignments one for one, exactly.
`C-0062` chose its design on that budget, sweeping the leg's rotation freely against two independent
misalignment **floors**.

`C-0065` has since **pinned** the base coordinate.
A leg's base sits only where the host's own backbone offers one; no row pitch closes at the station
itself; and no crossover phase can absorb the offset, because the phase moves the station and the
backbone together.
Pinning it re-read `C-0062`'s own recommended 10 bp row from a published **6.0°** to **57.0°** — past
the **45°** at which `C-0037`'s `TwoLinkBase` cannot represent a base at all — and moved the
buildable row to **9 bp at 18.0°**.

A pinned base is not a floor.
It fixes the leg's rotation, so the cap misalignment is no longer chosen: it **follows** from the
base and the leg's length.
And because all 34 caps sit at one height, **one leg length serves the whole array**.

### The acceptance predicate

`C-0052`'s `chordPairMisalignment(m)` re-composed against `C-0065`'s register, at **one global leg
length shared by all 34 instances**, over the crossbar lattices `C-0062` found closing and the
stations `C-0063` supplies, reporting:

1. whether a single leg length exists that serves every instance;
2. what becomes of `C-0065`'s **17 of 44** trios that survive every clause at once;
3. what becomes of its recommended **9 bp row at 18.0°**;
4. the mechanics re-quoted if the geometry moves — `C-0048`'s `12EI/w` bending and `4C/w` torsion,
   `C-0037`'s frame couple with `Σx² + Σy² = w²/2` conserved, and the buckling margin on **both**
   rigidities (CanDo's `EI = 230 pN·nm²` and Fields et al.'s implied `172.906 pN·nm²`);
5. and, if the shared-length constraint kills the design, what a **per-instance** leg length would
   cost in staple count and assembly — a real design axis, not a get-out.

### Units, geometry and sign conventions — restated, not inherited

- Lengths **nm**, forces **pN**, stiffness **pN/nm**, angles **degrees** in every reported number and
  **radians** in code; `k_BT = 4.141947 pN·nm` at **300 K** in aqueous **2 mM MgCl₂**.
- `x` runs **along** the host sheet's helices, `y` **across** them, `z` **normal and positive
  upward**. Origin at the tile centre.
- **A chord is a line**, so every chord azimuth folds modulo `π`; a **misalignment** is the folded
  distance and lies in `[0°, 90°]`, and a **signed deviation** `δ` lies in `(−90°, +90°]`.
- The **base** chord wants the flexure's own axis (`ŷ` in `C-0042`'s and `C-0052`'s frame,
  `wantedChordAzimuth = π/2`); the **cap** chord wants **across** it. The wanted separation is
  therefore **90°**, exactly as `C-0052` and `C-0048` state it.
- The twist is the square lattice's designed **10.67 bp/turn**, i.e. `τ = 33.7394°` per base-pair
  step; **10.5 bp/turn** is carried as a sensitivity, as `C-0052` carries it.
- **The leg envelope is `C-0052`'s 12–26 base-pair steps** (4.08–8.84 nm at the 0.34 nm rise).
- **One truss is two legs on ONE host duplex**, at the register centre ∓ `w/2` base pairs, sharing
  one crossbar; the two legs are the **same length in base pairs**, because legs of different lengths
  tilt the crossbar (`C-0052`'s own validity clause). 34 trusses are **68 leg bases**.
- **A register offset is signed and measured from the station**; a positive offset moves the
  instance toward `+x`. `C-0065`'s convention verbatim.

### What is modelled and what is not

A torsion closure is a **necessary** condition and never a sufficient one — `C-0029`, `C-0052`,
`C-0057`, `C-0059`, `C-0062` and `C-0065` all say so, and it is restated here because this task
composes their verdicts.
Every *"closes"* here is an **upper bound on buildability**.
**TRL 1–3: nothing is measured, no sequence is designed, no assembly is demonstrated.**

## Plan

### The cheap bounds, which run before the composition

**Bound 1 — the counting argument, which decides how large the problem is.**
`C-0065`'s bound 3 found that every one of `C-0063`'s 34 stations is the **same helical phase class**
of its **own** host duplex, so the register question is asked once rather than thirty-four times.
A leg base is a station offset by ∓`w/2` base pairs **along the same duplex**, so the same census
applies to the **68 leg bases**: they should occupy exactly **two** classes of **34** each — one per
leg of the pair.
If so, *"one leg length for all 34 instances"* collapses to *"one leg length for the two legs of one
truss"*, and the array clause costs **nothing**.

> **Falsifier.** More than two classes, or two classes of unequal population, means the 34 instances
> do **not** present the same pair of base azimuths and no single leg length can serve them — which
> would kill the shared-length design on arithmetic alone, before any junction is solved.

**Bound 2 — the chord-difference floor, exact and closed form.**
With both bases pinned at signed deviations `δ_A`, `δ_B`, the two cap misalignments are
`|fold(90° + δ + m τ)|` and differ from each other by `|fold(δ_A − δ_B)|` — **independently of the
leg length**. Hence for **every** `m`,

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;`max(ψ_cap,A, ψ_cap,B) ≥ |fold(δ_A − δ_B)| / 2`,

a floor on the cap misalignment that **no leg length can beat**.
And the 12–26 step envelope samples the chord circle at a spacing of `180°/16.02 ≈ 11.24°`, so the
best achievable worst cap is within **half a spacing, 5.62°**, of that floor.

> **Falsifier.** An observed best worst-cap **below** the floor (a coding error), or more than
> `floor + 5.62°` above it (the envelope not sampling the circle as claimed).

**Why the bounds run first.** Together they say what the expensive stage can possibly return: bound 1
says whether the array clause is a two-body or a thirty-four-body condition, and bound 2 says what
cap misalignment the pinned pair permits before any design is evaluated. Both are arithmetic and
cost nothing; the register field costs a few hundred junction solves.

### The expensive stage

1. **The register of signed azimuths.** `C-0059`'s `SingleJunctionFeasibleSet` and `C-0062`'s pruned
   `junctionClosesOnSomeAssignment`, evaluated over `C-0065`'s own 89-position, ±22 bp window at
   0.17 nm steps — but collecting **every** closing candidate at each position with its **signed**
   chord deviation, where `C-0065` kept only the best-aligned winner. The winner must reproduce
   `C-0065`'s table exactly; the rest is the freedom this task needs, because a design may prefer a
   worse-aligned base that buys a better cap.
2. **The pinned composition.** For each row pitch, at the closing pair centre nearest the station,
   for each leg length in 12–26 steps and each admissible pair of base candidates: base
   misalignments from the register, cap misalignments from `chordPairMisalignment`'s own arithmetic,
   `C-0062`'s cap and flexure floors imposed as lower bounds, and `C-0048`'s `capDesign` run.
   **One** leg length is then chosen per row — the one maximising the critical load, subject to all
   nine predicates.
3. **The consequence**, for `C-0065`'s 44 array placements and its 17 survivors.

**Cost justification.** The composition itself is 7 rows × 15 lengths × a handful of candidate pairs
— microseconds. The whole cost is the register field, ~400 junction solves at `C-0062`'s pruned
solver, i.e. minutes. A joint search over the crossbar lattice **and** the base's axial position
(`C-0065`'s open item 2) is the alternative and is 1 000× more expensive; it is **not** run here,
and this task's composition is the conservative statement that bounds it from the favourable side.

### The conservative composition, stated as such

The cap floor is imposed as `cap = max(cap_geometric, capFloor)` — `C-0062`'s own device, and the
same independence assumption: it presumes the crossbar can be arranged to close at **any**
misalignment at or above its floor. That bounds the achievable design from the **favourable** side.
A sharper statement needs the joint search.

### The five gates

1. **Dimensional** — a misalignment is an angle in `[0, 90°]` and a signed deviation in `(−90°, 90°]`;
   the composition is invariant under adding `180°` to any chord azimuth; unphysical arguments throw.
2. **Limiting cases** — **the two free ones the prompt names**: an **unpinned** base (`δ` free)
   reproduces `C-0052`'s budget and `C-0062`'s design table; and a **single instance** reproduces
   `C-0065`'s register table row for row. Plus `δ = 0` giving `ψ_cap = budget` exactly.
3. **Symmetry and conservation** — `ψ_base + ψ_cap ≥ budget` **always**, with equality exactly when
   the pinned deviation opposes the budget's sense (the pinned base can **overspend** where a free
   one merely spends); the two legs' cap misalignments differ by a constant independent of `m`;
   `C-0037`'s `Σx² + Σy² = w²/2` rank-one conservation; a chord is a line.
4. **Numerical convergence** — azimuth steps 120 → 240, candidates per position 4 → 12, axial steps
   2 → 4 per base pair, and the leg envelope widened.
5. **Literature and upstream** — `C-0052`'s 78.53° / 0.25° / 89.8° budgets, `C-0059`'s seven base
   floors, `C-0062`'s seven cap floors and its 2.45 / 1.84 best margin, `C-0065`'s seven register
   rows, `C-0048`'s `12EI/w` and `4C/w`.

### What would falsify the approach as a whole

- **Bound 1's falsifier firing** — the design dies on arithmetic and the task's answer is a plain no.
- **The register winner failing to reproduce `C-0065`'s table** — the register is not the same object
  and nothing composed on it is comparable.
- **The unpinned control failing to reproduce `C-0062`'s design table** — the composition is not
  `C-0052`'s and `C-0048`'s pipeline but a different one.
