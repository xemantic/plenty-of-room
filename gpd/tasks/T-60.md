## Formulate

### The question

`C-0027` reports `C-0018`'s 1.007–1.032 pull-in margin as **standing, with its movement unresolved**.
The reason is arithmetic, not physics:
`CH-0035` establishes that at a force-pinned operating point a multiplier `μ(h)` on the *level* of `|F_es|`
is absorbed entirely into the bias and reaches `k_es` **exactly not at all**,
so the only surviving quantity is `d ln μ/dh` —
and `d ln μ/dh` is known only as a finite difference between gaps that `T-3b`'s sweep visited **at different biases**.
Three difference schemes over those five gaps span `0.0133–0.0226 nm⁻¹`,
and that spread alone leaves the coupled tangent at the fold running `−2.5` to `+4.0 pN/nm` — straddling zero.

**So: what is `μ(h)` at fixed bias, what is its gradient, and does `C-0018`'s margin move?**

### Locked units and sign conventions

Restated in full, because the whole task is a transfer between two claims' conventions.

- `z` is normal to the electrode, positive **away** from it; the electrode is the plane `z = 0`;
  **the electrostatic gap IS the layer height, exactly** (`C-0012`).
- `x` is lateral, `x = 0` is the tile centre-line, the rim is at `x = 20 nm` (`C-0022`).
- The **stroke** `s = L₀ − h` is positive **downward**; **`L₀` is a FORCE-ONSET height** (`C-0011`, `CH-0010`).
- The **collar multiplier** is
  `μ(h, V) ≡ |F_es,2D(h, V)| / (Π_1D(h, V) · A)`, `A = 1600 nm²`,
  dimensionless, `μ > 1` an **enhancement**.
  It is emitted by `T-3b` as a force **deficit** fraction, so `μ = 1 − fraction` and the two mappings
  (minimum-margin and additive) bracket the unsolved corner.
- **`d ln μ/dh` is in `nm⁻¹` and is taken at FIXED APPLIED BIAS**, which is the state variable `k_es` is
  differentiated at — not at fixed diffuse-layer drop, and not along a path on which the bias moves.
- `k_es = |F_es| d ln|F_es|/dh`, and `ℓ = −1/(d ln|F_es|/dh)`, so **`k_es = −|F_es|/ℓ` identically**;
  `k_es < 0` above the force maximum (`CH-0011`, `C-0018`).
- The **load line** `R(s)` is positive **upward**: free (`R = 0`), dead load (`R = 100 pN`),
  coupled (`R = 33.333 s`, `C-0017`'s mandate).
  **A ceiling belongs to a `(bias, load line)` pair.**
- Lengths nm, forces pN, pressure `pN/nm² = 1 MPa` exactly, stiffness pN/nm, bias V,
  buffer mM MgCl₂ (2:1, so `I = 3c`), `k_BT = 4.142 pN·nm` at 300 K.

### Acceptance predicate

**`P1` — `μ(h)` at fixed bias.**
The collar multiplier solved at ≥ 10 gaps at **one fixed applied bias per (buffer, height) state**,
covering the gaps the equilibrium path actually visits,
with the bias-dependence measured rather than assumed
(≥ 3 biases at one gap), and both the minimum-margin and additive mappings emitted.

**`P2` — `d ln μ/dh` as a derivative of one function.**
The logarithmic gradient at the fold gap and at the operating gap, from
(i) central differences at ≥ 2 step sizes, (ii) a `C¹` interpolant through the solved samples, and
(iii) the closed-form cheap bound — with the spread between them reported and
compared against `C-0027`'s inherited `0.0133–0.0226 nm⁻¹`.

**`P3` — the fold, re-located.**
`C-0018`'s fold search re-run through a collar-corrected field at 10 nm / 2 mM and at 7 nm / 10 mM,
for all six `C-0003` layer models and all three load lines, under **three** field variants —
`μ ≡ 1` (which must reproduce `C-0018` exactly), `μ ≡ const` (level only) and `μ(h)` (level + gradient) —
so that the level part and the gradient part are **measured separately** rather than argued.

**`P4` — the verdict.**
Whether `C-0018`'s 1.007–1.032 margin moves, in which direction, and whether it goes below one,
at the states where pull-in is the binding ceiling.
Plus the 7 nm / 10 mM states, whose fold stroke (1.92–2.68 nm) is *shallower* than §3's 3 nm —
the one place a bias below pull-in is not sufficient — restated after correction.

### What would falsify this approach

1. **`μ` turning out to depend appreciably on the bias.** `C-0027`'s whole finite difference — and this
   task's fixed-bias sweep — rests on `μ = μ(h)`. `T-3b` saw 0.14 % across one bracket at one gap; if the
   spread across three biases here exceeds ~2 %, `d ln μ/dh` is not a well-posed single function and the
   task must report a two-argument surface instead.
2. **`μ` falling with the gap** anywhere inside the path's range, which would flip the sign of the surviving
   term and hand `CH-0026`'s original direction back.
3. **The `μ ≡ 1` variant failing to reproduce `C-0018`'s folds** to the rounding of its published table,
   which would mean the corrected field is not the same object as the uncorrected one plus a multiplier.
4. **The gradient failing to converge in the mesh.** Convergence is a property of the *quantity*
   (`CLAUDE.md`), and a gradient is a difference of two quantities that individually converge; if `d ln μ/dh`
   moves more between refinement 2 and 3 than the difference-scheme spread it is asked to collapse, the task
   has not collapsed anything and must say so.
5. **The margin moving by less than its own numerical resolution.** A golden-section maximum is floored by
   the noise of the bisection under it (`λ√(2t)`), so a margin movement below that floor is not a movement.

## Plan

### The cheap bound, before any 2-D solve

`C-0022` shows the whole edge effect amounts to a sub-Debye **collar**: the finite tile behaves as one
`w` larger on every side, so on the additive mapping `μ = 1 + 4w/L`. `C-0022`'s own *rigorous* width
ceiling is the transverse eigenvalue `w ≤ 1/q₀`, `q₀ = √(κ² + (π/2h)²)`, which gives a closed form for the
gradient with no solve at all:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;`d ln μ/dh = (4/L)(dw/dh)/μ`, &nbsp; `dw/dh = (π²/4) h⁻³ / q₀³`.

**Expected error, stated in advance:** the width ceiling overstates the solved collar by ~1.8× at the design
point, and it enters the numerator and the denominator with opposite effect, so the estimate is expected good
to **about a factor of two, one-sided in neither direction**. It is not a bound on the gradient — the ceiling
bounds `w`, not `dw/dh` — and it is quoted as an **estimate**, which is what `C-0022`'s own depth half of the
cheap bound failed to be.

**What it buys:** the sign, and the order of magnitude, before ~40 minutes of 2-D solving. If the solved
gradient is negative, or an order of magnitude away, the collar picture that both `CH-0026` and `CH-0035`
rest on is wrong and the task is a different one.

### The expensive calculation, and why it is the cheapest sufficient one

**Method.** Re-run `C-0022`'s `PoissonBoltzmannEdge` — unmodified, consumed as a library — at a **fixed
applied bias**, inverting the Stern series per gap so that the *applied* bias, not the diffuse drop, is what
is held. `μ` is read as `1 − edgeForceFraction`, both mappings, with the denominator taken as the solve's
**own** centre-line load rather than a separate 1-D solve: the two agree to 0.03–0.14 %, and 0.1 % of noise
in a ratio differenced over 0.5 nm is 15 % of the gradient. The independent 1-D solve is retained as a
**gate**, not as the denominator.

**Justification against cost.** The alternative — a full 2-D coupled equilibrium path, solving the edge
problem inside the fold search — is ~1300 field evaluations per state and 2-D solves are ~10⁴ times a 1-D
one. It is four orders of magnitude more expensive and answers the same question, because the edge enters
the actuator problem *only* as a multiplier on the footprint-integrated force (`CH-0026`'s own instruction:
"carry a multiplier, not a re-run"). Tabulating `μ` on a gap grid and interpolating it is the cheapest
sufficient method, and its sufficiency is testable: the `μ ≡ 1` variant must reproduce `C-0018` exactly.

**The decomposition, measured rather than argued.** Three field variants are run:
`μ ≡ 1`, `μ ≡ μ(h_fold)` (a constant — the *level* only), and the solved `μ(h)`.
`CH-0035` argues that the level cancels at a force-pinned point; running the constant-`μ` variant
**measures** how much of the margin movement is level and how much is gradient, and it settles which of
`C-0018`'s quantities are force-pinned without an appeal to the identity.

**Interpolation.** A cubic Hermite through `ln μ` with parabolic node slopes, so that the interpolant's
derivative *at a node is exactly the central difference there* — the number reported and the number used are
the same object. Clamped outside the solved range, with every clamped evaluation counted and the fold
asserted interior. A spline was rejected: `ln μ` changes sign in the sampled range and an oscillating
interpolant would put structure into a gradient that is the whole answer.

### Gates

1. **Dimensional** — `μ` dimensionless and asserted against the same file's own collar width;
   `d ln μ/dh` in `nm⁻¹` asserted through its own definition, not its formula;
   corrected force in pN, `k_es` in pN/nm.
2. **Limiting** — `μ → 1` as the tile edge grows (the collar is `1/L`); a zero collar gives a zero gradient;
   `μ ≡ 1` reproduces the uncorrected field **identically**; a constant `μ` has exactly zero gradient at
   every gap.
3. **Symmetry / conservation** — the 2-D charge balance of every solve; `μ` at one gap from three biases
   (it must be a function of the gap); the solve's centre-line load against an independent 1-D solve.
4. **Convergence** — refinement 2 / 3 / 4 on `d ln μ/dh` itself, not only on `μ`; difference step 1.0 /
   0.5 / 0.25 nm; the fold's own stroke tolerance.
5. **Cross-check** — `T-3b`'s published `μ` reproduced at its own `(gap, bias)` points; `C-0018`'s pull-in
   biases and margins reproduced at `μ ≡ 1`; `C-0027`'s inherited `0.0133–0.0226 nm⁻¹` compared against the
   fixed-bias derivative.

### Validity, carried in

`C-0005` puts the electrostatic one-loop correction at **123–214 %** of the leading term across this whole
gap range, and for the oppositely charged tile-electrode pair no published result gives even the direction.
Every margin here is two orders inside that. The result is a statement about **the model's own arithmetic**,
which is exactly what `C-0027` asked for: whether the correction the programme has already accepted moves
the margin it has already quoted.
