## Formulate

### The question

`C-0057` established two things that have to be taken together.

1. **The dihedrals do not close at any of the three reported optima.**
   `C-0029`'s single junction closes **0 of 4** links, `C-0042`'s pair **1 of 8**, `C-0052`'s trio **2 of 6**.
   Four of the eighteen are excluded by a **closed-form reach bound** — they close at no torsion whatever —
   and the rest fail on population: `ε = −22.9°` is carried by 0.015 % of 13 084 measured linkages
   and `β = 27.4°` by **zero** of 15 457 measured residues.
2. **And the branch is not dead**, because a torsion-feasible placement exists in `C-0029`'s own search space —
   **3 546** of 69 120 close on phosphate distance, **1 855** survive the reach bound, **18 of the 100 solved** close at torsion level —
   **which none of the three claims found, because all three optimised on distance**, the necessary-not-sufficient condition each had itself flagged.

So the routings are invalidated and the search space is known to contain feasible points.
What is *not* known is whether any feasible point is a point the **design** can use.
`C-0057` reports the chords of its two best feasible placements at **159.0°** and **−51.0°**,
against the **90.0°** `C-0042` needs on the flexure axis —
i.e. `cos²ψ` of **0.128** and **0.604** of the base couple reaching the plane that carries the load.
Those two placements were ranked by **reach margin**, not by alignment, so they are not evidence either way.

**The question this task answers is therefore the conjunction, not either half:
is there a placement that is simultaneously covalent, reach-feasible, torsion-closing AND aligned —
at each of the three scales — and if there is, what does the truss's mechanics become at the alignment it actually delivers?**

### Why the conjunction is not free, and why `CH-0056` makes it sharper rather than softer

`CH-0056` established that the **33.74°/bp azimuthal quantum belongs to the sheet**, not to a free duplex's own chord:
a standoff is a separate duplex and its rotation about its own axis is a **continuous** free parameter,
so `C-0042` could report every chord at exactly 90.0° and call the alignment free.

That argument is about the *distance* criterion, and it does not survive the torsion one unchanged.
The chord azimuth is `ψ₀ + Δ/2 + π/2`, a function of the standoff's own azimuth `ψ₀` alone —
still continuous — but **torsion feasibility is a function of `ψ₀` relative to the sheet's phosphate lattice**,
and that is exactly the coupling `CH-0056` says the *chord* does not have.
So the feasible set is a subset of a continuum, and the design's question is whether it is a **dense** subset or a sparse one.
`CH-0056`'s correction stands; what this task asks is what it costs once feasibility is imposed on the same variable.

### Acceptance predicate

**`Q1` — the feasible set is re-derived, not inherited.**
`C-0057`'s census is reproduced from its own libraries, not read off its result file:
69 120 placements, **3 546** covalent, **1 855** reach-feasible for `INDEPENDENT_STAPLES`,
**280** / **137** for `SCAFFOLD_EXCURSION`, and its reported best placement.
Any departure is quoted.

**`Q2` — the three routings are re-optimised on feasibility, not on distance.**
For each of `C-0029`'s single junction, `C-0042`'s pair on one sheet duplex and `C-0052`'s trio on the lone 13 bp crossbar,
the objective becomes **lexicographic**:

1. covalent — both links of every junction inside the measured `[0.60, 0.70]` nm step, no van der Waals contact (hard);
2. **reach-feasible** — every link inside `[0.2228, 0.4095]` nm of `O3′···C5′` at three measured σ (hard, closed form);
3. **closes at torsion level** — every link's covalent geometry within 3 σ *and* every torsion in a ≥ 0.1 %-occupied ten-degree bin (hard, expensive);
4. **aligned** — minimise the worst chord's misalignment `ψ` from the direction the design wants;
5. tie-break on the rarest torsion's occupancy.

**`Q3` — the mechanical consequences are carried through**, at the alignment the feasible placement delivers and not at the one the design wanted:
`C-0042`'s `chordBaseAxes` (`loaded = 2k_θ + 2k_s a² cos²ψ`, `free = 2k_θ + 2k_s a² sin²ψ`, with `loaded + free` a conserved budget),
`C-0037`'s frame couple `k_a Σd_i²`, `C-0048`'s cap (`12EI/w` bending, `4C/w` torsion),
the nine predicates `P1`–`P9`, and the buckling margin on **both** rigidities —
CanDo's `EI = 230 pN·nm²` and Fields et al.'s implied **172.9 pN·nm²**, which is 25 % below it (`C-0028`).

**`Q4` — the leg-is-one-body constraint survives the re-optimisation.**
`C-0052`'s finding that a leg's base and cap chords differ by `m × 33.74°` folded mod π,
and that only the **difference** is quantised, is imposed on every design point:
a base misalignment taken from the sheet search and a cap misalignment taken from the crossbar search
are admissible together only at leg lengths whose `relativeChordAzimuth(m)` connects them.

**`Q5` — the verdict.** Which of the three junctions survive, at what placement, and what the truss's margins become;
or the statement that no feasible placement supports the recommended designs, with the binding reason named.

### Units, geometry and sign conventions — restated, not inherited

- Lengths **nm**, forces **pN**, moments **pN·nm**, stiffness **pN/nm**, rotational stiffness **pN·nm/rad**, `k_BT = 4.141947 pN·nm` at **300 K**, aqueous **2 mM MgCl₂**.
- Torsions in **degrees**, IUPAC, folded to `(−180, 180]`; a torsion is **odd** under reflection and a bond angle **even**.
- The sheet duplexes run along **x̂** at `z = 0`, spaced `INTERHELICAL = 2.69 nm` in **ŷ**; a standoff stands along **+ẑ**.
- A phosphate of strand `s` at base pair `i` sits at azimuth `i·2π/10.67 + s·Δ` with `Δ = 120°` nominal (154° wide carried as a sensitivity), on radius `r_P = 1.00 nm`.
- **The chord azimuth is `ψ₀ + Δ/2 + π/2`** and a function of the standoff's own azimuth alone.
- **The flexure axis is at `π/2`** — across the sheet helix — so a single junction's or a pair's misalignment is `foldedChordMisalignment(chord, π/2) ∈ [0, π/2]`;
  a *cap* junction on the crossbar wants its chord **along** the crossbar (`0`) and the flexure's own end wants it **vertical** (`π/2`), which is `C-0052`'s convention and is kept.
- `cos²ψ` is the share of the base couple that reaches the plane the flexure loads; `sin²ψ` the share reaching the free plane; their sum is `4k_bond,θ + 2k_bond,s a²`, independent of `ψ`.
- **A duplex end has exactly TWO strand termini**, so at most two covalent links, on a chord of half-width `a ≤ r_P = 1.00 nm`; the phosphodiester step is a **window**, `[0.60, 0.70]` nm, not a reach.

## Plan

### The cheap bound, which runs before any solve

Two, and both are closed form.

| | bound | why it is cheap | what it can settle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **`C-0057`'s free-phosphate reach**, `O3′···C5′ ∈ [0.2228, 0.4095]` nm at three measured σ | three atom placements and a distance | a link outside it closes at **no** torsion, so it is a *proof* of exclusion and it already halves the distance-feasible set |
| **2** | **the alignment band as an azimuth interval.** Because the chord azimuth is `ψ₀ + Δ/2 + π/2`, `C-0029`'s own ±16.87° allowance (`cos² = 0.9158`, 8.4 % of the couple) is the azimuth interval `ψ₀ ∈ 120° ± 16.87° (mod 180°)`, i.e. **18.7 % of the circle** | it is arithmetic on the grid indices | if **no** reach-feasible placement has its azimuth in that band, the aligned design is dead **before any torsion solve** and the branch closes on a bound |

Bound 2 is the one written to bind, and running it first is what makes the expensive search affordable:
instead of solving the 100 best-ranked placements by *reach margin* — which is what `C-0057` did, and which has no reason to be aligned —
the survivors are ranked by **misalignment** and solved in that order, so the first closing placement found *is* the best-aligned one on the grid.

**Falsifier for the cheap bound.** If bound 2 admits placements at essentially every azimuth — i.e. if the reach-feasible set is
azimuthally dense — then it decides nothing, the alignment is free at the reach level, and the whole answer has to come from the
expensive torsion solve. That is a real possibility (`C-0057` reports 1 855 reach-feasible placements over 120 azimuth values,
an average of 15 per azimuth) and it is declared here rather than discovered later.

### The method, and its cost justification

**What is not done, and why.** An all-atom or oxDNA relaxation of a candidate is now the right *next* spend — `C-0057` says so —
but it is not this task's: the question here is a **search** over ~10⁵ placements at three scales, and a relaxation costs more per point
than the whole search. The instrument is `C-0057`'s own inverse-kinematic closure, re-run as a library at the placements
this task's objective selects. Nothing about the chemistry is re-derived; what is re-derived is **which placement**.

**Cost control.** The expensive step is `bestLinkClosure`, which sweeps donor end, both polarities and both puckers over a
`(γ, β)` grid with refinements. Every scale therefore runs: enumerate → filter on covalent → filter on reach (both free) →
**sort by misalignment** → solve in that order under a stated cap. A negative result is then *"not found within the budget"*,
never *"does not exist"* — exactly `C-0057`'s own census caveat, and it is repeated in the validity range.

**What would falsify the approach.**

1. **The limiting case failing.** Restricting the feasible set to `C-0029`'s reported optimum must reproduce `C-0057`'s
   verdict *exactly* — 0 of 4 links, worst `z = 4.553`, `ε = −22.9°`, `β = 27.4°`. If it does not, the re-optimisation is
   measuring a different instrument and nothing downstream may be believed.
2. **The rigid-cap limit failing.** `C-0048`'s cap at an asserted rigid head must reproduce `C-0037`'s truss frame couple
   and critical load. If it does not, the mechanics carried through are not the mechanics the claims were written on.
3. **The census not reproducing.** If the re-derived counts depart from `C-0057`'s 3 546 / 1 855 / 18, the feasible set
   being optimised over is not the feasible set the challenge was raised on.
4. **The answer being the phosphate radius's.** `C-0057`'s falsifier 4 fired: at `r_P = 0.90` nm the single junction's
   links *do* close and at 1.00 nm they do not. Any verdict here must be reported across the same bracket, or it is a
   statement about a convention.

### The five gates

1. **Dimensional** — a misalignment is an angle in `[0, π/2]`; `cos²ψ + sin²ψ = 1` makes `loaded + free` independent of `ψ`;
   a reach is a length; a frame couple is `pN·nm/rad` and a critical load `pN`; unphysical arguments throw.
2. **Limiting cases** — the two named above, plus: an aligned band of the full circle must return the unrestricted census;
   a placement 50 nm away is reach-excluded with no solve; and `ψ = 0` must reproduce `realisablePerpendicularBase`'s favourable reading.
3. **Symmetry and conservation** — the conserved base budget `loaded + free`; the chord azimuth's invariance under the
   standoff's axial position and lateral seat; a chord is a **line**, so `ψ₀` and `ψ₀ + π` are the same design.
4. **Numerical convergence** — the verdict under a coarser and a finer `(γ, β)` grid and under a finer azimuth grid,
   with the departure quoted; the solve cap swept.
5. **Literature and upstream** — `C-0057`'s census counts, its three scale verdicts and its reported binding links;
   `C-0042`'s 9.77 pN loaded-plane critical load; `C-0048`'s recommended design point through `capDesign`;
   `C-0052`'s 78.53° budget at 21 steps and its 0.25° best at 24.
