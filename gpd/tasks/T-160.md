and by the same claim's validity range:

## Formulate

### The question

`C-0086`/`C-0095` established that a seamless boustrophedon raster quantises the tile's
**along-helix** width, that §3's 40.0 nm is not an admissible row length and that
**38.08 nm = 112 bp** is.
`C-0090` then re-read the whole anchoring branch at that width and found the tile **12.0 % flatter**
(`0.0621469105` of the stroke against `C-0063`'s `0.0706145537`).

It did that with `C-0022`'s **interior** pressure rescaled to the new footprint —
which is exact, and cancels out of a dishing ratio —
and with `C-0022`'s three **collar** terms carried unchanged:

| term | `C-0022`'s value at a 20.0 nm half-width | where it is used |
|---|---|---|
| the smooth taper **depth** | `−0.302887367` | `coupling.CollarTerm(depth, width)` |
| the smooth taper **width** | `8.93928311` nm | the same |
| the **rim residual depth** over a 1 nm standoff | `−0.593889278` | the second `CollarTerm` |

The argument for carrying them is that the collar is a sub-Debye rim feature
and that the total fringing enhancement scales as `1/L`,
so a 4.8 % narrower tile is worth about `+0.7 %` on the **level** of the force —
and the level cancels out of a dishing.
**The question is whether the collar's SHAPE moves**, because that does not cancel.

`PoissonBoltzmannEdge` carries `tileHalfWidth` as a constructor parameter and `C-0022` never varied it.

### Numeric target and acceptance predicate

> A 2-D Poisson-Boltzmann edge solve at a 38.08 nm along-helix footprint,
> giving the collar's taper depth, taper width and rim residual at that width —
> **or** the statement that the collar is width-independent
> to within `C-0090`'s **0.32 %** placement sensitivity.

**Acceptance** (each a predicate in the result file):

| # | predicate |
|---|---|
| **P1** | the **cheap bound runs first** and is reported with its own bracket, and the decision to run or not run the 2-D solve is justified against that bracket rather than asserted |
| **P2** | the taper depth, the taper width and the rim residual are given at a **19.04 nm half-width** (38.08 nm footprint), at 300 K, aqueous 2 mM MgCl₂ (`I = 3c`), a 10 nm gap and `C-0012`'s located 0.192 V, with their convergence in the mesh |
| **P3** | at a **20.0 nm** half-width the same pipeline reproduces `C-0022`'s `(−0.302887367, 8.93928311 nm)` and its `−0.147080774` min-margin force fraction (the **+14.71 %**) — the free gate, and if it fails the sweep is on the wrong object |
| **P4** | the movement of each of the three terms is quoted **as a departure at matched refinement**, and its own mesh convergence is reported beside it, because a difference of two solves is far better resolved than either |
| **P5** | `C-0090`'s **`0.0621469105`** is re-evaluated under the re-solved collar on `C-0090`'s own winning placement, and the movement is stated against the **0.32 %** that claim declares and against `T-5b`'s **0.10** |

### Locked units, geometry and sign conventions

- Lengths **nm**, forces **pN**, line forces **pN/nm**, pressure **pN/nm²** (= 1 MPa exactly),
  `k_BT = 4.141947 pN·nm` at **T = 300 K**, aqueous **MgCl₂**, which is **2:1**, so `I = 3c`.
- **`z`** is normal to the electrode, positive **away** from it, origin on the electrode surface;
  **`x`** is lateral, `x = 0` the tile centre-line and a symmetry plane, the rim at `x = a`.
- **`a` is the tile half-width along the helices**: `20.00 nm` at §3's nominal 40.0 nm footprint,
  **`19.04 nm`** at `C-0086`'s buildable 112 bp row.
  The **across**-helix span is `15 × 2.69 = 40.35 nm` and is **unchanged** — `C-0090`'s Deliverable 1.
- The load is reported **downward**, positive when it pushes the tile toward the electrode.
- **`s`** is distance measured **inward from the rim**, so `s = 0` is the rim and `s = a` the centre-line.
- A **depth** is `1 − load/interior` and is **negative for an enhancement**;
  a **width** is the raised-cosine width matching the deficit's first two moments;
  the **rim residual** is the part of the global deficit inside the 1 nm standoff, as a line load.

### Verification type

**in-silico** (`electrostatics.PoissonBoltzmannEdge`, `C-0022`'s own solver, re-run at a changed
`tileHalfWidth`; `structure.PlateOnFoundation` and `anchoring.UpwardRootInfluenceBank` consumed
**read-only** for the flatness consequence)
**+ logical** (a closed-form exponential-tail model of the fit's own width-dependence, which is
the cheap bound and is falsifiable by the solve).

## Plan

### The cheap bound runs first, and it is a closed form

**The collar cannot move for a physical reason, and it can move for a bookkeeping one.**

*Physically* the collar is local: `C-0022`'s solved deficit centroid at the design point is
`2.658 nm` and the far tail decays over about `2.1 nm`, so the two rims of a 38.08 nm tile are
**≈ 18 decay lengths** apart and their interaction is `e^{−18} ≈ 1e−8`. Nothing in the field near
one rim knows where the other one is.

*But the fit does.* `fitEdgeTaper` references the profile to the **centre-line** load, integrates
the deficit over `[σ, a]` with `σ = 1 nm`, and hands both moments to
`width = 3.3628790424 × M₁/M₀`. All three of those carry `a`. Model the profile beyond the
near-rim structure as

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;`p(s) = Π∞ + A e^{−s/ℓ}`,&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;`τ(a) = p(a) − Π∞ = A e^{−a/ℓ}`

and the two truncated moments have exact closed forms,

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;`M₀(a) = E + τ(a)(a − σ + ℓ)`,
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;`M₁(a) = F + τ(a)[(a² − σ²)/2 + ℓ(a + ℓ)]`,

with `E`, `F` the untruncated moments about the true asymptote — width-independent by
construction, and calibrated from the reference solve. The same expression with `σ = 0` predicts
the global deficit, hence the rim residual. **So the whole width-dependence of all three
deliverables is one number, `τ(a)`, and it is exponentially small.**

Calibrated two ways, deliberately:

| anchoring | `ℓ` | `τ(20)` | why this is the reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| **best estimate** | fitted to `C-0022`'s own emitted far profile | fitted with it | what the solve is expected to do |
| **pessimistic** | `1/q₀ = 3.342 nm`, the transverse-eigenvalue **ceiling**, a rigorous upper bound within linear theory | `centrelineLoad − oneDimensionalLoad`, which is contaminated by the 1-D/2-D cross-solver difference and is therefore an over-estimate | the largest movement the family admits |

**Hand arithmetic, before any code**: the best estimate gives depth `+0.07 %`, width `−0.11 %`,
rim residual `−0.01 %`; the pessimistic anchoring gives depth `+0.28 %`, **width `−0.44 %`**.

### Why the 2-D solve is warranted anyway, and this is the cost justification

`C-0090` declares its flatness stable to **0.32 %** (its own guard-inset sweep, 0.0621469087 /
0.0623464033 / 0.0622667411). The cheap bound's bracket is **0.07 % to 0.44 %** and it
**straddles that threshold**. A bound that straddles its own acceptance threshold has not settled
the question, and `SESSION-PROMPT.md`'s rule is that the cheap bound runs *first*, not that it
runs *instead*. So the solve runs — and the cheap bound is not wasted: it becomes a **falsifiable
model** of what the solve should find, and the solve is its test.

The cost is small and it was measured before being spent: `C-0022` reports 6 Newton steps and
~2 370 CG iterations for one refinement-3 solve on ~90 000 nodes, so one solve is seconds, not
minutes. About **25 solves** are budgeted:

1. **the gate** — the design point at `a = 20.0`, refinement 3, against `C-0022`'s published triple;
2. **the answer** — the design point at `a = 19.04`, refinement 3;
3. **convergence** — both half-widths at refinements 1 / 2 / 3 / 4, nested, never 1/2/3/4
   (`CLAUDE.md`), with the **departure between the widths** tracked separately from either
   absolute value, because a difference of two solves on the same mesh is far better resolved than
   either (`C-0022`'s own depth is only converged to ~4 % at refinement 3, and that is exactly why
   the answer must be a departure);
4. **a half-width sweep** at refinement 2, `a = 12 … 30 nm`, which tests the exponential-tail model
   over a 6× range of `τ(a)` rather than at one point;
5. **three further states** — the second 10 nm bias, the 5 nm gap, and the 10 mM point where
   `C-0022`'s sign reverses — to say whether the answer is a property of the design point or of the
   geometry.

### What the numbers are consumed by, and how the flatness question is closed

`C-0090` builds its load as `coupling.edgeCollarPressure(interior, 38.08, 40.35, [smooth, rim])`
with the two collar terms read out of `gpd/results/T-3b-tile-edge-load-profile.json`. So the
flatness consequence needs no new lattice and no new search: **`C-0090`'s own winning placement is
recorded in its result file as a `bestKey`**, and re-evaluating exactly that placement under a new
load field is one `UpwardRootInfluenceBank` per field. The gate is that the carried collar
reproduces `0.0621469105`.

### What would falsify this approach

Declared **before** execution, per §5.

| # | falsifier |
|---|---|
| **F1** | at `a = 20.0 nm` and refinement 3 the pipeline does **not** reproduce `C-0022`'s `−0.302887367`, `8.93928311` and `−0.147080774`. Then the sweep is on the wrong object and nothing after it means anything. |
| **F2** | the solved departure between the two half-widths falls **outside** the cheap bound's `0.07 %–0.44 %` bracket. Then the exponential-tail model is wrong about *why* the collar depends on the width, and the mechanism has to be found before the number is quoted. |
| **F3** | any of the three collar terms moves by **more than `C-0090`'s 0.32 %** between `a = 20.0` and `a = 19.04`. |
| **F4** | the departure between the two half-widths does **not** converge in the mesh — i.e. refinements 1 / 2 / 4 disagree about it by more than the departure itself. Then the movement is discretisation, not physics, and must be reported as such. |
| **F5** | the re-evaluated flatness under the carried collar is **not** `C-0090`'s `0.0621469105`. Then the flatness consequence is not being read on `C-0090`'s object. |
| **F6** | the collar's width-dependence is **not monotone** in the half-width across the 12–30 nm sweep. The tail model predicts a strictly monotone `τ(a)`; alternation would mean the effect is mesh, not tail. |

### What this task does NOT do

- It does not revisit **mean field**. `C-0005` puts the one-loop correction at **123–214 %** of the
  leading term at these gaps, and for the oppositely charged tile-electrode pair no published
  result gives even the direction. A 2-D mean-field solve at a second width is still a mean-field
  solve, and the *departure* between two widths inherits that error as a common factor rather than
  escaping it.
- It does not solve the **corner**. `C-0022`'s two mappings bracket it at 1.8 percentage points of
  total force at 40 nm; the bracket is re-reported at 38.08 nm and not narrowed.
- It does not change the **rim charge** reading. `C-0022`'s falsifier 5 fired and left a 1.85×
  bracket on the depth; that bracket is a common factor on both half-widths and is not re-opened.
- It does not re-run `C-0090`'s **enumeration**. One placement — the one that claim recommends — is
  re-evaluated under a new load. Whether a *different* placement would win under the re-solved
  collar is named as open, not answered.
