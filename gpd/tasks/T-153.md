## Formulate

### The question

A seamless boustrophedon has only **progressive** scaffold crossovers,
so Rothemund's odd-half-turn rule binds the **row length**,
which must be an odd multiple of the 16 bp crossover spacing: 16, 48, 80, 112, 144 bp.
§3's 40.0 nm is 117.6 bp and is not on the list; the nearest admissible width is **112 bp = 38.08 nm**.

**Nothing in this programme has been evaluated at 38.08 nm.**

### Numeric target and acceptance predicate

> `C-0063`'s placement, `C-0015`'s phase census and every plan margin in the branch,
> re-read at a row length of 112 bp — or a demonstration that one of `CH-0101`'s four escapes restores 40.0 nm.

**Acceptance** (falsifiable, each a predicate in the result file):

| # | predicate |
|---|---|
| **P1** | the best 34-root upward placement at 38.08 nm dishes **less than `T-5b`'s 0.10** of the stroke under `C-0022`'s solved load |
| **P2** | the self-consistent upward arm count at 38.08 nm is still **34** |
| **P3** | every quantity this task declares **invariant by construction** departs by `< 1e-9` between the two widths |
| **P4** | `C-0015`'s phase census is re-taken at 38.08 nm and stated under a **named** end-of-row convention |
| **P5** | every plan margin in the branch (`C-0069`, `C-0072`, `C-0074`, `C-0066`, `C-0053`, `C-0085`) is re-read and its movement reported |

### Locked units, geometry and sign conventions

- Lengths **nm**, forces **pN**, stiffness **pN/nm**, pressure **pN/nm²** (= 1 MPa exactly),
  `k_BT = 4.141947 pN·nm` at **T = 300 K**, aqueous **2 mM MgCl₂**.
- `x` runs **along** the helices, `y` **across** them, `z` normal and positive **upward** (away from the grafted layer); `w` positive **downward**.
- **The width the raster rule binds is the along-helix one.**
  `Gen1Tile.EDGE_X` is *"the §3 tile footprint **along the helices**"*; the across-helix span is `15 × 2.69 = 40.35 nm` and is a **count of duplexes**, not a length the scaffold rasters.
  So `T-153` changes `EDGE_X` from 40.0 to `112 × 0.34 = 38.08` nm and changes **nothing** across the helices:
  the duplex count stays at **15**, the interhelical distance at the SAXS 2.69 nm, and `lengthY` at 40.35 nm.
  Getting this wrong would move the wrong axis, and `CLAUDE.md` records that a discreteness criterion must pair lengths in the same direction.
- A **root** is the crossover tying one upward arm to its host duplex; a **station** is its `(x, y)`.
- A **plane** is `C-0055`'s 8 bp crossover plane; a **column** is the sheet's own 16 bp column lattice, of which the planes are every other member.

### Verification type

**logical** (three closed-form lattice congruences that settle most of the branch before any solve)
**+ in-silico** (`C-0063`'s placement pipeline, `C-0009`'s grillage and `C-0058`'s exact Woodbury surrogate re-run as libraries at the changed width).

## Plan

### The cheap bounds, which run first and settle most of it

Ordered by cost, all of them integer arithmetic or one subtraction:

1. **Which axis.** 40.0 nm is along-helix. If so the across-helix geometry — 15 duplexes, 2.69 nm, `lengthY = 40.35` — is untouched and cannot move. **One line, and it removes half the branch.**
2. **The plan margins are lattice differences and carry no `edgeX`.**
   `C-0069`'s budget is `pitch − d = 10.88 − 2.69`; `C-0072`'s identity is `M = p − d − L`;
   `C-0085`'s razor is a ratio of joint stiffnesses. None contains the tile width, so all are **invariant by construction** — asserted at both widths, not argued.
3. **`C-0063`'s count vector is `3a + 2(15 − a) = 34`**, which contains the duplex count and nothing else — invariant.
4. **The column census is a truncation of a periodic lattice**, so it is `floor`/`ceil` arithmetic over 32 phases and costs one pass.
   `38.08 = 7 × 5.44` **exactly**, so the tile is an integer number of column pitches and the census is qualitatively different from the 40 nm one, where `40/5.44 = 7.35`.
5. **The arm-in-row bound.** A three-arm row needs `arm ≤ pitch − d = 8.19 nm` (the inboard pair) **and** `outermost site + arm ≤ edgeX/2` (the outboard one).
   The second contains the width and is the only place it enters the placement at all. One subtraction per phase.

### The expensive part, and why it is affordable

`C-0063`'s sweep is a **bank** problem, not a solve problem: the stations enter the grillage as *loads*, so one Cholesky per phase serves every candidate placement (`C-0058`'s `InfluenceSurrogate`, exact by superposition).
The whole 32-phase descent plus the exhaustive centro-symmetric enumeration is ~2 × 10⁶ surrogate solves at ~3 × 10³/s.

Method: re-run **`C-0063`'s own pipeline** — `upwardRootLattice`, `armDirections`, `rowRootOptions`, `centroSymmetricPlacements`, `descendPlacement`, `UpwardRootInfluenceBank` — as libraries at the changed width. Nothing is retyped.

**Justification against cost.** The alternative — rebuilding a placement search — would cost days and could not be checked against `C-0063`. Re-running its libraries costs one study and makes the gate below possible.

### The free gate

At `edgeX = 40.0` the pipeline must reproduce `C-0063`'s **0.0706145537** and **0.0873905056**
(the exhaustive centro-symmetric optima at phases 24 and 8) and `C-0069`'s **8.19 nm** budget, exactly.
Asserted as a test; if it does not, the sweep is on the wrong object.

### Named modelling decisions

- **The end-of-row crossover.** At 112 bp a column lands exactly on the row end at the phases `b ≡ 8 (mod 16)`.
  In a seamless boustrophedon that column **is** the scaffold crossover that turns the raster, so it is physically present —
  but `CrossoverLayout.phased` excludes it (`EDGE_MARGIN = 0.05 nm`, which exists so a column cannot seed a zero-length beam element).
  Both readings are carried: **`interior`** (the model's own convention, the column dropped) and
  **`end-of-row admitted`** (the column retained, inset by the model's own 0.05 nm = 0.147 bp).
- **The arm length.** `C-0039`'s elastica root is 8.16439083 nm = **24.0129 rises**, which no duplex can be.
  `C-0085` has already established that a plan length is quantised at the rise. Both readings are carried: the **elastica** root and the **buildable** 24 rises = 8.16 nm.
- **The load field.** `C-0022`'s collar terms were solved on a 40 nm tile; they are re-applied at 38.08 nm with the interior pressure rescaled to the new footprint.
  The collar is a sub-Debye rim feature and the total fringing enhancement scales as `1/L`, so this is an approximation of order 5 % in the *collar*, not in the interior — **named as a validity limitation, not swept**.

## Verify — the five gates

1. **dimensional** — a base-pair count is an integer; a width is `bp × rise`; the overhang is a length; unphysical arguments throw.
2. **limiting cases** — at `edgeX = 40.0` every function reproduces the published number; at a width that is an exact multiple of the column pitch the census collapses; a zero-length arm has no overhang.
3. **symmetry and conservation** — the centro-symmetry congruence re-derived at the new width; the station lattice reflected; the Woodbury surrogate against the assembled solve at departure 0; a uniform load on a free tile dishing exactly zero.
4. **numerical convergence** — nested subdivisions 1 ⊂ 2 ⊂ 4, the dishing sample grid 41/81/161, and the descent repeated for an identical argmin key.
5. **literature and upstream** — `C-0063`'s 0.0706145537 and 0.0873905056, `C-0069`'s 8.19, `C-0072`'s 0.0256, `C-0015`'s 56/49, `C-0055`'s 34 and 10.88, `C-0086`'s admissible-width list.
