## Formulate

### The question, as the queue states it

> An equilibrium centre-to-centre separation for two parallel **unbonded** duplexes at this project's buffer,
> against `C-0076`'s placement threshold of **2.715609 nm** —
> or the statement that the plan model's exclusion width cannot be given a value.

### Why it is the highest-value open item

`C-0076` established that every plan model in this repository writes `d = 2.69 nm` as a **steric exclusion width**,
and that 2.69 nm is the SAXS Bragg **lattice constant of a crossover-BONDED pair**.
The `d` in `C-0072`'s margin identity `M = p − d − L` is the **girth of a free body**,
and the gap in `C-0066`'s bound 4 and `C-0069`'s `Q5` is occupied by **one unbonded duplex**.
**The number has never been measured in the role it is used in.**

The verdict is a **step function at `pitch − arm = 2.715609 nm`**:
34 of 34 stations place below it, 22 above.
So a single number decides `C-0069`'s recommended output element outright.

### Locked conventions, restated rather than inherited

- Lengths **nm**, forces **pN**, energies **pN·nm** and `k_BT`; `k_BT = 4.141947 pN·nm` at **300 K**;
  `1 zJ = 1 pN·nm` exactly; pressure **pN/nm² = 1 MPa** exactly.
- Medium: aqueous **2 mM MgCl₂**, `ε_r = 78`, `l_B = 0.7141 nm`.
  **MgCl₂ is 2:1, so `I = ½Σc_i z_i² = 3c = 6 mM`, not 2 mM** — the Debye length is `√3` shorter than a monovalent
  intuition gives.
- **`D` is always an AXIS-TO-AXIS separation** unless the name says otherwise;
  `D_s` is a **surface-to-surface** separation, and the surface is the **phosphate locus** at `T-71`'s measured
  radius `R = 0.908638 nm`. **Never mix this with the osmotic-stress literature's 2.0 nm hard-diameter
  convention** — the two differ by 0.183 nm, which is 41 % of the quantity under test. Where a literature fit is
  used, it is used in **interaxial** coordinates, in which it is convention-free.
- The **steric floor** is phosphate-backbone contact, `2R = 1.817276 nm` (`T-71`, 13 084 crystallographic
  linkages, **measured by this repository**).
- The **placement threshold** is `pitch − arm = 10.88 − 8.16439 = 2.715609 nm` (`C-0066` bound 4, `C-0069` `Q5`).
- A **repulsive** interaction energy is **positive** and decreases with `D`; a **force** is `−dE/dD`, positive
  when repulsive.

### Acceptance predicate

**Either**

1. an equilibrium centre-to-centre separation `D_eq` for two parallel unbonded duplexes at 300 K in
   2 mM MgCl₂, with a bracket, and a verdict `D_eq ≷ 2.715609 nm`;

**or**

2. the statement that the plan model's exclusion width **cannot be given a value**, with the reason,
   *and* — because a bare refusal does not discharge a step function — whatever **is** decidable in its place:
   the floor, the threshold-versus-width relation, and the placement verdict that follows.

A `PASS` requires, in either branch, that **each of `C-0066`'s bound 4, `C-0069`'s `Q5`, `C-0072`'s four floors
and `C-0074`'s 30-root margin** is named and said to move or not move.

## Plan

### The cheap bound runs first, and there are three of them

**Cheap bound 1 — a packer is a FEASIBILITY predicate, and a feasibility predicate is not a force balance.**
`C-0053`'s footprint convention asks *"may these two bodies both be placed here?"*,
not *"where do these two bodies go?"*.
For rigid bodies the first question is answered by the **steric floor** and the second by an equilibrium.
`CLAUDE.md`'s *"a stable equilibrium is not a confinement"* is the same distinction one level out;
here it is sharper, because there may be **no equilibrium at all**.
**Cost: zero. What it settles: which of the two acceptance branches applies, before any code runs.**

**Cheap bound 2 — the range argument, and it decides the METHOD.**
At 2 mM MgCl₂ the Debye length is **3.93 nm**, which is **longer than the entire disputed bracket**
(1.817 → 3.60 nm, 1.78 nm wide).
A screened Coulomb interaction therefore varies by only `e^(−κΔD)` over the whole bracket
and **cannot place an edge at 2.7156 nm**.
**A hard-body exclusion width is a sharp feature and electrostatics at this buffer has no sharp feature there.**
So a bespoke two-cylinder nonlinear Poisson-Boltzmann solve — the expensive option, days of work on
`PoissonBoltzmannEdge`'s machinery — **cannot change the verdict**, and building one would be spending the
budget on the one channel that provably has no resolving power.
**This is the cost justification for closed-form Debye-Hückel plus Derjaguin instead of a 2-D solve.**
It is falsifiable: see **F2**.

**Cheap bound 3 — the three roles are three GEOMETRIES, and only one of them is "parallel".**
`C-0076`'s Deliverable 1 separates `ROW_PITCH`, `BODY_WIDTH` and `COLLINEAR_CLEARANCE`.
On the actual bodies those are three *different* pair geometries:

| role | geometry | what faces what |
|---|---|---|
| `ROW_PITCH` | **parallel cylinders** | two host duplexes side by side — **bonded**, measured, not in question |
| `C-0066` bound 4 | **crossed cylinders** (90°) | a vertical tie's flank against a horizontal arm's end |
| `C-0069` `Q5` | **coaxial rods, end to end** | one arm's blunt end facing the next arm's root |

The three have different closed forms and they do not differ by a constant.
**The question "what separation do two parallel duplexes hold" is not the question either knife edge asks.**

### The method, and what it costs

1. **Closed-form Debye-Hückel pair energies** in all three geometries, with the finite-radius cylinder
   correction `1/(κR K₁(κR))²` and Manning-renormalised line charge from `DnaOrigamiTile`
   (`τ_eff = τ/(qξ_M)` = 0.700 e/nm at `q = 2`).
   The crossed and coaxial forms integrate in closed form:
   `E_cross = 2πτ²l_B k_BT e^(−κD)/κ` and `E_coax = τ²l_B k_BT[e^(−κg)/κ − g E₁(κg)]`.
   The parallel form needs `K₀`. **Cost: one file, one afternoon.**
2. **Van der Waals** at the only published Hamaker constant for the right geometry —
   Dryden's cylinder-cylinder **4.33–5.90 zJ** (`C-0021`, read directly there).
   `CLAUDE.md`: *no planar `A_DNA` exists*, and the `10⁻²⁰ J` in circulation is Rau & Parsegian's own
   explicit **overestimate**, introduced to prove van der Waals is too weak. **Cost: two formulas.**
3. **Hydration** by Derjaguin from a measured flat-flat exponential, with the fit taken from the
   osmotic-stress literature **in interaxial coordinates**. **Cost: a literature fetch.**
4. **The mean-field bracket, stated and not hidden.** `C-0005` puts the one-loop correction at 123–214 %
   and `Ξ` at 17–24 for Mg²⁺ at a DNA surface, with `Ξ ∝ q³` and **no systematic theory**.
   Strong coupling is exactly the regime in which PB has the **wrong sign** for like-charged rods.
   The bound that actually constrains it is **empirical**: Mg²⁺ does not condense DNA at room temperature.
   **A mean-field number quoted without that bracket is not an answer.**
5. **Literature before simulation**, per this project's standing rule: osmotic-stress force curves for
   exactly this geometry beat any solve this box can run.

### What would falsify this approach — DECLARED BEFORE EXECUTION

| # | falsifier | what it would mean |
|---|---|---|
| **F1** | **The pair interaction has a stationary point above steric contact** — a real minimum at finite separation. | Acceptance branch 1 is non-empty, the answer is a number, and cheap bound 1's *"a packer is not a force balance"* is beside the point. |
| **F2** | **The electrostatic range is SHORTER than the disputed bracket** at this buffer, so a solve *can* place an edge inside it. | Cheap bound 2 fails, the method choice is wrong, and the 2-D pair solve has to be built. |
| **F3** | **The energy of placing a body in `C-0066`'s gap at the measured girth EXCEEDS what the host sheet demonstrably pays** to hold its own duplexes at the measured 2.69 nm lattice constant. | The feasibility argument fails: the design cannot afford the floor, and the width is genuinely above it. |
| **F4** | **Van der Waals is ≥ 10 % of the repulsion anywhere in the bracket.** | A secondary minimum may exist, DLVO must be solved rather than bounded, and F1 becomes live. |
| **F5** | **The coaxial geometry costs MORE than the crossed geometry at the same separation.** | The three roles cannot be separated, one width serves all of them, and cheap bound 3 is empty. |

### Verification gates

1. **Dimensional** — every energy is `pN·nm`, every pressure `pN/nm²`; the whole pair model is invariant under
   a common rescaling of `(length, 1/κ)`; unphysical arguments throw.
2. **Limiting cases** — `E_coax(g → 0)` is finite and equals `τ²l_B k_BT/κ`; `E_cross(κ → 0)` diverges as `1/κ`;
   the finite-radius factor → 1 as `κR → 0`; the Bessel functions reproduce their small- and large-argument
   asymptotics; Manning at `q = 1` is exactly twice `q = 2`.
3. **Symmetry and conservation** — **the DH crossed-rod closed form must equal a direct 2-D numerical
   quadrature of the screened Coulomb kernel** (two independent routes to one number, and nothing forces them
   to agree); the Derjaguin crossed and parallel hydration forms must agree with direct numerical integration
   of the same gap profile; **charge conservation** — the Manning-surviving line charge times the length must
   equal the bare charge times the surviving fraction, and the far-field of the finite-radius cylinder must
   equal the far field of its equivalent line.
4. **Numerical convergence** — the quadratures refined 2× and 4×; the bracketed root exiting on its own step.
5. **Literature cross-check** — the Bessel functions against tabulated values; the DH parallel-rod energy per
   length against the published DNA–DNA osmotic pressure at the same interaxial spacing; the premises of
   Derjaguin (`D_s ≪ R`) and of Debye-Hückel (`ψ_0 ≪ k_BT/e`) **checked against this material** and reported
   as violated where they are.
