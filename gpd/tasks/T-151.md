## Formulate

### The question

`C-0081` finds that a scaffold seam takes **6–12** of `C-0063`'s 34 stations off `C-0076`'s weave node,
restores the **1.2–1.75 nm** amplitude bracket `C-0076` annihilated, and at the cryo-EM amplitude drives the worst
across-row clearance to **−0.0023 nm**, a clash. A **seamless** routing would buy all of that back for nothing.
Nothing in this programme has checked that a 15-duplex 40 nm single-layer sheet can be folded from one scaffold
without a seam — indeed **the tile has no scaffold routing at all**.

### Locked units and conventions

- Lengths **nm**, energies `k_BT` (`k_BT = 4.141947 pN·nm` at **300 K**), aqueous **2 mM MgCl₂**.
- `x` runs **along** the helices, `y` **across** them. The sheet is **15** duplexes at the SAXS **2.69 nm**, 40.0 nm wide, rise **0.34 nm**, square lattice at **32/3 bp per turn**.
- **A row** is one duplex. **A scaffold segment** is a maximal run of scaffold inside one row.
- **A seam** is Rothemund's: *"a contour which the path does not cross"* — equivalently, the locus where a row carries two scaffold segments meeting end to end.
- **Seamless** means every row carries **exactly one** scaffold segment.

### Acceptance predicate

A scaffold routing for a 15-duplex 40 nm single-layer sheet with **no seam**, or the statement that one is
unavailable at this aspect ratio.

## Plan

### The cheap bound, which runs before any code

**A seam is a graph-theoretic parity, not a fabrication convention.** Crossovers join only *adjacent* duplexes, so
the row-adjacency graph of a single-layer sheet is the **path** `P_D` — a tree. A **closed** walk on a tree
traverses every edge an **even** number of times, so a fully folded **circular** scaffold gives *every* row at least
**two** segments, i.e. a seam crossing every row. A **linear** scaffold needs only a Hamiltonian *path*, which `P_D`
has, uniquely, for every `D`.

So the answer is decided by a **specification** — the scaffold's topology and whether it is fully folded — and the
theorem also predicts which of Rothemund's own shapes have seams and which do not. That prediction is the
falsifier, and it costs nothing to check against his paper.

### Method, justified against cost

Everything is integer arithmetic on a 15-node path graph plus a 64-state dynamic program. The Hamiltonian
path/cycle counts are **brute-forced** rather than asserted, so the theorem is computed. The only literature spend
is Rothemund's own main text and Supplementary Notes, already fetched.

**What would falsify this approach**: a published seamless raster on a single-layer sheet folded from a *fully
used circular* scaffold. That would mean the row graph is not what the theorem assumes.

### Declared falsifiers

| # | falsifier | what it would mean |
|---|---|---|
| **F1** | a Hamiltonian **cycle** exists on the 15-row path graph | a circular scaffold folds a sheet seamlessly and the whole theorem is wrong |
| **F2** | no seamless routing exists at this aspect ratio under **any** scaffold specification | the acceptance's second branch, and `C-0081`'s cost is unavoidable |
| **F3** | Rothemund's own record contradicts the theorem — a seamed design with a linear scaffold left over, or a seamless one with a fully folded circular scaffold | the theorem does not describe the field's practice |
| **F4** | the seamless route violates Rothemund's own **odd half-turn** constraint at the Gen-1 tile's width, and no admissible width lies near 40 nm | the routing exists on paper and not on the lattice |
| **F5** | no **staggered** seam assignment clears every station, so a mandated circular fully folded scaffold has no remedy | the fallback fails and `C-0081`'s 6–12 stations stand |

### Five verification gates

1. **Dimensional** — a nucleotide count is an integer; a loop contour and a radius of gyration are lengths and scale with the contour per nucleotide.
2. **Limiting cases** — `D = 2` has a Hamiltonian cycle and needs no seam even circular; `D = 1` is trivial; the double raster returns exactly one seam at every `D ≥ 3`.
3. **Symmetry and conservation** — a route covers every row exactly once (seamless) or twice (seamed); the segment count equals the closed-walk edge parity; the staggered assignment is invariant under reflecting the sheet.
4. **Numerical convergence** — none needed and that is asserted: every quantity is an integer or a closed form. The Hamiltonian counts are exhaustive.
5. **Literature** — Rothemund's 26-helix square (*"no vertical reversals in raster direction, required a linear scaffold"*) and his 8-helix third-square (*"a circular M13mp18 scaffold DNA was used … the unfolded portion … could easily bridge the corners"*) both reproduce the theorem's prediction, read directly.
