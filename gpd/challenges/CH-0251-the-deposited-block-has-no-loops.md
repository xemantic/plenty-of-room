## The claim under challenge

`C-0193` holds, from the caDNAno paper's own accounting sentence and from Supplementary Figure S4
of the Nature paper, that the seven built honeycomb blocks put **28 unpaired scaffold nucleotides**
at every raster turn — *"the two **DUPLEX** ends it joins are **28** unpaired nucleotides apart,
14 on each side"* — and that this is what **buys** freedom from caDNAno's `±5 bp` residue
condition, because an unpaired base has no azimuth. Its headline states that the zero-loop turn is
*"drawable and lattice-legal, and **nobody has folded it on the honeycomb**"*, on the evidence that
*"`7 of 7` built cross-sections do the other thing"*.

The sentence it rests on is real and is quoted correctly:

> *"Each helix was allotted **126** bases of scaffold. Of those 126 bases, **98** were paired with
> complementary staples, and the remaining **28** bases were divided into front and rear unpaired
> loop fragments at the ends of each helix."*

## The artifact it did not read

The **seven design files for those seven cross-sections** are deposited by the same paper, in the
gallery that same paper names as its record, and `T-255` retrieved and parsed them.

| | the sentence | the deposited files |
|---|---|---|
| scaffold allotted per helix | **126** | **126** — agrees exactly, at all seven |
| paired with staples | **98** | **126** |
| unpaired, in front and rear loops | **28** | **`0`** |
| unpaired scaffold bases in the whole block | 1 680 | **`0`** |
| raster turns bound by the `±5 bp` residue condition | none, by `C-0193` §4 | **all of them** |
| scaffold crossings off the `±5` lattice | not applicable | **`0` of 118** (`i`, `iii`, `v`: `0` of 126) |

> **ANNOTATED (`C-0200`, `T-302`, iteration 47).** The right-hand column of the table above is correct about the deposited **drawing** and is not a statement about the folded object. Under the staple order the same file's `10 × 6` block pairs **`98`** bases on every one of its 60 helices, over the identical window `28..125`, and leaves **`28`** unpaired — split `12 / 16`, not `14 / 14` — so `60` of its `118` scaffold crossings sit in single-stranded scaffold and the `±5 bp` residue condition cannot bind them. See [`CH-0253`](CH-0253-a-register-census-of-a-drawing.md).

`60 × 126 = 7 560` and `64 × 126 = 8 064` **both hold in the files**, which is what identifies them
as the paper's designs; the paper's own scaffold-pairing list (*"i: p8064, ii: p7560, iii: p8064,
iv: p7560, v: p8064, vi: p7560, vii: p7560"*) is reproduced by the files at **7 of 7**. The
allotment is confirmed. The **split** is not.

And the `10 × 6` block — the one `C-0193` §3 reads Supplementary Figure S4 for, and the one this
repository models — is deposited **twice**, by the caDNAno paper *and* by the Nature paper, and the
two files are **bit-identical in every `vstrand`**
(`ac46b69a04813ee0cc15eddd5fc33265f56a8072bfda3063924b3c5034317053`). Its scaffold occupies bases
`16..141` of every helix and so do its staples; helix 0 turns to helix 1 by a direct covalent step
from base 141 to base 141, with **zero** intervening unpaired nucleotides — which is `C-0193`'s own
route A, drawn.

The end regions are covered by **20 staple strands of exactly 12 nt, each lying entirely within one
helix**. They are short, and whether they were ordered is not something a design file can say. That
is precisely the point: **two primary artifacts of the same authors disagree, and only one of them
was consulted.**

## What is challenged, and what is not

**NOT challenged.** The quotation, its arithmetic, the FJC tether pricing, the reach bound, the
scaffold budget of §8, and the whole conditionality table of §7 as a statement about *route B*.
`C-0193` is careful, its numbers reproduce, and its `F1`–`F3` reasoning is sound **given** the
sentence.

**Challenged**, on three points:

1. **§3's *"what the built block ACTUALLY does"*.** A Methods sentence and a design file are both
   primary and they disagree; the section's title claims to settle which. It does not, and the
   file is the artifact closer to what caDNAno emitted.
2. **§4's mechanism.** *"The built design buys freedom from the residue condition and pays 28 nt of
   scaffold for it"* cannot be the mechanism for these seven blocks, because their deposited files
   **close the residue condition exactly** — `0` forced scaffold crossings of 118 (or 126) — while
   paying **nothing**. Whatever the 28 nt is for, on this evidence it is not that. `C-0193` §5
   already quotes the papers saying it is for **aggregation**, and that half survives untouched.
3. **The headline's *"nobody has folded it"* and its `7 of 7`.** Both are inferences from the
   sentence. `C-0193`'s own existence sweep — *"0 of 186 unique records over 30 queries in 9
   families"* — searched the **literature**; the answer, whichever way it falls, is in the **data**,
   in an archive the caDNAno paper points at in the very sentence `C-0152` §6 was raised on.

## What would settle it

The SI staple **sequence tables** of the two 2009 papers: a design with 28 unpaired scaffold bases
per helix orders `60 × 98 = 5 880` staple nucleotides for the `10 × 6` block, and the deposited file
draws **7 560**. `T-255` extracted the Nature SI and found its text layer garbled by font
subsetting past the front matter; the tables carry a `Length` column and the count is one careful
pass away. **That is a cheap, decisive experiment and it is named rather than run here.**

> **RUN (`C-0200`, `T-302`, iteration 47), and it was cheaper than budgeted.** The garble is a property of the SI's **figure** pages only — pages 12–23 carry the twelve staple tables and their text layer extracts verbatim, which one `pdftotext -bbox -f 12 -l 26` settles. The sum of the `Length` column is **`5 880`**. Every row resolves to a strand of the deposited file at the same length **and** the file's own stored staple colour, at `1 492` of `1 492` rows over eight tables.
