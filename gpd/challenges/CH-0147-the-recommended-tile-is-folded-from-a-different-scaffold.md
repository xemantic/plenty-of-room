## The charge

`C-0119` §5 reads the caDNAno paper's own scaffold pairing correctly:

> *"The design: scaffold pairings are as follows: i: p8064, ii: p7560, iii: p8064, iv: p7560, v: p8064, vi: p7560, vii: p7560."*

and correctly attaches **p8064** to design **(i), the 15 × 4 cross-section**, which was the tile `C-0119` was written about.
Its table is labelled *"p8064 — designs i, iii, v, **including ours**"*.

[`C-0120`](../claims/C-0120-cross-section-comparison.md) then moved the recommendation to **10 × 6** — design **(ii)** —
on the grounds that it is 6.6× flatter at the same 60 helices *and* has no composite-fraction threshold at all.

**Design (ii) is folded from p7560.**

## Why it is worth a challenge rather than a footnote

The two cross-sections consume **identical** scaffold — 60 helices at 112 bp either way — so
`C-0119`'s own sentence *"the scaffold budget genuinely is unchanged between them"* is right,
and it is exactly what makes the difference invisible: the number that changes is the **scaffold**, not the tile.
A reader checking the budget finds it consistent and the remainder wrong by **1.6×**.

`C-0125` measures what that 1.6× is worth in the quantity `C-0022` is written on:
the whole remainder smeared on the tile's gap-facing plane moves `σ_eff` by
**0.0350791486** at p8064 and **0.0262693428** at p7560 — so the verdict (*bounded away*) does not move,
which is the only reason this is a correction rather than a reversal.

## The repair

One row and one sentence of `C-0119` §5, marked as superseded rather than overwritten.
The **table itself is right** and should stay: it is the only place in the corpus that records
which scaffold each of the seven published cross-sections was folded from,
and `C-0125` reads it as an input.
